# Digital Chameleons (Bailenson & Yee, Psychological Science 2005)

Bailenson, J. N., Yee, N. "Digital Chameleons: Automatic Assimilation of Nonverbal Gestures in Immersive Virtual Environments." Psychological Science 16(10):814-819, 2005. Author PDF from vhil.stanford.edu.

## What was read
- Full text (6 pages).

## Claims
- "participants interacted with an embodied artificial intelligence agent in immersive virtual reality. The agent either mimicked a participant's head movements at a 4-s delay or utilized prerecorded movements of another participant as it verbally presented an argument. Mimicking agents were more persuasive and received more positive trait ratings than nonmimickers, despite participants' inability to explicitly detect the mimicry."
- Method: 69 students; pitch, yaw and roll copied exactly at a 4 s delay. The delay was chosen because earlier work found 4 s "optimal in minimizing detection of mimicry and maximizing responsiveness". The agent's only other behaviours were random blinks and amplitude-driven lips during a 195 s recorded message. No turn-taking.

## Evidence
- Detection: two blind raters coded open-ended paragraphs. "If either rater gave a participant a 1, that participant was removed": 8 removed, 7 of them from the mimic condition (which had 35 participants). Final N = 61.
- Effectiveness composite (25 items, α = .87): mimic M = 0.58 (SD 0.59) vs recorded 0.07 (SD 0.62), F(1, 53) = 13.74, p < .001, η² = .21. Social presence and agent impression p < .001; agreement p = .07.
- Gaze proxy: maximum yaw away from the agent, recorded 38.46° vs mimic 24.21°, F(1, 52) = 5.60, p < .02.

## Failures documented (as the authors state them)
- **Mimicry or mere contingency is unresolved.** "it is not clear whether the reason for the greater effectiveness of the mimicking agent ... was that the agent was mimicking the participants' exact movements or merely that the agent was acting in a manner that was contingent on the participant's behavior."
- **Exact copying is detectable.** 7 of 35 participants in the mimic condition (20%) wrote enough for a rater to code them as detecting it, even at a 4 s delay and with head motion only. The later paper (bailenson-2008-detecting-digital-chameleons.md) cites this study as "5% of all instances". The two numbers come from different counts and should not be merged.
- **Narrow setting.** "the interaction was limited in that there was no verbal turn taking." It is a one-way persuasive monologue with head motion only.
- The authors propose "probabilistic mimics and scaled mimics" as harder to detect than "the delayed mirroring used in the current study".
