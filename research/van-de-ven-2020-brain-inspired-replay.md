## Claims
- On split MNIST, the established methods all succeed in the task-incremental scenario. In the class-incremental scenario, "the metaplasticity-inspired methods EWC and SI dramatically failed and only GR was able to successfully learn all digits". Hence "for Class-IL ... some form of replay might be required."
- Replay can be scarce and poor and still work: "even with a single replayed sample per mini-batch (i.e., one replayed sample for every 128 samples from the current task), GR performed competitively in the Task-IL scenario and outperformed all non-replay methods in the Class-IL scenario". Shrinking the VAE to "only 10 units resulted in low-quality samples ... but only moderately affected the performance".
- The proposed reason: "having to learn something new is substantially harder than not forgetting it." A control supports it. If the network is re-initialized before each task, so that replay must rebuild old tasks from scratch, "replaying only a few or low-quality examples was indeed not enough".
- Plain generative replay (GR) does not scale. On permuted MNIST "its performance rapidly declined after about 15 tasks". On split CIFAR-100 it "failed ... even in the Task-IL scenario".
- Four brain-inspired changes fix this: replay-through-feedback, conditional replay, gating based on internal context, and internal replay. A fifth change, distillation, comes from machine learning. With all five, "brain-inspired replay ... achieves state-of-the-art performance ... without storing data."
- Function space versus parameter space. "GR maintains this memory in the function or output space of the network", whereas EWC and SI keep it "entirely in the parameter space". The two "are complementary mechanisms", which the authors relate to systems consolidation and cellular consolidation.
- A stated limit: "in semi- or unsupervised settings, the conditional replay and the gating based on internal context components would need to be modified as their current implementation depends on the availability of class labels ... It might be possible to instead condition on other context information."
- A second stated limit: biological replay has "temporal structure", and this method lacks it. It replays static samples.

## Evidence
- Protocols: split MNIST, 5 tasks of 2 digits, about 6000 training images per digit. Permuted MNIST, 100 permutations of zero-padded 32×32 images, each task a 10-way classification over 60,000 images (domain-incremental). Split CIFAR-100, 10 tasks of 10 classes, 500 training images per class. Each task trains for 2000 iterations (MNIST) or 5000 (CIFAR) with Adam, learning rate 0.001 or 0.0001, batch 128 or 256. Repetitions: 20 (split MNIST), 5 (permuted MNIST), 10 (CIFAR-100). Error bars are ±1 SEM.
- Networks: two hidden layers of 400 units (split MNIST) or 2000 units (permuted MNIST). For CIFAR, 5 convolutional layers pretrained on CIFAR-10 and frozen, then 2×2000 fully connected units. The VAE has 100 latent units.
- Prior results on permuted MNIST after 100 tasks (Masse et al.): SI "~82%", online EWC "~70%", XdG "~61%", SI+XdG "~95%". The XdG results use the task label at test time, which makes them task-incremental results.
- Brain-inspired replay combined with SI, with and without replay-through-feedback: permuted MNIST "0.904 ± 0.005 versus 0.892 ± 0.004"; class-incremental CIFAR-100 "0.344 ± 0.002 versus 0.334 ± 0.002".
- Class-incremental split CIFAR-100 after 100 classes (read off Fig. 6c): joint training about 0.53, BI-R+SI about 0.34, BI-R about 0.21. LwF, SI, EWC, plain GR and no method all sit near 0.08 to 0.10, about the level of training on the last task alone.
- Ablations (read off Fig. 8). Permuted MNIST: GR about 0.40, BI-R about 0.81. Removing conditional replay gives about 0.40. Removing context gating gives about 0.41. Removing distillation gives about 0.68. Removing replay-through-feedback gives about 0.82. Class-incremental CIFAR-100: GR about 0.08, BI-R about 0.21. Removing internal replay gives about 0.11. Removing gating, distillation or conditioning gives about 0.16 to 0.19. Printed text: "none of the individual modifications were sufficient ... while all of them—with the exception of RtF—were necessary". Also: "the benefit of conditional replay and that of gating based on internal context depended on their combination with other components". For permuted MNIST and class-incremental CIFAR-100, the full combination gained more "than the sum of the effects of adding each of them in isolation".
- Quality of the generated samples (Fig. 9): the modified Inception Score, the modified FID, and precision-recall curves computed with an embedding network that shares the frozen convolutional layers. Brain-inspired replay improved "both the quality and the diversity of the generated samples to similar extents".

## Mechanism
- Stream weighting, Eq. 3: L_total = (1/N_tasks) · L_current + (1 − 1/N_tasks) · L_replay. The generator uses the same weighting. Each iteration replays a fixed batch split over all previous tasks, so replay cost does not grow with the number of tasks.
- Replayed samples are generated by a frozen copy of the model "directly after finishing training on task T − 1". The generator is a teacher, frozen at the end of the previous episode.
- Replay-through-feedback: one symmetric VAE with a softmax classifier on the encoder's last hidden layer. The paper casts the lower layers as "cortex" and the top layers as "hippocampus". Loss on current data: L_C + L_G. Loss on replayed data: L_D + L_G. Here L_G = L_recon + L_latent, the negative ELBO; L_latent for the standard prior is −½ Σ_j (1 + log σ_j² − μ_j² − σ_j²) over 100 latent units.
- Distillation, Eqs. 7-8. The target is ỹ_c = p^T_{θ̂(K−1)}(Y = c | x), with temperature T = 2. The loss is L_D = −T² Σ_c ỹ_c log p^T_θ(Y = c | x). The T² factor keeps the gradient scale comparable to hard targets. Replay therefore asks the network to reproduce its own past output distribution, not a label.
- Conditional replay, Eq. 9. The prior is a Gaussian mixture p_χ(z) = Σ_c p(Y = c) · N(μ_c, σ_c² I), with trainable μ_c and σ_c and a uniform class prior. Hard-labelled data has a closed-form KL term (Eq. 10). Soft-labelled replay needs a one-sample Monte Carlo estimate (Eq. 11). To generate, pick a class c, sample z ~ N(μ_c, σ_c² I), decode, then relabel the result with the feedforward pass.
- Gating based on internal context: each task or class gets a fixed random mask that zeroes X% of units in each decoder hidden layer, with X set by grid search. Only the generative (backward) path is gated, and inference uses the ungated feedforward path. That is why gating still works when the task is unknown at test time. The mask used during generation is the one for the class chosen for conditioning.
- Internal replay: the decoder stops at the hidden level, and the reconstruction loss becomes squared error on hidden activations, L_irecon = E Σ_i (h_i − ĥ_i)² (Eq. 12). This requires a front end that stays frozen or barely changes, since replay never retrains it.
- Combination with SI, Eqs. 13-15. Importance ω_i = Σ_t (θ_i[t] − θ_i[t−1]) · (−∂L/∂θ_i), normalized: Ω_i = Σ_k ω_i^(k) / ((Δ_i^(k))² + ξ), with ξ = 0.1. Penalty: Σ_i Ω_i (θ_i − θ̂_i)². The Fisher penalties of EWC and online EWC are Eqs. 16-18, with γ = 1.

## Runs on this Mac without a GPU?
Yes, at the MNIST scale. The README gives "~12 minutes" on "a standard laptop" for brain-inspired replay on class-incremental split MNIST, and "~50 minutes" for the full comparison. The CIFAR-100 runs (5000 iterations × 10 tasks × 10 repetitions, with 2000-unit layers) are heavier but feasible on Apple-silicon MPS. Our engine would replay low-dimensional feature streams, which costs less than CIFAR.

## Implications for the engine (our inferences unless quoted)
- **What gets replayed.** Internal replay matches Principle 7 directly: replay latent states and feature-stream summaries, not pixels or audio. The paper names the precondition: the layers below the replay level must not drift. For us, the perception front end (blendshapes, prosody encoders) must be frozen or very slow, otherwise latents replayed from old days decode against a moved feature space. This is a real design constraint on S4, where the body route's inverse model is meant to keep learning. Replay targets above a changing layer go stale.
- **Scarce data.** "Learning something new is substantially harder than not forgetting it." Even 1 replayed sample per 128 new ones protects old knowledge, but replay alone cannot rebuild a task (the re-initialization control). So replay will not make up for a lack of operator hours in learning something new. It can keep the slow store from being overwritten by today's few hours. For temperament and the relationship to form from a few hours a day, the new learning must come from the real hours. Replay's job is to protect the integral over many days.
- **Weighting over time.** With days as tasks, Eq. 3 gives today's data weight 1/N_days, which is 1% after 100 days. That pushes toward a fixed temperament and also toward no adaptation at all. The paper's N counts tasks, not time. A sensible analogue is the number of distinct contexts or tag modes, not calendar days. That choice is open, and it changes the S6 stability claim.
- **Distillation is the right target for an affect engine.** Replay asks the current network to match its own past output distribution (soft, T = 2), with no external label. That fits Principles 1-2: no emotion labels exist, but the engine's own past responses and predictions do. Temperament could be defined operationally as what function-space distillation preserves across days. The ablation is cheap: hard targets lose about 0.13 on permuted MNIST (read off Fig. 8a).
- **State tag versus context label.** Here the generative path is conditioned and gated by class or task identity. The authors say this must change when no labels exist, and suggest "other context information". Two candidates for us. (a) Condition the generative prior on a mixture mode per internal-state region, so the agent's own latent affect state is the tag. (b) Condition on partner identity as an external context. (b) is a context label, not an emotion label, so it is allowed by Principle 1. (a) is closer to what S6 means by "state tagging", but its modes are learned and can drift. A mode whose meaning moves turns conditional replay into replay of the wrong thing.
- **Why tagging should be ablatable, and what to expect.** On permuted MNIST, removing conditional replay or context gating drops BI-R from about 0.81 to about 0.40, which is plain GR's level (read off Fig. 8a). Both components help only in combination. That is the expected shape of S6's claim that removing state tagging removes specificity: without conditioned generation, replay drifts toward a blurred average over contexts. The paper's add-one and remove-one design is the template for that ablation.
- **Partner-specific mapping without forgetting others.** The gating trick matters most here. Context masks apply only to the generative path, and inference stays shared and ungated. Translated to our case: a per-partner (or per-state-mode) mask or prior mode lets the engine regenerate what that partner was like. Replay then trains the one shared response pathway on all partners' regenerated contexts, interleaved. The distinct, partner-specific response must still be learnable by the shared pathway from its input. So partner identity, or features that reveal it, must be part of the input at inference, or the mapping cannot differ by partner. This is our inference. The paper's class-incremental setup has no analogue of a partner channel.
- **Fast and slow stores.** The paper drops the separate hippocampus network. One network holds both: the top layers serve as the "hippocampus" generator and the lower ones as "cortex", through feedback weights. Replay-through-feedback was not necessary for accuracy but was the most efficient design. For a single-objective engine (Principle 10), one model that is both world model and generator is the better fit than bolting on a separate VAE.
- **Function plus parameter protection.** BI-R+SI was best everywhere (0.344 vs 0.21 on class-incremental CIFAR-100, read off Fig. 6c). An engine whose replay comes from its own objective may still need a parameter-importance term. Synaptic intelligence's path integral is computable online at no extra data cost.
- **Missing pieces relevant to us.** There is no temporal structure: samples are static. Our replay must be of sequences, because timing and contingency are the content, and this paper offers nothing on generating sequences. There is also no prioritization: classes are drawn uniformly. Emotional salience weighting of replay must come from elsewhere (see McGaugh, Payne & Kensinger, and the Schaul note).

## Verdict: adopt (internal replay, distillation targets, generation conditioned and gated on context with an ungated inference path); watch (conditioning on learned state modes without labels)
- Under Principle 10, replay must follow from the core. This paper is compatible: the generator is the model's own feedback path, and the replay loss is the model's own generative loss plus self-distillation. If the core is a latent world model trained on a variational objective, replay-through-feedback is that objective applied to internally generated latents. That makes it derived, not added.
- It meets the brief's privacy and no-label constraints. Replay happens at the feature or latent level with no stored raw data, and the targets are self-distilled rather than labelled.
- Watch: everything that makes this work at scale (conditioning and gating) uses labels. The S6 version needs label-free tags, and the paper gives no evidence that conditioning on learned, drifting modes keeps the benefit.
