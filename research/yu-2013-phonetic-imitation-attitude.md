# Yu, Abrego-Collier & Sonderegger 2013: attitude to the talker sets the direction of phonetic imitation

Yu, A. C. L., Abrego-Collier, C. & Sonderegger, M. (2013). "Phonetic imitation from an individual-difference perspective: subjective attitude, personality and 'autistic' traits." *PLoS ONE* 8(9):e74746. doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0074746. Open: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3786990/ (CC BY).

**How fully read:** the full text from PMC, from the abstract through the conclusion, including Model 1 and Model 2 and the discussion. Several coefficients and the variance-explained statistic were rendered as images or MathML and were lost in extraction, so only the effects quoted in the prose are reported. The stimulus table and the supporting information were not read.

Read in place of Babel 2012 and Babel & Bulatov 2012, which had no reachable open copy.

## What it says
- **Participants and design.** 84 participants analysed (93 recruited). Each read 72 /p t k/-initial words, then heard a male narrator tell a blind-date story containing target words with lengthened voice onset time (VOT), then read the words again.
- **Between-subject manipulations:** whether the story ended well or badly for the date (the narrator abandons her), and the narrator's implied sexual orientation.
- **Measured:** each participant's attitude to the narrator, autism-spectrum quotient subscales, Big Five, working memory.
- **Results.**
  - *Average shift:* 0.47 ms, not significant. There is no imitation on average.
  - *Individual spread:* subjects differ strongly. 95 % of subject offsets lie between −6.8 and +7.8 ms beyond the modelled predictors.
  - *Attitude sets the direction.* Subjects who liked the narrator lengthened VOT toward his, and subjects who disliked him shortened it, away from him. A 2-SD change in attitude moves the predicted shift by 4.53 ms.
  - *Other predictors:* openness (+3.53 ms per 2 SD) and the attention-switching subscale (+2.58 ms) also predict the shift. Hearing the bad ending gave 2.58 ms more shift than the good ending, which the authors tentatively attribute to attention.
  - *Not predictive:* subject gender and the narrator's orientation.
  - The subject-level predictors are significant but explain very little of the total variability in shift (authors' statement; the statistic was not recovered).
- **Caveat stated by the authors:** there was no control condition with unmodified VOT.

## What matters for this engine
- **Convergence can flip sign with how one regards the partner.** Liking pulls toward, disliking pushes away. In this engine a held coupling whose sign follows the relationship already exists: the cone's ω_g, set by the general person-valence rule (design-cayley-ledger.md, S37). Yu's result is in the same direction, at the person level and on a slow time scale. That matches ω's role, not a within-session gate.
- **Small and individual.** The average is zero and individual differences dominate, so a fixed innate accommodation gain would misdescribe most people. This supports keeping the voice coupling small and relationship-signed.
- **Wrong feature for the question.** VOT is segmental, not a held voice level (mean pitch, loudness, rate). Pitch was not measured. The transfer to the engine's six voice channels is by analogy only.
- **Approval.** The direction follows the listener's evaluation of the talker, built from the story's content. No reward from the talker's reactions is involved, since he never reacts. So the relevant engine analogue is approval-closed.

## Verdict: watch (attitude-signed convergence; segmental, one exposure, no control)
