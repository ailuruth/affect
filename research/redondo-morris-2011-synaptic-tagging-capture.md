# Redondo & Morris 2011, "Making memories last: the synaptic tagging and capture hypothesis" (Nature Reviews Neuroscience 12:17-30; doi 10.1038/nrn2963)

Source read: the full published PDF (15 pages), all main text, Box 1 (assigning roles to molecules), Box 2 (molecules needed for tagging), the glossary margins and all figure captions. Read on 2026-11-25. The figure panels themselves came through as garbled glyphs, so the curves and values plotted in Figs 2, 3 and 6 were not read; only the captions were. The reference list was skimmed. No equations: the review is qualitative. The state-based computational models it cites (Clopath et al. 2008, "Tag-trigger-consolidation"; Barrett et al. 2009) were not read.

## Claims
- Encoding creates the potential for a lasting change but does not commit to it: "the induction of synaptic potentiation creates only the potential for a lasting change in synaptic efficacy, but not the commitment to such a change. Other neural activity, before or after induction, can also determine whether persistent change occurs."
- Four steps (Fig. 1): "first, the expression of synaptic potentiation with the setting of a local synaptic tag; second, the synthesis and distribution of plasticity-related proteins (PRPs); third, the capture of these proteins by tagged synapses; and forth, the ultimate stabilization of synaptic strength."
- The tag is local and synapse-specific; PRPs are cell-wide and non-specific. Cross-capture shows "a common pool of PRPs ... from which all tagged synapses can benefit regardless of the direction of their synaptic change" (LTP or LTD).
- Revision 1: tag setting and early-LTP expression are dissociable. Low-dose KN-93 (CaMKII autophosphorylation) or latrunculin (actin) block the tag while E-LTP is still expressed.
- Revision 2: the tag is "a temporary structural state of the synapse that probably involves a large number of proteins", a permissive "unlocking", not one molecule.
- Revision 3: PRP stands for "plasticity-related products", including dendritic mRNAs, because translation can be compartmentalized.
- Behavioural tagging: "unexpected novelty exploration around the time of memory encoding should augment the persistence of a separate behavioural memory." The effect works both backward and forward in time.
- Cellular consolidation as a filter: "cellular consolidation can be thought of as the filter that determines the subset of newly encoded information that may be subject to systems consolidation."
- Forgetting is desirable: "a memory system that retained everything would rapidly saturate".

## Evidence (numbers as stated in the text)
- Tag lifetime: "approximately 90 min" from 'weak before strong' slice protocols; "synapses exhibiting E-LTP fail to capture new PRPs arriving more than 90 minutes after the induction of LTP".
- Tag reset: low-frequency stimulation (LFS, "250 pulses at 1 Hz") resets the tag only if given "less than 10 min" after induction (the Fig. 2 caption says "within 5 min" of strong stimulation). Later LFS depotentiates the response but leaves the tag, so the synapse can repotentiate if another pathway supplies PRPs. In mouse forebrain, CaMKII inhibition disrupts short-term memory "only when done within 10 min of encoding".
- Slow-onset LTP: a D1/D5 agonist (SKF38393) plus LFS gives potentiation that builds "over the course of 1-2 h" on active synapses only, with no immediate EPSP change.
- Novelty in vivo: unexpected exploration after LTP induction lengthens LTP/LTD persistence, and so does a water reward to a thirsty rat. The authors call this "supportive but indirect". In vivo two-pathway STC is "still not definitively established".
- Inhibitory avoidance (Moncada & Viola 2007): a weak shock gives only transient memory. Novelty before or after training makes it last, and "the 'rescue' of LTM was symmetrical". The rescue is blocked by hippocampal D1/D5 antagonist SCH23390 or by anisomycin. It generalizes to object recognition and to taste memory in insular cortex.
- Everyday spatial memory (Wang, Redondo & Morris 2010; Fig. 6): rats encode one food location per day. Memory is good at 30 min, forgotten at 24 h. Five minutes of novel-box exploration 30 min after encoding makes the 24 h memory persist. The effect depends on D1/D5 and protein synthesis. Encoding with 3 pellets instead of 1 also lasts without novelty, unless SCH23390 is present at encoding. Prior novelty rescues it again.
- Reconsolidation link: retrieval routinely tags engram synapses, which proteasomal degradation destabilizes. Normal PRP levels restabilize the synapses or let them update. Scarce PRPs, as under anisomycin, lose the engram. Proteasome inhibition prevents both the loss and new learning (Lee et al. 2008).

## Mechanism (our formalization; the paper gives none)
Each synapse i has an efficacy w_i, an early change e_i and a tag τ_i ∈ [0,1]. A cell-wide (or compartment-wide) PRP pool P(t) is driven by strong events and by neuromodulators (dopamine D1/D5 from novelty, reward).
- Encoding sets e_i and τ_i together, but they are separate variables. Both decay, e on its own time course and τ with a time constant of about 90 min.
- Consolidation: dw_i/dt ∝ τ_i(t) · P(t) · e_i(t). The persistent change happens only where tag, products and the early change overlap in time.
- P is non-specific: one salient event anywhere within about ±90 min consolidates every tagged trace, including unrelated trivial ones (the "flashbulb" peripheral details).
- Capture is competitive when P is scarce (Fonseca et al. 2004's "competitive maintenance").
- Retrieval re-tags. Re-tagged synapses need P again to persist, which is reconsolidation.
This is the shape of the Clopath and Barrett models the review cites, but we did not read them.

## Implications for the engine (our inferences)
- **The tag is the precise meaning of "state tagging".** STC gives "tag" an exact meaning: tagging marks which traces are eligible, and a separate, later, non-specific signal decides whether eligible traces are kept. For S6, each stored feature-stream segment gets an eligibility tag at encoding: a decaying trace set by local learning activity, such as prediction error or the update magnitude in the fast store. Persistence is decided later by a global consolidation signal P(t), which in our core should be a derived quantity, such as a large change in expected homeostatic error or a novelty spike, not a hand-set "importance" score. Replay priority then becomes τ_i × P integrated over the window. It is not prediction error alone, as in Schaul's |δ|.
- **The window is two-sided.** A significant moment in a session with the operator (a sharp drop in the agent's regulated variables, a repair after a rupture) should also consolidate the ordinary exchanges in the roughly 90 minutes before and after it. With a few hours a day, this is how routine operator-specific behaviour (their timing, their usual expressions) can be kept without needing to be salient itself. It is also a mechanism for relationship-specific memory: ordinary moments get bound to the significant ones they surround.
- **Forgetting by default.** A trace that is tagged but never captured decays. So the fast store can be small, with most of a day's stream dropped and only captured segments entering replay. That matches the "filter before systems consolidation" claim and keeps the buffer from saturating on months of data.
- **Direction-agnostic pool.** Capture works for LTP and LTD alike, so one consolidation signal can make both a learned approach response and a learned withdrawal response to the operator persist. The tag carries the sign; the pool does not.
- **Retrieval re-tags.** Reactivating a partner-specific memory in a new interaction makes it labile and updatable while P is available. This is a route by which the relationship model updates without overwriting other partners' mappings: only re-tagged traces are open to change. See the reconsolidation note.
- **Ablations the mechanism gives directly:** (a) remove the tag and use P alone, so everything near a salient event is kept: specificity should drop and the buffer bloat; (b) remove P and keep everything tagged, so the agent keeps everything weakly and nothing preferentially: temperament should not form around significant events; (c) make P one-sided (after only): the backward rescue of preceding ordinary moments should disappear.

## Verdict: adopt (the tag × global-signal factorization as the definition of state tagging); watch (the specific time constants)
- It fits Principle 10 only if both the tag and P come out of the core's equations. A natural derivation is an eligibility trace (the tag) multiplied by a global third factor (P), which is three-factor learning. P should be a derived quantity, such as the change in expected homeostatic deviation, and never "the operator smiled" (Principle 6).
- The 90-minute and 10-minute constants are rat-hippocampus slice values. For an agent that lives in sessions, the analogous constants are free parameters to fit, not to copy.
- In vivo STC was "not definitively established" at the time of the review. The behavioural-tagging results are the stronger evidence for our purposes.
