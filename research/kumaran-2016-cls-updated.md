# Kumaran, Hassabis & McClelland 2016, "What Learning Systems do Intelligent Agents Need? Complementary Learning Systems Theory Updated" (Trends in Cognitive Sciences 20(7):512-534; doi 10.1016/j.tics.2016.05.004)

Source read: the full-text PDF from the third author's Stanford page (stanford.edu/~jlmcc/papers/KumaranHassabisMcC16CLSUpdate.pdf), pp. 512-530. That covers all main-text sections, Boxes 1-10, the Glossary, the Outstanding Questions and the Concluding Remarks. Read on 2026-11-25. Not read: the reference list beyond the first few entries. Figures were read from their captions.

## Claims
- The core is kept: "intelligent agents must possess two learning systems". The neocortex "gradually acquires structured knowledge representations while the second quickly learns the specifics of individual experiences".
- Replay is not faithful: it "allows goal-dependent weighting of experience statistics" so "the neocortex is not a slave to the statistics of its environment". The hippocampus marks "salient but statistically infrequent experiences, thereby ensuring that such events are not swamped by the wealth of typical experiences – but instead are preferentially stabilized and replayed to the neocortex". Relevant factors are "surprising or novel; high in reward value (either positive or negative) or in their informational content".
- Significance can be assigned after the fact. Neuromodulatory mechanisms "allow the persistence of individual experiences in the hippocampus to be modulated by events that occur both before and afterwards", so "episodes may be retrospectively reweighted".
- The cost of reweighting: "in post-traumatic stress disorder, a unique aversive experience may be transformed into a persistent and dominant representation through a runaway process of repeated reactivation." An open question asks when reweighting "result[s] in a biased neocortical model of environmental structure".
- Amendment 1: slow cortical integration is required "only ... when new information is inconsistent with existing knowledge". Amendment 2: "the rate of learning in the neocortex [is] dependent on prior knowledge rather than being slow per se". Because hippocampal input comes from cortex, "hippocampal learning will also be dependent on prior knowledge".
- The hippocampus generalizes by recurrence rather than by overlapping codes. REMERGE performs "recurrent similarity computation", feeding retrieved episodes back as input until a stable state links items never seen together (A to C through AB and BC). "Generalized replay" may build "stored generalizations".
- Pattern separation is a continual-learning device in its own right, allowing "the rapid creation of distinct non-interfering representations for multiple tasks". Rodents hold more than 10 environments, each as a separate CA3 "chart".
- Which old memories are interleaved with new ones is "an open question". Options are all recent traces, related traces recruited by recurrence, or cortical patterns "consistent with the structured knowledge implicit in the neocortical network", which amounts to generative pseudo-replay.
- Outstanding questions include the "index maintenance" problem, "Are hippocampal representations updated to incorporate changes in neocortical representations ... and if so how?", the fate of traces after consolidation, and whether hippocampal traces reconsolidate.

## Evidence
- Sparsity estimates (Boxes 4-5): dentate gyrus 0.5-1% population sparseness, CA3 about 2.5%, entorhinal cortex 7-10%, V4/IT about 10% (lifetime). Rodent entorhinal cortex has about 200,000 neurons projecting to about 1 million granule cells. Each DG cell's "detonator" synapse contacts about 15 random CA3 neurons.
- Replay. Sharp-wave ripples are about 150 Hz and replay is "time-compressed by a factor of about 20". Disrupting ripples impairs systems consolidation. Blocking CA3 output optogenetically reduces SWRs and impairs consolidation of contextual fear. During slow-wave sleep, hippocampal replay precedes activity in reward-sensitive ventral striatal neurons. Replay is biased toward rewarded locations and can be non-local, form novel shortcuts, or pass through seen but unvisited space.
- Event arena (Tse et al. 2007, 2011). Six flavour-place pairs took multiple sessions over several weeks. Two new pairs were then learned from one exposure each, but only in the familiar arena. A hippocampal lesion 48 h later left them intact. Plasticity genes in neocortex were higher 80 minutes after new pairs in the familiar arena than in a novel one.
- Simulation (McClelland 2013, the paper's ref. 115). The 1995 semantic network learned schema-consistent new items fast, with large weight changes, and without interference. Inconsistent items were learned slowly and needed interleaving. Both used "the same small value of the learning rate parameter".
- DQN (Box 9): disabling experience replay dropped performance "to at best 30%" of the replay version. Prioritizing by reward prediction error "yields further gains".
- A rat water-maze study: early behaviour reflected individual episodic traces, while behaviour at 28 days was consistent with a parametric representation.

## Mechanism
- No new equations. The mechanisms are these:
  1. Interleaved replay into a slow parametric learner.
  2. Replay sampling reweighted by significance: novelty, signed reward magnitude, information gain. Weights can be set retrospectively, and storage persistence and replay probability are both affected.
  3. The effective cortical learning rate depends on the network's state: input consistent with learned structure produces large, non-interfering weight changes under a fixed ε. The authors cite Saxe et al.'s analysis of deep linear networks for why.
  4. DG/CA3 pattern separation and completion. Low input overlap is reduced further and creates a new trace; moderate or high overlap completes to a stored trace (Box 3, Fig. IC). The overlap threshold is modulated by acetylcholine.
  5. Big-loop recurrence (REMERGE): hippocampal output goes back to entorhinal cortex as input and iterates to an attractor.

## Implications for the engine (our inference, not the paper's)
- **"State-tagged replay" is significance-weighted replay, plus a known failure mode.** The paper supports replay probability set by the agent's own signals: surprise, homeostatic error and information gain. It also names the failure: an aversive episode that is replayed more becomes more dominant, so it is replayed even more. The engine needs a bounded priority, or an importance-sampling correction as in the Schaul note, so that the slow model of the operator is not biased toward rare bad moments. It also needs an explicit test, for example that the relationship estimate stays calibrated after one bad session.
- **Retrospective tagging fits scarce data.** An episode early in a session can be up-weighted by what happens later in the same session, for example a repair after a rupture. Replay weight should be computed at sleep time over the whole day's buffer, not frozen at encoding. This is the synaptic-tagging idea (see the Redondo & Morris note) at the systems level.
- **Operator-specific learning is fast where it fits the schema and slow where it does not.** Once the slow model holds a general model of people's faces and voices, operator-specific regularities that fit it can be absorbed in a few nights. Idiosyncrasies that contradict it need many interleaved nights. This predicts a two-phase S6 curve that can be measured: fast early alignment, then slow idiosyncratic fit. Building that general prior must not come from emotion datasets (Principle 1-2). It would have to come from the innate body and early living.
- **Partner as context: separate charts.** Pattern separation is the paper's continual-learning answer for multiple environments. The analogue is to treat "who I'm with" as a context that, when distinct enough, recruits a new trace population, while shared structure lives in the slow store. The partner-specific mapping is then the slow model conditioned on a learned partner embedding, trained by tagged replay. Other partners' mappings survive because their tags keep them separate in replay batches. With one operator, "others" means the engine's earlier states and the generic person model.
- **The index-maintenance question is the S6 "survive model upgrades" question.** When the conceptual layer or encoder is upgraded, stored keys must be re-derived. The paper leaves this open. Storing raw features (Principle 7) and re-encoding on replay is the engineering answer.
- **Generative pseudo-replay is endorsed as an option.** Interleaving with "cortical activity patterns consistent with the structured knowledge" licenses replaying samples from the slow model instead of storing all old episodes (see the van de Ven 2020 note).

## Verdict: adopt (significance-weighted, retrospectively assigned replay; consolidation rate that depends on prior knowledge), watch (the bias and runaway risk)
- Against Principle 10: "significance" must be a quantity the core already computes, such as prediction error on body variables or change in expected free energy, not a new module. The paper is a review with no new data or code, and its quantitative support for reweighting is DQN/PER-level, not affect-specific.
