# Affiliative zygomatic synchrony in co-present strangers (Golland, Mevorach & Levit-Binnun)

Yulia Golland, Dana Mevorach & Nava Levit-Binnun, *Scientific Reports* 9:3120, 2019, doi:10.1038/s41598-019-40060-4 (PMC6395718).

## What was read
The full PMC open-access text: introduction, methods (participants, procedure, EMG and ECG processing, synchrony computation, real vs random dyads, dyadic self-report indices), results (Table 1, figure captions), discussion and conclusions. Supplementary figures (bootstrap distributions, smile coding) not seen.

## Findings, precisely

**Design.** 26 dyads of unacquainted women, **side by side** in front of one screen, told not to talk. Non-emotional baseline (5 min), then a positive clip ("Britain's Got Talent", 364 s) and a negative clip ("Paranormal Activity", 394 s), counterbalanced. Zygomaticus EMG (left face) → 45–200 Hz power in 1-s windows (1 Hz series); heart rate (1 Hz). Synchrony = maximum cross-correlation within **±3 s**. Baseline for dyad-specific coupling: **random dyads** (people who watched the same clip in different sessions), bootstrapped.

**Zygomatic synchrony (mean real vs random; p for real > random):**
| Condition | Real | Random | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Baseline | 0.09 | 0.08 | .24 |
| Negative clip | 0.24 | 0.06 | < .001 |
| Positive clip | 0.44 | 0.30 | < .001 |

- **No coupling without a shared external driver** (baseline real ≈ random).
- In the positive clip, about two-thirds of the synchrony (0.30 of 0.44) is common stimulus drive; the dyad-specific excess is **≈ 0.14**.
- Lag of the cross-correlation peak: **0.48 s** (positive) vs **1.75 s** (negative), t = 4.65, p < .001. The authors read the slower negative-clip coupling as appraisal-mediated (smiling at each other during a horror clip).
- Shared smiles (video-coded) correlated with zygomatic synchrony (ρ ≈ 0.53 in both clips).
- Heart-rate synchrony real > random in the positive clip (0.17 vs 0.12), marginal in the negative; zygomatic and cardiac synchrony correlated in the positive clip (r = 0.45), not the negative (r = 0.05).
- Zygomatic synchrony during the **last** clip predicted post-session affiliation ratings (ρ = 0.74), during the first clip it did not (ρ = −0.04).

**Limits.** Not a conversation: partners sat side by side facing a screen, so faces were seen only peripherally or by turning. Women only, 26 dyads with exclusions per condition (n = 19–21 in some tests). Correlational.

## What it means for the designs
This is evidence *for* face-to-face smile coupling, included because it shows the conditions under which copying appears and how small its partner-specific part is.

1. **Face coupling needs a common cause.** With nothing shared to react to, real and random dyads did not differ. Most of the smile synchrony in the positive clip is the shared stimulus; the partner-specific excess is about a third. For a design: **matching on the face should be mostly explained by both parties responding to a shared event**, with a small partner-specific residual. A design where the partner's face drives the agent's face directly, without a shared referent, overproduces coupling relative to this baseline.
2. **The lag of face coupling changes with context: ~0.5 s vs ~1.75 s.** When the shared event and the smile disagree (smiling during a horror clip), coupling is slower. A fixed-delay matching path (as Hale 2020 found for head pitch) cannot produce this; the face needs at least two routes with different delays, the slower one carrying the context-dependent part. This repeats the fast-match-then-context pattern in the mimicry synthesis.
3. **Time scale of relational consequence:** coupling in the second 6-min clip, not the first, predicted affiliation — the coupling that matters builds over ~10 min.
4. **Test to borrow:** the random-dyad baseline (same stimulus, different session) is the right control for any agent evaluation that shares stimuli with the partner. The agent's face–partner coupling must be compared with its coupling to a recording of a different partner exposed to the same events.

## Verdict
**watch (evidence for coupling, used as a calibration)** — dyad-specific smile coupling exists but is a small excess over shared-stimulus drive (0.44 vs 0.30), absent without a shared driver, and slower (1.75 s vs 0.48 s) when the shared context runs against the smile; side-by-side co-viewing, not conversation.
