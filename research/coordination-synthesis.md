# Coordination synthesis: how interpersonal coupling is organized beyond one gain

Written 2026-11-25. It draws on fourteen notes written the same day, and on notes already in the index where they are named.

**The question.** In all twelve sketches, the partner enters the agent through one scalar. Turning it runs the agent from flat affect to copying (research/foundations/sketches/v2-README.md). The harness's dial analysis (research/harness/HARNESS.md, D75) asks whether people show only a gain change across conditions, or a change in the response profile itself. This note collects what the coordination and dyadic-affect literatures say about that question before any Seamless data is run.

## 1. The structure the evidence supports

Each item below is something a model with one gain on a fixed partner-to-self map cannot produce. "Fixed map" means a delayed or filtered identity map, or any fixed matrix scaled by one scalar. The strength of the evidence is stated with each item.

### 1a. The sign or mode of coupling is a separate parameter from its strength

- **HKB and the virtual partner.** In the VP coupling K = (A + B(x − μy)²)(ẋ − μẏ), the sign of μ, not its size, decides whether in-phase or anti-phase is the stable mode (Kelso 2009).
- **Couples' physiology changes sign with context.** Reed 2013 (44 couples, blood pressure) found anti-phase linkage when influence was low and in-phase linkage when it was high. Liu 2013 found negative diurnal cortisol linkage at low strain and positive linkage at high strain (both read via Timmons 2015).
- **The sign differs by channel within one pattern.** In Kuelz 2022 (53 couples), the "disengaged" profile was in-phase in skin conductance (EDA) and anti-phase in vagal heart-rate variability (RSA).
- **The sign differs by content.** In Dimascio 1957, a single therapy case, heart rates moved together in tension segments and oppositely in antagonism segments (via Kleinbub 2017).
- **One dyad holds two couplings of opposite sign at once.** Turn-taking is anti-phase on readiness to speak while speech rate aligns in phase (Fusaroli 2014, theory).
- **Hasson & Frith 2016** generalize this: alignment, the identity map, is only one of three coupling types. The second is conditional transformation, a lawful non-identity map such as threat in one face predicting fear in the other. The third is synergy, mutual constraint toward a joint function.

A gain on an identity map has only a positive sign. Scaling a fixed signed matrix keeps its signs, so a context-dependent sign flip is a profile change by definition. **Strength:** consistent across motor, conversational and physiological work, but the affective sign-flip evidence is small-sample physiology at the scale of minutes to days, and most of it was read through a review.

### 1b. Coupling is defined on a joint or task variable, not on the partner's surface

- **Reciprocal compensation.** In a synergy, one person's deviation is cancelled by the other's opposite deviation. The uncontrolled-manifold (UCM) ratio of compensated to uncompensated variance was 2.31 for in-phase and 1.68 for anti-phase interpersonal coordination, against 2.71 within one person (Riley 2011; secondary summaries, no n). Copying produces positive covariance, which is the opposite pattern.
- **Synergy predicts outcome better than alignment.** In Fusaroli & Tylén 2016, recurrence on the merged, role-blind turn sequence predicted joint performance (r = .89), while cross-partner alignment did not (r = .58, p = .17). Adding alignment added nothing. With n = 16 dyads and stepwise selection over 18 predictors, this is likely overfit. Indiscriminate alignment hurt performance and selective alignment helped (Fusaroli 2012, via the 2014 note, not read in its primary source).
- **Coregulation is negative feedback on the joint deviation.** Butler & Randall 2013 define coregulation as a bidirectional, damped oscillation back to a stable level. They call contagion and reciprocity, which are copying, positive feedback (morphogenic) rather than regulation, and they say correlation alone cannot tell the two apart. They also say there is no conclusive evidence that adult coregulation of this form exists.

### 1c. Roles are asymmetric, and they emerge and can swap

- **Leader and follower emerge without instruction.** In the collision-avoidance task (Richardson 2015, 11 pairs), the correlation between ellipticality difference and coupling directionality rose across blocks from .08 to .37 to .85. Its model needs participant-specific repeller strengths γ. Equal γ gives no lag and no role.
- **Lag asymmetry marks the leader.** Mutually adapting tappers show negative lag-0 and positive lag-±1 interval correlations, and the +1/−1 asymmetry indexes who leads. In Vesper's jumping task, the partner with the easier jump did the adapting (Hasson & Frith 2016, secondary).
- **One partner damps while the other amplifies.** In Kuelz 2022's coregulatory profiles, women amplified in RSA to counterbalance the men.
- **The two directions differ.** Man-to-woman and woman-to-man linkage relate differently to satisfaction (Hubler 2013 and Helm 2012, via Timmons).
- **The role effect is in direction, not amount.** Secure-attachment priming raised synchrony in which the therapist led, but not overall synchrony (Palmieri, 18 dyads, via Kleinbub).

A symmetric scalar gain has no direction. A role is an asymmetry in who carries which coupling, channel by channel.

### 1d. Coupling differs by channel and time scale

- **Physiological subsystems differ in time scale, and their linkage means different things.** The mean oscillation period was 4.2 min for EDA and 2.7 min for RSA (Kuelz). Cortisol linkage goes with poorer relationship quality in 8 of 9 studies, while RSA linkage goes with higher satisfaction (Timmons).
- **The slowest scale.** The only adult coupled-oscillator affect result Butler & Randall cite (Butner 2007) found coupling for positive affect only, at about one cycle per 5 days.
- **Levels.** Low levels (posture, speech rate) align, and higher levels complement (Fusaroli 2014). This is asserted from a review, not measured.
- **Within a session.** Alignment falls while complexity matching rises, and the rise predicts performance (Dale 2013; no numbers in the chapter).
- **Lags.** The listener's brain follows the speaker's at about 3 s (Stephens 2010, via Hasson & Frith).
- **Against this, at the fastest scale imitation is real.** Facial EMG mimicry is clear after 500 ms (dimberg-thunberg-2012-rapid-facial-reactions.md). Copying the speaker beat every learned REACT baseline on correlation (react-challenge-baselines.md).

So the honest picture is imitation-like coupling at sub-second facial scales, alongside complementary and regulatory coupling at turn, task and physiological scales. A single gain must choose one point for all of them.

### 1e. Tracking the partner is separate from aligning with the partner

- **Two stages.** Hoehl et al. 2021 separate automatic tracking of the partner's timing regularities, which is always on, from alignment, which is gated by relationship and socio-emotional value.
- **Gated by relationship.** Children aged 5–9 synchronized brain activity with their mother but not with a stranger (Reindl 2018, via Hoehl).
- **A threshold, not a gain.** Anxious caregivers responded to small fluctuations in their infant's arousal, while non-anxious caregivers responded only to peaks (Wass 2019, via Hoehl).
- **Anticipation without a model.** A delay-coupled term k(x − y(t−τ)) leads the partner without any internal model, and people lead a chaotic target at 200–400 ms feedback delay (Stepp 2009, n = 10, t(9) = 4.74). This removes lag, but its output is still a leading copy. It is a way to track, not a way to complement.

This matches the internal-model principle already in the index (francis-wonham-1976-internal-model-principle.md): robust regulation needs a model of the exosystem that generates the cancelling input, not a copy of the other. In all twelve sketches, one map did both jobs.

### 1f. The target regime is metastable, and more synchrony is not better

- **Dwell and escape.** Tognoli & Kelso describe coordination as dwelling near the ghosts of 0 and π and then escaping, when detuning δω ≠ 0 and coupling is weak.
- **Conflicting intentions produce new patterns.** In VPI, a human told to be in-phase against a VP set to anti-phase produced joint patterns neither chose: at low frequency 42.5% of trials were stable, and at high frequency 65% were unstable (n = 10).
- **The best level is mid-range.** Both very high and very low prosodic synchrony predicted insecure infant attachment (Jaffe 2001, via Kleinbub). Hoehl argues a model of human coordination needs a way to leave synchrony, and cites Dahan 2016's term that builds up during lock and then triggers escape.
- **More coordination can go with worse outcomes.** Cortisol correlation was higher in dyads with less behavioural coordination (Saxbe 2017, via Hoehl).

A gain run upward produces monotonically more lock. These sources say the human operating point is intermittent coupling.

### 1g. Partner-specific state builds slowly and does not vanish with the partner

- **The hidden regulator.** Sbarra & Hazan 2008 (abstract only) describe the partner as a learned, partner-specific "hidden regulator". Losing the partner causes dysregulation, not a return to the uncoupled baseline.
- **Roles form over time.** In Richardson 2015, roles formed over about 21 trials and held within a pair.

A one-gain model with the partner removed simply returns to baseline.

**Hazard.** An agent designed to become the user's hidden regulator would, by this account, cause dysregulation when it is removed. This is the dependence risk in knox-2025-harmful-traits-ai-companions.md and the unbounded-b warning in solomon-corbit-1974-opponent-process.md.

## 3. What this means for the design target (requirements, not a design)

**From the evidence, a coupling that is not a single dial needs at least:**
1. Signed coupling terms specific to channel and lag, whose signs can differ between channels at the same moment (1a, 1d).
2. A context or role state that changes the signs and the direction of those terms, not only their size (1a, 1c).
3. Partner tracking that is separate from what the agent expresses, so the partner can be modelled fully and still not copied (1e).
4. Regulation defined on a joint deviation (damping toward a set point) rather than on the partner's surface (1b).
5. An escape from lock, so the operating point is intermittent (1f).
6. Slow, partner-specific state, bounded so that losing the partner does not create dependence (1g).

**Two cautions.**
- Requirement 2 can hide a new dial. If one scalar context variable flips every sign together, the system is still one-dimensional. The profile test in D75 must check that profiles differ in more than one direction.
- None of the published formal models learns its role or sign parameters online. μ is set by the experimenter, γ is set by hand, and the rties parameters are fitted after the fact, per dyad. How such structure is acquired in real time from a few hours of data is not answered anywhere read here.

**Additions the evidence suggests for the dial analysis.** These are not yet decided; they belong to the operator.
- **Report sign as well as norm.** Report the sign pattern of B (diagonal and off-diagonal, per lag bin) by condition, not only ‖P(familiar) − P(stranger)‖_F. A sign flip in a few cells is the specific human signature from 1a.
- **Hold role fixed.** Speaker and listener roles almost certainly differ in profile (for example jaw anti-phase at turns). Familiar and stranger should be compared within the same role, or the test detects turn-taking.
- **Add lag asymmetry and directionality per pair** (the lag ±1 asymmetry and the d index) as role measures.
- **Coregulation needs a disturbance.** Without one, contagion and coregulation are indistinguishable (Butler & Randall). In naturalistic corpora the nearest disturbances are topic shifts or the partner's laughter onsets. The still-face episode (mesman-2009-still-face-meta-analysis.md) is the designed one.
- **Do not reward more synchrony.** The E3 "coupling beyond surrogates" criterion in evaluation.md should not be read as "higher is better". The human optimum appears to be mid-range (1f).

## 4. Open problems

1. **No within-person, profile-versus-gain test exists for facial affect.**
   - The sign-flip evidence comes from autonomic and cortisol data at minute-to-day scales. Samples are 53 couples or fewer, and several primary papers were read only as abstracts.
   - Kuelz shows profiles differing between couples, not within a person across partners or conditions.
   - So the D75 question is open in the literature, not only in this project.
2. **Fast and slow coupling must coexist.** Facial mimicry at about 500 ms is imitation-like, and complementarity is documented mainly at turn, task and physiological scales. The claim that low levels align while high levels complement is asserted, not measured. Nothing read shows the same dyad doing both on facial channels.
3. **How role and sign are selected and learned** is unmodelled. Every equation here takes them as given or fits them afterwards.
4. **Synergy is hard to identify.** Merged-sequence recurrence is role-blind and cannot say whose contribution mattered. TE cannot separate unique from synergistic information. The UCM needs a known task variable, which affect does not have.
5. **Coregulation is not established in adults.** Its own proponents say the evidence is inconclusive. In the rties example data, coupling added about 2% R². The case that adult affect coregulates, as opposed to co-escalating, rests on theory and small samples.
6. **Metastability in affect dyads is unmeasured.** No dwell-and-escape statistics for facial affect were found.
7. **Real-time human–machine evidence is thin.** VPI (n = 10, fingers) and Zhang 2016 (arousal higher when coordination was stable) are the only real-time human–artificial-partner coupling studies read, and in both the machine's coupling was fixed.
8. **Hidden-regulator harm.** An agent that coregulates well is, on Sbarra & Hazan's account, one whose loss dysregulates. Partner-specific slow state needs a bound in the design, not only in evaluation.
