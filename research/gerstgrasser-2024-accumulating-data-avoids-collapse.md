# Gerstgrasser, Schaeffer, Dey, Rafailov, Pai et al. 2024: accumulating real and synthetic data avoids model collapse

Gerstgrasser, M., Schaeffer, R., Dey, A., Rafailov, R., Pai, D., Sleight, H., Hughes, J., Korbak, T., Agrawal, R., Gromov, A., Roberts, D. A., Yang, D., Donoho, D. & Koyejo, S. (2024). "Is Model Collapse Inevitable? Breaking the Curse of Recursion by Accumulating Real and Synthetic Data." arXiv:2404.01413 v2 (29 April 2024). https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.01413.

Read as the counterpart to Shumailov et al.: it names which data regime produces collapse and which guard removes it.

**How fully read:** arXiv v2 main text read in full on 2026-11-28 (§1–4: the language-model, diffusion and VAE experiments, the linear-regression theory with Theorems 1–2, and the Discussion), including footnotes. ## What it says
- **Two regimes.**
  - *Replace:* each generation trains only on the previous generation's samples. Most collapse papers assume this.
  - *Accumulate:* each generation trains on the original real data plus all synthetic data so far.
- **Experiments (each generation trained from scratch).**
  - GPT-2 (9M) and Llama-2 (12M, 42M, 126M) on TinyStories: replace raises test cross-entropy every generation; accumulate keeps it equal or lower, at temperatures 1.0 and 0.3.
  - GeoDiff diffusion on molecular conformations: replace degrades at once and stays bad; accumulate stays flat over 8 generations.
  - VAE on CelebA: replace collapses to one mode. Accumulate slows the rise of test error but does not stop it; minor modes (glasses, accessories) are lost.
  - Ablation: replace with a synthetic set grown to the accumulated size still degrades, more slowly. So the protection comes from keeping the real data, not from having more data.
- **Theory (linear regression, after Dohmatob et al. 2024).** Each generation fits ŵ_n by least squares to targets generated by ŵ_{n−1} plus fresh noise, T samples per generation, dimension d, noise σ².
  - Replace: E_test = σ²d/(T − d − 1) × n, which grows linearly in the number of generations.
  - Accumulate: E_test = σ²d/(T − d − 1) × Σ_{i=1}^{n} 1/i² ≤ σ²d/(T − d − 1) × π²/6, bounded for every n. Generation i's noise enters with weight 1/i, so its squared effect is 1/i², and Σ 1/i² converges.
  - The halfway regime (replace with a synthetic set of size iT) grows like log n.
- **Their caveat.** "Model collapse" names at least four different things: unbounded error, collapse to a few modes, collapse to uniformity, and amplification of artefacts. Results on one need not carry to another.

## What matters for this engine
- **What decides collapse is whether real data stay in the mix, not whether self-generated data are present.** The engine's replay buffer holds only real recorded moments, but it holds them for only 30 days (Q34 item 6). The data are real; the *weights* are carried forward from night to night and pulled toward θ₀. Two derivations follow (mine, new-core-search-16-synthesis.md §3):
  - **The pull to θ₀ is a parameter-space version of keeping p_0.** With a quadratic loss of curvature H and pull λ, each night's fixed point is (H + λ)⁻¹(H θ_data + λ θ₀). The noise from one night's finite data then decays geometrically across nights instead of adding up as a random walk. So the stored weights do not undergo Shumailov's random walk. The cost is that anything learned decays toward θ₀ unless the 30-day window keeps re-supporting it.
  - **Deleting data at 30 days is the "replace" regime for anything older.** After that, what the engine knew about a month-old moment exists only in weights that are themselves pulled toward θ₀. Nothing older than 30 days is re-supported by real data. This is forgetting, not collapse: weights return to the innate prior, not to a degenerate point. It is the right direction for safety, but it means the relationship of S6 rests on the last 30 days plus the slots and snippets.
- **The co-adaptation loop (Π̃ ↔ Δ) has no real-data anchor at all.** Both are fitted to the agent's own output. The only anchors are the parameter pulls (Π̃ to Π̃₀, Δ to 0). The paper's lesson suggests a data-level anchor: mix fixed innate pairs (Π₀'s own state → face map, which is innate and never changes) into Π̃'s nightly fit. That is the analogue of accumulating real data.

## Verdict: adopt (the regime distinction and the bound as design guidance for the nightly loops)
The theory is for linear models trained from scratch. The engine fine-tunes from yesterday's weights with a pull, which the paper does not analyse. The derivation for that case is mine.
