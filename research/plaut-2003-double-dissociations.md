# Plaut 2003, "Interpreting double dissociations in connectionist networks" (Cortex 39:138-141, discussion forum)

Source read: full text from Plaut's CMU page (Plaut03Cortex.DD-forum.pdf). Juola & Plunkett 2000 ("Why double dissociations don't mean much", in Cohen, Johnston & Plunkett (Eds.), Exploring Cognition, pp. 319-327) was NOT read. The eScholarship copy of the 1998 CogSci version refused automated download. Its claim below is as Plaut reports it.

## Claims
- In Plaut (1995), with "1000 instances of each lesion type", the variability across instances "is sufficiently large to give rise to statistically reliable double dissociations within each condition — that is, between pairs of individual lesions at the same location and severity." Such dissociations "are essentially chance occurrences that provide no information about the functional organization of the system."
- Juola & Plunkett (2000), as reported here, argued from inflectional morphology that the same concern applies to single-case human neuropsychology.
- Plaut's reply: a network lesion samples far fewer elements than a brain lesion does. Its variance is therefore inflated (by the Central Limit Theorem), so "interpreting idiosyncratic effects of individual lesions to networks is likely to be misleading." By contrast, "effects of damage in connectionist networks that are based on the mean rather than the variance resulting from multiple lesions ... can be informative".

## Evidence
A commentary. It re-presents Plaut (1995) Figure 1b: distributions of the difference in correct performance between concrete and abstract words after 20% O→I and 70% S→C lesions.

## Verdict: adopt (as a rule for reading ablations)
An ablation result for the engine is the mean over many random instances and seeds, with its distribution shown. A difference between single lesion instances, or between single training runs, is not evidence. Failure: the claimed effect lies inside the spread of effects produced by equivalent lesions (same site, same size, different random draw).
