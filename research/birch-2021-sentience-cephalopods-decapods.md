# Birch, Burn, Schnell, Browning & Crump 2021, "Review of the Evidence of Sentience in Cephalopod Molluscs and Decapod Crustaceans" (LSE Consulting report for the UK Department for Environment, Food and Rural Affairs (Defra), November 2021)

Source read: the full report PDF from lse.ac.uk. Read verbatim: §1.6 (criteria) and §1.7 (grading scheme), plus the executive summary. The taxon-by-taxon evidence chapters were not read in detail. Note on authors: the brief's "Birch, Schnell & Clayton 2020" is a different paper ("Dimensions of animal consciousness", TiCS 2020), which I did not read. The eight criteria are in this 2021 report.

## Claims
The eight criteria, verbatim:
1. "The animal possesses receptors sensitive to noxious stimuli (nociceptors)."
2. "The animal possesses integrative brain regions capable of integrating information from different sensory sources."
3. "The animal possesses neural pathways connecting the nociceptors to the integrative brain regions."
4. "The animal's behavioural response to a noxious stimulus is modulated by chemical compounds affecting the nervous system". This holds through (a) "an endogenous neurotransmitter system that modulates (in a way consistent with the experience of pain, distress or harm) its responses" and/or (b) "Putative local anaesthetics, analgesics (such as opioids), anxiolytics or anti-depressants" that modify responses in that way.
5. "The animal shows motivational trade-offs, in which the disvalue of a noxious or threatening stimulus is weighed (traded-off) against the value of an opportunity for reward, leading to flexible decision-making."
6. "The animal shows flexible self-protective behaviour (e.g. wound-tending, guarding, grooming, rubbing) of a type likely to involve representing the bodily location of a noxious stimulus."
7. "The animal shows associative learning in which noxious stimuli become associated with neutral stimuli, and/or in which novel ways of avoiding noxious stimuli are learned through reinforcement. Note: habituation and sensitisation are not sufficient".
8. "The animal shows that it values a putative analgesic or anaesthetic when injured", by (a) learning to self-administer it, (b) learning to prefer a location where it is available, or (c) "prioritis[ing] obtaining these compounds over other needs (such as food) when injured."

How the criteria are scored:
- Confidence levels per criterion: "very high confidence, high confidence, medium confidence, low confidence, very low confidence and no confidence".
- "Very high" means "no room for reasonable doubt"; "high" means "convinced ... even though some room for reasonable doubt remains".
- Grading by the number of criteria met with high or very high confidence:
  - "7-8 criteria are satisfied: Very strong evidence of sentience."
  - "5-6 ... Strong evidence".
  - "3-4 ... Substantial evidence".
  - "2 ... Some evidence".
  - "0-1 ... Sentience unknown or unlikely".
- "No single criterion is intended as a 'smoking gun'". Criterion 1 "could easily be satisfied by a non-sentient animal"; "Criterion 8 provides particularly compelling evidence". The scheme is "a rule of thumb", not a weighted score.
- Calibration: lab rats satisfy all eight; cnidarians "would not convincingly satisfy any".

## Evidence
A systematic review of over 300 studies. Results: very strong evidence for octopods, strong evidence for true crabs, and substantial evidence for squid, cuttlefish and some decapods.

## Verdict: watch (not usable as published on this agent)
The criteria assume an animal: nociceptors, neural pathways, drugs. Criteria 1-4 and 8 have no published adaptation to an artificial agent. Criteria 5-7 (motivational trade-offs, location-specific self-protection, associative avoidance learning) have functional analogues the engine could show. But satisfying them would be an adapted result, to be reported separately. Birch's own gaming problem (birch-2024-edge-of-sentience-gaming-problem.md) also applies to any part of the engine that runs through Claude.

Failure, if used: the confidence grading must be done by someone other than the builders, the way the report's own authors graded it. If the analogues of criteria 5-7 appear only in Claude-generated text, and not in the engine's non-linguistic choices, they count as gamed and score zero.
