# Heerey & Gilder 2019, "The subjective value of a smile alters social behaviour" (PLoS ONE 14(12):e0225284, doi 10.1371/journal.pone.0225284)

Link: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6886806/ (open access). Data: OSF mxt63 (not opened).

## What was read
The full PMC text: introduction, both experiments (methods, results, discussion), general discussion, table 1. Supporting files (pilot validation of the false feedback, the non-social affect control experiment) were not opened; their results are used only as the main text states them.

## What it says
- **Experiment 1 (N = 90, 30 per condition).** Participants expected to meet a partner and got false feedback: acceptance ("looking forward to meeting you"), rejection ("not looking forward to meeting you"), or negative non-rejection ("not looking forward to meeting anyone"). Then a smile-valuation task: six computer "opponents" (photos), 3 blocks × 60 exposure trials (30 per face); three faces gave a win on 80 % of trials, three on 60 %, regardless of choice; on wins one face of each pair smiled genuinely (AU12 + AU6), one politely (AU12 only), one stayed neutral with text. Test: 120 choices between pairs. A per-participant logistic model θ = β_money X_money + β_genuine X_genuine + β_polite X_polite gives each smile's value in money.
  - Genuine smiles were valued by all groups (1.39, 0.97, 1.62 pence; no group difference, F(2,87) = 0.96).
  - Polite smiles were valued by the acceptance (1.03 p) and negative-control (0.66 p) groups but became **aversive** after rejection (−0.85 p; group effect F(2,87) = 14.56, η²p = .25). Money's weight did not change. Smile discrimination (d′ ≈ 1.6–1.9) and bias did not change.
- **Experiment 2 (72 same-sex dyads, 24 per condition).** One member got the same feedback; the other none. Free 5-min conversation on video; hand-coded smile onsets and type (κ .93 genuine, .91 polite). A partner smile counted as returned if the target smiled the same type within 4 s.
  - Genuine-smile return did not differ by condition (F(2,69) = 1.29). Polite-smile return was lower after rejection (F(2,69) = 10.14, η²p = .23; lower than acceptance, p < .001, and negative control, p = .002). Return rates are shown only in a figure; the text cites 70–80 % return in natural interactions from earlier work.
  - Polite-smile return predicted the naive partner's liking of the target (β = .33, p = .009) after feedback type and the target's own liking; genuine-smile return did not.

## What matters for this engine
- **Per-expression gating within one session, set by one piece of information about the partner.** A single sentence about the partner's attitude changed return of one smile type and not the other, over a 5-min conversation. It is gating by type within the smile channels: the polite smile (lip pull without cheek raise) lost its return; the genuine one (with cheek raise) kept it.
- **The authors' mechanism is approval-open.** Their account is that the subjective value of a polite smile, as a reward, falls when social need rises, and responses to a cue follow its value. Experiment 1 cannot be read as prediction: money was held fixed across smile types, the smiles predicted nothing beyond the same win, and the devaluation generalised to unrelated photographed faces. That is the partner's expression valued as a reward, which Principle 6 forbids the engine.
- **An approval-closed reading fits Experiment 2's overall reduction, not its type selectivity.** The rejection was conveyed in words, before the conversation; a gate that reads the partner's earlier words (T) and closes smile return after rejecting words would produce fewer returns without any reward. But such a gate closes all smile return alike. Keeping genuine return while dropping polite return needs the return to depend on the partner's own cheek-raise at that moment.
- **For the build core.** The echo gate g reads his words, his masked face and the agent's own face, and multiplies the whole smile–laugh group; it reads none of his LP, CR, BU_L, RHY or BRE. It can reach "fewer smiles returned after his rejecting words" (if recorded listeners show it, F15), not "fewer polite but not fewer genuine returns". The type selectivity is out of reach under the gate's input rule.

## Limits
Return rates and effect directions in Experiment 2 are partly reported only in figures; hand-coded categorical smiles at 25 fps; Bangor undergraduates; the manipulation is one false sentence; the rejection condition also gives a partner who is plausibly less pleasant, which the authors do not separate from smile value.

## Verdict: watch (per-type gating in a real conversation, set by words about the partner; the mechanism is approval-open and is not adopted)
