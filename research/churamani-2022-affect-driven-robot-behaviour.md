## Claims
- Robots should not map an observed human expression one-to-one onto a robot action. Instead the robot keeps an "intrinsic mood" towards each user, shaped by a per-user affective memory and by an "affective core" of dispositions (patience, emotional actuation), and learns task behaviour from that mood.
- Different affective cores give significantly different mood trajectories for identical input videos (Table 3), and participants in a negotiation game perceive a "patient high-arousal" robot as more persistent and an "impatient low-arousal" robot as more generous and altruistic.
- RO-MAN 2020: continual learning (CL) is "an essential paradigm" for affective robots, because data arrive one user and one context at a time; personalised perception and behaviour learning are both recast as CL problems ⟨h_{i−1}, Tr_i, M_{i−1}, C_u, t_i⟩ → ⟨h_i, M_i⟩, with user attributes C_u and task context T_i.

## Architecture (as given)
Four assembled components (Figure 1):
1. Perception. A multi-channel CNN (MCCNN): face channel on 64×64 greyscale mean faces every 500 ms (two conv layers, 9×9 and 7×7 with shunting inhibition, FC 512); audio channel on 64-bin mel-spectrograms per 500 ms (two conv layers, FC 512); fused FC 1024 → FC 200 → a 2-unit linear head that regresses arousal and valence. Trained supervised: face channel on Aff-Wild plus AFEW-VA arousal-valence labels; then RAVDESS and SAVEE faces are labelled by the face channel and those labels train the audio-visual net. After training the head is removed, the 200-d features are kept, and the same head later converts prototypes back to arousal-valence.
2. Perception-GWR. A Growing-When-Required network (Marsland et al. 2002) prototypes the 200-d features unsupervised (458 neurons on about 20k points); its two best-matching units per 500 ms stand for the current percept.
3. Affective memory, affective core and mood, all Gamma-GWR networks (Parisi et al. 2017) with recurrent context. Distance d_i = α_w‖x(t) − w_i‖² + Σ_k α_k‖C_k(t) − c_{i,k}‖² (eq. 2), global context C_k(t) = β w_{b(t−1)} + (1 − β) c_{k−1,b(t−1)} (eq. 3), updates Δw_i = ε_i η_i (x − w_i), Δc_{i,k} = ε_i η_i (C_k − c_{i,k}), firing-counter habituation Δη_i = τ_i κ (1 − η_i) − τ_i (eqs. 4-6).
   - Affective memory: one Gamma-GWR per user, 10 context steps, sized to remember "at least one complete interaction (5–8 s)".
   - Time-perception core: trained on a hand-made decay y = exp(−τ x) from a fixed start (arousal, valence) = (0.5, 0.5), τ = 0.01 "patient" or 0.08 "impatient".
   - Social-conditioning core: trained on high-arousal (A > 0.3, "excitatory") or low-arousal (A < 0.05, "inhibitory") video segments.
   - Mood: a Gamma-GWR fed every 500 ms with 2 perception BMUs (as arousal-valence), the mean of the user's affective memory, 5 BMUs from the social-conditioning core and 2 from the time core. Mood is the mean arousal-valence of all mood neurons. More BMUs from a core means more weight for it; the weighting is chosen by hand.
4. Behaviour. DDPG actor-critic (actor 4 → 50 → 1, critic 50+50 → 10 → 1). State = (mood arousal, mood valence, last rejected offer r, h). Action = a real-valued change of the offer. Reward = offer reward (positive for raising the respondent's share while keeping own share above 50%) + mood reward (positive for a positive cosine change in mood). Pre-trained on synthetic users: 20 encoded videos plus 500 random arousal-valence vectors with an exponential decay, and a hand-set acceptance rule p(accept) = 1 if offer ≥ 0.7, = offer if 0.5-0.7, 0.1 if 0.4-0.5, else 0 (eq. 7).

What is innate (hand-set) vs learned: the perception net is supervised on human-annotated arousal-valence; the GWR prototypes are learned unsupervised; the "dispositions" are hand-designed decay constants and hand-picked training clips, then frozen; the mood is an average; the policy is learned by RL on synthetic users before the study. Emotion dimensions are named (arousal, valence) from the start.

Continual-learning mechanism: GWR growth (add a neuron when activation falls below a threshold, habituation slows updates) is the only CL element in use. The RO-MAN paper lists four CL families (regularisation, rehearsal, dynamic architectures, CLS-inspired with generative replay) and NI / NC / NIC settings, but in the user study "the affective core models are pre-trained and used after freezing the weights". Only the per-user affective memory and mood adapt during the session.

## Evidence with numbers
- Perception: face channel CCC 0.68 arousal, 0.57 valence (Aff-Wild + AFEW-VA, 60:20:20 split); audio-visual MCCNN CCC 0.75 arousal, 0.53 valence.
- Mood under cores (40 test videos from KT Emotion and OMG-Emotion, same inputs, only the core changed, two-sided Mann-Whitney vs "No Core", Table 3): impatient differs on both arousal and valence (p < 0.0001); patient differs only on valence (p = 0.020); excitatory and inhibitory differ only on arousal (p < 0.0001, p = 0.003); impatient low-arousal differs on both (p < 0.0001).
- RL pre-training: converges to offers of 40-60% in about 10 interactions (Figure 7).
- User study: n = 31 university students and staff (20 m, 11 f, 16 countries, mostly aged 25-34), one session each, between-group (patient high-arousal n = 16, impatient low-arousal n = 15), each also seeing a No Core baseline in randomised order. First offer forced to a random unfair 1-20 points; abort after 20 rejections.
  - Interactions to acceptance: baseline 8.71 ± 0.64, patient high-arousal 9.35 ± 1.13, impatient low-arousal 8.33 ± 1.20; pairwise tests not significant (p > 0.05); Hedges' G 0.77, 0.44, 0.87 reported instead.
  - Accepted offer 44 / 45 / 43 points; success rate 90% / 87% / 80%; offered ≥ 50%: 77% / 62% / 80%; final offer if rejected 49 / 47 / 50 (G > 2.0 between conditions). None of the success or accepted-offer differences significant.
  - GODSPEED and Mind Perception: no significant differences on any dimension. Asch impressions: "generous" (U = 67, p = 0.018) and "altruistic" (U = 74, p = 0.033) favour impatient low-arousal; "persistent" (U = 75, p = 0.034) favours patient high-arousal in a post-hoc test with the hypothesis direction reversed. Ten dimensions tested, one-sided, no correction for multiple comparisons.
- The authors' own limitation: "the general perception of the robot did not change", because conditions differ only in how offers are updated; speech content and facial expressions were identical across conditions.

## Failure modes
- Classifier at the front. Perception is a supervised arousal-valence regressor trained on human-annotated datasets, and the robot's mood is literally built from those regressed values. This is the "emotion read as labels" route that Principle 1 forbids, in its dimensional form. The paper's claim that the robot does not "merely mimic" rests on averaging the user's regressed affect with hand-made disposition prototypes, not on reading the user through the robot's own body.
- Assembled modules. CNN, three kinds of GWR, a hand-weighted average (2 vs 5 vs 2 BMUs) and DDPG, each trained separately with its own objective; no single objective links them. The RO-MAN paper adds CL as a further layer on top.
- Hand-named, hand-set dispositions. "Patience" is a chosen τ; "emotional actuation" is a choice of training clips. Temperament is installed and frozen, not grown from living.
- Rewarded for the user's positive affect. The mood reward pays the robot for a positive change in a mood that is mostly the user's regressed valence, so the policy is trained to evoke positive user expressions. Participants noticed and exploited it: some "followed a fawning approach trying to manipulate the robot by smiling more often", and it worked "to some extent" (offers up to 52%). This is the flattery and manipulation loop Principle 6 forbids, observed with the roles reversed.
- Persistence. Mood starts from the same state each session; affective memory spans 5-8 s of context per user; no multi-day test. The conclusion calls for longitudinal studies.
- Ablation. The No Core condition is a genuine lesion of the core and was run both offline (Table 3) and with users. But the behavioural effects with users were not significant pairwise, the face and speech did not depend on the state, and no component other than the core (perception-GWR, affective memory, mood reward) was removed.

## Verdict: reject as an architecture (classifier input, assembled modules, mood reward trains toward user smiles); watch for two specifics: the No-Core lesion design with identical inputs, and the fawning observation as a documented failure case.

## What it implies for this engine
- This is the clearest recent example of the failure Principle 1 and Principle 6 exist to prevent, with a reported human consequence: a reward on positive change in the robot's user-derived mood let users steer the robot by smiling. Cite it when defending "no reward for the user's smiles".
- Its "affective core" is the nearest prior-work analogue of temperament, and it is a hand-set exponential decay. The engine's temperament has to be a slow manifold of the learned state that forms over S6, with an ablation that shows it is specific to the operator; this paper shows what a hand-installed version looks like and that users barely notice it when the face and words do not depend on it.
- Its own explanation of the null GODSPEED result supports the brief's stance: a state that changes only a scalar decision, while the face and speech stay fixed, is not perceived. The state must reach the face, the voice and Claude's processing, not one policy output.
- Good method to copy: replay the same recorded input through the engine with one structure removed and compare state trajectories with a nonparametric test (Table 3). The engine can do this on published dyadic recordings, which fits the brief's rule that derived behaviours are tested on published data.
- Continual learning: the RO-MAN taxonomy (regularisation, rehearsal, dynamic growth, CLS replay; NI/NC/NIC) is standard and is covered in more depth by mcclelland-1995-complementary-learning-systems.md, ewc-si-2017-weight-importance.md and van-de-ven-2020-brain-inspired-replay.md. The useful additions are two recommendations: collect a per-user normative baseline in neutral introduction rounds and treat deviations from it as signal, and allow controlled forgetting. Both should come out of the engine's replay and homeostatic equations rather than be added as modules.
- The paper is evidence for the brief's prior-work claim in a second lineage (the Hamburg/Cambridge affective-robotics line): continual learning was proposed, but the deployed system froze its dispositions, ran single short sessions, and named its affect dimensions.
