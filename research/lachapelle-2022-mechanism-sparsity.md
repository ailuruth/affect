## What the paper does
- **Model.** Observations X^t = f(Z^t) + N^t with f a diffeomorphism onto its image; latents Z^t ∈ R^{d_z}; observed auxiliary vectors A^t, called actions (an agent's action, or an environment index). Latents are conditionally independent given the past: p(z^t | z^{<t}, a^{<t}) = Π_i p(z_i^t | z^{<t}, a^{<t}), each in an exponential family with natural parameter λ_i(G^z_i ⊙ z^{<t}, G^a_i ⊙ a^{<t}). The binary masks G = [G^z G^a] are the causal graph: which past latents and which actions are parents of each latent.
- **Equivalences.** Linear equivalence: the sufficient statistics of the two representations agree up to an invertible affine map L (and the λ's correspondingly). Permutation equivalence: L is block-permutation, P y = [y_{π(1)} L_1 … y_{π(d_z)} L_{d_z}]. Disentanglement is defined as permutation equivalence. With k = 1 (Gaussian, fixed variance) each recovered coordinate is a **scaled and shifted** true coordinate; the scale may be negative.
- **Theorem 4 (linear identifiability, extending Khemakhem et al. 2020).** Minimal sufficient statistics plus sufficient variability (the vectors λ(z^{(p)}, a^{(p)}) − λ(z^{(0)}, a^{(0)}) span R^{k d_z}) give identifiability up to L.
- **Theorem 5 (disentanglement via mechanism sparsity).** Add: k = 1 with T a diffeomorphism; sufficient time-variability (Jacobians of λ in z span the space of matrices with the support of G^z); sufficient action-variability (for each action ℓ, partial differences Δ_ℓ λ span the subspace of R^{d_z} on the children of ℓ). Then the learned graph contains a permuted copy of the true one: P G^z Pᵀ ⊂ Ĝ^z and P G^a ⊂ Ĝ^a. With **sparsity** ‖Ĝ‖₀ ≤ ‖G‖₀, the graphs are equal up to permutation. With a **graphical criterion** (for each latent p there are sets of parents, children and action-children whose intersection is exactly {p}), the model is disentangled. Self-loops plus no 2-cycles suffice (Proposition 6); actions alone can suffice without time (Fig. 2: three actions each touching two of three latents).
- **Actions as unknown-target interventions (Sec. 2.5).** With one-hot A^{t−1} ∈ {0, e_1, …}, G^a says which latents the ℓ-th intervention targets; sparse G^a is exactly the sparse mechanism shift hypothesis of Schölkopf et al. (2021).
- **Estimation.** A VAE with encoder q(z^t | x^t), learned masked transition MLPs, and an ELBO penalised by α_z‖Ĝ^z‖₀ + α_a‖Ĝ^a‖₀ via Gumbel-sigmoid masks. Too weak or too strong regularisation gives graphs too dense or too sparse; α is chosen by a filtered unsupervised disentanglement ranking.
- **Results (synthetic only).** d_z = 10, d_x = 20, random invertible MLP mixing, 10⁶ examples, near-deterministic Gaussian transitions (variance 10⁻⁴), T = 2 for time-sparsity. With the right regularisation MCC and graph recovery (normalised SHD) improve markedly over TCVAE, PCL, SlowVAE and iVAE, which reach high R² (linear identifiability) but low MCC. When assumptions are violated, regularisation still helps but less.
- **Stated limits.** f must be invertible; occlusion (a ball hidden behind a tree) breaks it (A.5). The transition model in experiments looks at a fixed window of lags; recurrent λ is future work. Combining identifiability results is open.

## The mathematical object
**A latent dynamical model whose transition mechanisms are masked by a sparse bipartite graph from the agent's own actions (and from past latents); the mask is identifiable, and with it the latents, up to permutation and per-coordinate scale and shift.**

## What it means for the design
1. **"What I caused" has an identifiable object: Ĝ^a.** The set of latents whose mechanism has the agent's own efference as a parent is recovered from data, not declared. A partner who answers the agent's acts shows up as edges from the agent's actions to latents that render the partner. Contingency is then a learned edge plus its mechanism, kept as content.
2. **Sign and scale of each latent are gauge.** Permutation equivalence leaves each coordinate's scale (including its sign) free. A coupling's sign means something only relative to latents whose sign is anchored elsewhere (for the agent's own body: efference and interoception). "Sign-flippable coupling" is therefore not a property data can fix by itself.
3. **Identification needs the agent to vary.** Sufficient action-variability requires the agent's own acts to move each of their children in enough independent directions. A still, reserved agent cannot identify what it causes. An agent that needs identification acquires a reason to vary its face at the partner.
4. **It identifies, it does not bound.** Nothing in the theorem limits how strongly a latent standing for the partner may drive the agent's expression latents. Ĝ^z can contain partner → self edges; sparsity prefers few edges, not weak ones.
5. **Asymptotic and stationary.** The graph is a property of the distribution over long data. A partner whose contingency switches within seconds (an abrupt stop and restart) is not a different graph on that time scale.

## Verdict
- **adopt (identifiability result as a design constraint), reject as a core.** It states exactly when self-caused structure can be separated from the rest: sparse, sufficiently varied own actions; injective rendering; sparsity regularisation. It is nonlinear ICA with action auxiliaries.
