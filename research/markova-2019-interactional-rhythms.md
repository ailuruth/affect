# Neurobehavioral interpersonal synchrony in early development: the role of interactional rhythms (Markova, Nguyen & Hoehl)

Citation: G. Markova, T. Nguyen, S. Hoehl, "Neurobehavioral Interpersonal Synchrony in Early Development: The Role of Interactional Rhythms", Frontiers in Psychology 10:2078 (2019). doi:10.3389/fpsyg.2019.02078.

Source read: Frontiers PDF, text-extracted, read in full. It is a **perspective article**, not new data; every empirical claim below is the authors' summary of other work, which was not read.

## Claims
- Distinguishes **concurrent synchrony** (joint action, mutual gaze, mirroring) from **sequential synchrony** (turn-taking, reciprocity, imitation), after Feldman 2007, and **dyadic** synchrony (achieved within the dyad through speech or affect) from **triadic** synchrony (induced by an external rhythm such as music or a toy) (Fig. 1).
- Early interactions are "mutually regulated systems" of alternating engagement and disengagement (Brazelton, Field, Tronick, Trevarthen, as cited). At 2 months infants anticipate the regularities of a pick-up action (Reddy et al. 2013, as cited).
- Proposal: entrainment to rhythms the caregiver supplies (affective touch, infant-directed singing) underlies interpersonal synchrony; rhythmic input lets sensory sampling align with high-excitability phases (Calderone et al. 2014, as cited).
- Reported adult–infant dual-EEG (Leong et al. 2017, as cited): direct gaze while singing increased mutual influence in the infant alpha band (6–9 Hz), measured by partial directed coherence. Infant and adult rhythms differ in frequency (infant alpha 5–9 Hz vs adult 8–12 Hz), so cross-frequency coupling may be needed.
- Caveat stated by the authors: neural synchrony may reflect shared input rather than mutual coordination; only one study (Novembre et al. 2017, tACS) established causality, in adults.

## What it means for the design
1. **Concurrent and sequential synchrony are different objects.** "You responded to me" is sequential; mirroring is concurrent. A core must not merge them into one coupling.
2. **Rhythm as a carrier, not a copy.** The caregiver supplies a rhythm the infant entrains to without matching its content; entrainment is a phase relation at the rhythm's time scale. This is the kind of partner influence that changes *when* rather than *what*, and it argues that the agent's time scales should be able to lock to a partner rhythm without its expressive content following.
3. Weak evidence; no numbers that a design can be fitted to.
