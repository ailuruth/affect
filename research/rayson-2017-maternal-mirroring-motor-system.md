## Key claims
- **Question:** is there a facial action-perception (seen ↔ produced) mechanism in 9-month-olds, and does maternal mirroring at 2 months build it? Faces are "opaque" (one cannot see one's own face), so self-observation cannot build the map; "maternal imitation … with caregivers acting as 'biological mirrors'" could.
- **EEG at 9 months** (final N = 19 of 34 tested; 128-channel; mu 6-9 Hz ERD over central vs occipital clusters):
  - execution of own facial expressions: central mu ERD (left M = −14.40 %, right −19.91 %), central > occipital (F(1,16) = 16.96, ηp² = .52);
  - observation: central ERD to mouth opening (left −10.63 %, right −11.82 %), to the source's "happy" stimulus (right only, −16.25 %) and "sad" stimulus (both sides, about −11 %); none to scrambled faces; none occipitally.
- **Mirroring at 2 months** (3-min home face-to-face, coded blind; mirroring = exact match of the infant's behaviour or its main features with minor modification):
  - mothers mirrored 14.86 % of all infant facial expressions; 73.35 % of maternal responses to infant faces were mirroring;
  - per expression: smiles 55.49 %, mouth opening 27.58 %, negative expressions 6.29 % (only two mothers ever mirrored one);
  - distributions were bimodal, so mothers were split high/low per expression (mouth opening 7 high / 8 low; smiles 6 / 10).
- **Result (expression-specific):** high mirroring of an expression at 2 months → stronger central ERD when observing **that** expression at 9 months: right hemisphere smile stimulus t(51.56) = −2.64, p = .01; mouth opening right p = .053, left t(50.72) = −2.20, p = .03 (three-way condition × hemisphere × group F(1,31.10) = 7.31, p = .01).
- **Controls:** infant base rates of producing the expression (mouth opening 2.47/min, smiles 1.82/min) and mother base rates of producing it (2.61/min, 4.11/min) did not predict ERD (p > .15); overall mirroring rate and overall prominence of mirroring did not either (p > .38). So the predictor is the **contingent correspondence** for that expression, not motor practice, visual exposure or general responsiveness.
- **Limits stated:** correlational; small sample; few execution trials (conditions pooled for execution).

## What it means for the engine
1. **A non-approval learning signal, per expression.** What the mother's mirroring trains is a perception-side correspondence (the agent's own motor code ↔ the seen partner face) for exactly the channels she mirrors. Nothing in it rewards the infant for producing anything: it is co-occurrence of own act and seen partner act. In pass 7's lattice it is a past-term (representation) objective.
2. **The partner selects which channels get a strong seen↔done map.** Mothers mirror one channel about nine times as often as another (smiles 55 % vs negative 6 %). A partner can therefore make the agent's own-body reading of some channels sharp and of others weak. In the engine that is a perception route; its effect on expression is bounded by the per-channel ceilings (D93), but its effect on what the agent knows about the partner (D81) is not.
3. **A second training source for S4.** The build core's inverse body model is learned by self-reproduction (S4). Being mirrored is another source of (own channel, seen face) pairs, usable only for pairs where the agent's own logged variation came first (the instrument of contingency-causal-synthesis.md), so that the agent following the partner is not mistaken for the partner mirroring the agent.
4. **Vocabulary.** The stimulus names are the source's labels for ADFES clips; in the engine the channels are the cheek raiser/lip-corner puller, jaw drop and brow channels.

## Verdict
- **adopt (evidence for a learning signal; small N, correlational).** It is the first direct evidence that contingent partner mirroring builds an expression-specific seen↔done mapping in human infants, with motor-practice and visual-exposure controls. For the engine: a perception-side, past-term training signal for the own-body reading of faces, never an expression reward.
