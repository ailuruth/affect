# Lerner & Keltner 2001: Fear, anger, and risk

Citation: J. S. Lerner, D. Keltner. "Fear, anger, and risk." *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology* 81(1): 146–159, 2001. doi:10.1037/0022-3514.81.1.146.

Source read: the full PDF from the second author's Berkeley page. The study designs and the main numbers of Studies 1, 2 and 4 were read closely. Study 3 was read for its design. The registered-report replication (Lu, Efendic & Feldman, JPSP) was **not read**; a search snippet says it supported the appraisal-tendency account for general risk optimism, but not for risk preference or for optimism about negative events.

## Claims
- The **appraisal-tendency framework**: emotions of the same valence differ in their appraisal themes, and those themes carry over to unrelated judgements. "Whereas a sense of situational control and uncertainty defines fear, a sense of individual control and certainty defines anger" (after Smith & Ellsworth 1985).
- Fear and anger push risk perception in opposite directions. Angry people resemble happy people more than fearful ones. "Appraisals of certainty and control moderated and (in the case of control) mediated the emotion effects."

## Evidence
- Study 1: N = 75, dispositional fear and anger, Asian-disease choice task. Fear and anger dispositions correlate r = .49.
  - More fear went with more choice of the sure option, B = −0.19, t(72) = −1.70, p < .05 one-tailed.
  - More anger went with more choice of the gamble, B = 0.24, t(72) = 2.11, p < .05 one-tailed.
  - Both held regardless of frame.
- Study 2: N = 601 undergraduates; optimism about 26 future life events. Fear went with pessimism and anger with optimism, after each was controlled for the other.
- Study 3 (design only): selected anger-prone (n = 43), fear-prone (n = 41) and happy-prone (n = 34) groups. It tested whether events that are ambiguous about controllability show larger emotion effects.
- **Study 4:** N = 63 (59 df in the analyses), fear or anger induced by recall. Means are standardized and adjusted for baseline affect.
  - Anger against fear on individual-control appraisal: M = 0.68 against −0.51, F(1, 59) = 28.37, p < .01.
  - Certainty: 0.16 against −0.40, F(1, 59) = 4.33, p < .05.
  - Optimism: 0.28 against −0.25, F(1, 59) = 4.91, p < .05.
  - Mediation path: emotion → control β = .59, control → optimism β = .27. The direct emotion → optimism path fell from β = .28 to .19 (n.s.).
  - Certainty did not meet the mediation criteria (α of the certainty scale = .63). Certainty and control correlated only r = .12.

## Formal model
None. The verbal model is emotion → appraisal tendency (certainty, control) → judgement and choice. The paper notes that Study 1 measured preference (u(x)), and Studies 2–4 measured probability (p(x)).

## Verdict: adopt (as evidence that felt control is a carried-over state, not a per-event appraisal), with the replication caveat
- It shows that the anger-like state carries a sense of *individual control* that biases later, unrelated predictions toward optimism.
- Control, not certainty, carried that bias.
- So "control" behaves like a persistent prior. It does not behave like a fresh computation.

## What it implies for the single core
- **Coping potential as a slowly updated prior.** If controllability is a latent belief about how much one's own policy moves outcomes (Huys & Dayan's notion; see the controllability notes), then:
  - it should persist after the eliciting event;
  - it should bias forecasts in *other* contexts.

  That is the "appraisal tendency": the control prior generalises. The same generalisation produces learned helplessness when the prior is low.
- **Anger and fear are then regions that differ in the control prior and in uncertainty, not labels.** High controllability with low uncertainty after a partner-caused loss is the approach/protest region. Low controllability with high uncertainty about the threat is the withdrawal and vigilance region.
- **Ablation.** Reset the controllability belief at each new context, so it cannot carry over. Prediction: the carry-over bias vanishes, meaning a state induced in one exchange no longer shifts the agent's forecasts and its willingness to take an uncertain action in the next. The within-episode response stays. This tests element 2 of emotion-elements-synthesis.md (central persistent state) for the control dimension specifically.
- **Caution.** The replication's mixed results mean the size of the carry-over is uncertain in humans. The engine should predict a direction, not a magnitude.
