# Pleasure systems in the brain

Kent C. Berridge and Morten L. Kringelbach, *Neuron* 86(3):646–664, 2015, doi:10.1016/j.neuron.2015.02.018 (PMC4425246, NIH author manuscript). It complements Berridge 2004 (berridge-2004-motivation-concepts.md), which covers the critique of drive reduction. This note covers the circuit claims.

## What was read
The PMC main text (the first 100k of 231k characters), read through a fetch tool that returned summaries and the quotes I asked for. It covered the abstract through the conclusion. The rest is mostly references. I did not read it line by line, and figures were seen only as captions.

## Claims
- **Reward has three components:**
  - **liking**: "core reactions to hedonic impact";
  - **wanting**: the "motivation process of incentive salience";
  - **learning**: Pavlovian and instrumental associations and cognitive representations.
  They "can occur together at any time". Wanting dominates the appetitive phase and liking the consummatory phase.
- **Liking is generated by small hedonic hotspots.** These are in the NAc medial shell, ventral pallidum and parabrachial nucleus, with OFC and insula hotspots "emerging". Each is "about 1 cubic millimeter" in rats; the NAc hotspot is about 10% of NAc volume.
  - Mu, delta and kappa opioid agonists and anandamide in the NAc hotspot "can at least double the hedonic impact of sucrose"; VP stimulation "more than double[s]" liking reactions.
  - A posterior **coldspot** cuts liking "to just half normal levels".
  - Dopamine and AMPA blockade in the hotspot do not enhance liking.
- **Dopamine is wanting, not liking.**
  - Dopamine depletion to "approximately 1% normal levels" leaves sucrose liking "completely intact and unimpaired".
  - Hyperdopaminergic mice want more without liking more.
  - L-DOPA does not raise rated pleasure in people.
- **Affective keyboard.** The NAc medial shell is arranged rostrocaudally and "can generate intense desire or even dread". Anterior sites drive appetitive behaviour and posterior sites fearful behaviour, and the same site can release both. A stressful environment "tilted the causal keyboard toward dread"; a home-like one expands the desire zone.
- **Taste reactivity** is homologous across newborn humans, apes, monkeys, rats and mice. It is "not mere brainstem reflexes", because forebrain structures control it.
- **Subcortical competence.** Subcortical structures "may be surprisingly competent to generate many normal hedonic reactions". Evidence: lobotomy, a patient with massive cortical damage, and hydranencephalic children who respond to caregivers and music. The midOFC "appears to best code the subjective experience of pleasure".
- **State dependence.** Liking is modulated by appetite and satiety. OFC tracks sensory-specific satiety. (The word "alliesthesia" did not appear in the part read.)

## Evidence
Mostly rat microinjection and optogenetics with taste-reactivity measures. Human evidence comes from Parkinson's patients, L-DOPA, neuroimaging of OFC and lesion cases. The numbers are the ones above.

## Mechanistic commitments
1. **Two separable signals about the same outcome:**
   - a hedonic evaluation, made when the outcome arrives;
   - an incentive-salience gain on cues, which drives approach.
   They have different substrates and can be pushed in opposite directions.
2. **Incentive salience is computed fresh from current physiological state and cue value.** It does not have to be learned again (see zhang-berridge-2009-incentive-salience-model.md).
3. **Valence is context-gated.** The same circuit can produce approach or defense depending on the environment. Valence is not hard-wired to location.
4. **Liking is narrowly localized**; wanting is broad.

## What it implies for a single formal core
A core whose only affect signal is a single scalar value gradient cannot produce wanting without liking (already flagged in berridge-2004-motivation-concepts.md). In terms of a single formal core:
- **liking** is the realized change in the objective when an outcome is consumed, the drive-reduction term at the moment of reward;
- **wanting** is a cue-attached, need-scaled gain on approach: a *multiplicative* interaction between the current need and the learned cue value, $\kappa(b)\,\tilde V(c)$.

The two are both functions of the same core quantities but enter in different places: liking at consumption, wanting at the cue. So they can dissociate under lesion without a second objective. That is the derivation target.

The keyboard finding (context flips valence at the same site) suggests that the sign of a learned cue response should depend on the agent's background state. In the brief's setting: the same partner face should invite approach in a safe context and wariness after a stressful episode.

## What an ablation could separate
- **Wanting lesion.** Set the need gain on cue value to zero ($\kappa \equiv$ const). Predictions:
  - the consummatory hedonic readout is unchanged, so the readout at contact (on reunion) is intact;
  - approach toward cues for the partner's arrival drops, so bids and orienting are reduced.
  This is the engine's dopamine-depletion analogue.
- **Liking lesion.** Remove the realized-reduction term from the readout but keep the gain on cues. The agent should still approach cues but show no positive readout on reaching them: wanting without liking. This is the sensitization analogue, and a flattery risk if the cue is the user's attention.
- **Context gating.** Run the same cue after a stressful versus a safe background. The response should flip between approach and avoidance if valence is context-gated.

## Verdict: adopt (wanting/liking as a required dissociation; numbers for lesion analogues)
Together with Berridge 2004, this is the strongest case that one scalar affect signal is not enough. The engine must show the dissociation by ablation.
