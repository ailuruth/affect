## The computation, precisely

**There is no computation in this paper, and that has to be said first.** It is a definitional framework from an NIH Blueprint workshop. Its substantive contributions for this project are a vocabulary and one structural claim.

**The structural claim: interoception is a closed loop, and the descending half is part of it.**

> "whereas the terms 'sensing' and 'integrating' seem to imply a one-way communication to the brain from other organs, the links between brain and body are often bidirectional and also include communications from the brain to the other organs and in turn, modulation of the internal body signals sent back to the brain. Therefore, a more comprehensive definition of interoception should encompass the complex interplay between the brain and other organs necessary to monitor and regulate internal states."
> "The concept of regulating interoception via descending pathways has not been incorporated into most definitions of interoception ... We will refer to these signals generated by the CNS, often in response to interoceptive input or cognitive and exteroceptive factors, to regulate the interoceptive processes, as 'regulating signals of interoception'."
> "The primary function of the regulating signals of interoception is to regulate the generation and transmission of interoceptive signals of the targeted internal organs ... and thus complete the circle of interoceptive processes."

**The boundary redefinition, which is the one idea with design content:**

> "As the anatomical boundaries between interoception and some forms of exteroception become blurred, such as in the case of proprioception and somatosensation, a complementary approach would be to assess whether the signals and the body's responses **represent, rather than originate from,** the internal or external world."

**The taxonomy.** Interoceptive signals are of three types — "biochemical signals that range from inorganics, such as acidic ions, to organic molecules and small peptides"; "mechanical forces that alter structures, such as cellular shape, through stretch or tissue extension"; "thermal and electromagnetic signals". *Interoceptors* are the molecular sensors; *central interpreters and integrators* are the CNS neurons; *central regulators* generate descending signals. Two ascending routes: vagal/cranial ganglia → nucleus tractus solitarii, and dorsal root ganglia → spinal cord, with "vagal afferents appear to carry primarily mechanoreceptor and chemosensory signals; spinal afferents carry signals related to temperature, pain, and tissue injury", and a hypothesis that the two "may represent opposing parasympathetic and sympathetic signals, and may thus interact within the brain's interoceptive regions to inhibit each other". Non-neural routes (circulatory, lymphatic, the HPA/HPG/HPT axes) are given equal standing with neural ones.

**On affect, the paper is explicitly ignorant and says so:**

> "it is mostly unknown how affect — a brain state that can be described as a low-dimensional representation of high-dimensional behavioral state in the body — is influenced by interoception."

**On modelling, it reports a gap rather than a method:** "the sensory or perceptual aspect of interoception and the body regulation aspect are often modeled as separate processes. It may be desirable to develop computational models that could capture and describe the proposed framework."

## Evidence with numbers
**None.** This paper contains no quantitative results of its own; it is a framework and gap analysis. The only concrete mechanistic example given with a manipulation is the voluntary-urination circuit: "Stimulation of CRF-labeled neurons in the PMC caused immediate urination in mice, suggesting that the PMC neurons are sufficient to drive behavior. When neural activity was blocked in CRF-labeled neurons in the PMC, voluntary urination was inhibited" — cited, not measured here. It also notes that human interoception measurement "remains mostly correlational" and lists heartbeat tasks, skin conductance, self-report and microneurography.

## Is this a new computation relative to the excluded frameworks?

Parts: (i) ascending sensing; (ii) central integration; (iii) descending regulation that alters the signals subsequently sensed.

(i)–(iii) together **are** the perception-action loop of active inference and the plant-plus-controller loop of optimal control — both excluded, and both already covered in this repository (`barrett-simmons-2015-epic-interoceptive-predictions.md`, `seth-2013-interoceptive-inference.md`, `ramsay-woods-2014-homeostasis-allostasis.md`). The paper offers no update rule, no equation, no invariant.

**Verdict on novelty: no. Nothing here is a computation at all, let alone a new one.** This is worth recording precisely because the operator's candidate list included interoceptive circuits: the honest finding is that the most-cited recent framework review on interoception contains no mechanism that could serve as a new primitive, and it concedes that the affect–interoception link is "mostly unknown".

Two framings are still worth keeping:

- **"Represent, rather than originate from"** is a usable criterion for the core: a channel is interoceptive if its values represent the agent's own state, whatever its physical source. For a software agent with no viscera this is the only workable definition, and it legitimises treating computational quantities (latency, uncertainty, resource pressure, conversational self-monitoring) as genuine interoceptive signals rather than metaphors.
- **The descending half is constitutive.** A core that only *reads* its own state is incomplete by this definition; it must also emit signals that change what it will later sense. That is a weak constraint but a real one, and it rules out designs where body variables are inputs only.

## What it suggests for an affective core where the partner is structured, not a gain

1. **A definitional licence, not a mechanism.** By the "represent not originate" criterion, the Bot's interoceptive channels are whatever quantities represent its own state. This matters for the brief's Principle 1–2 (nothing hand-named): the channels can be chosen on structural grounds (they are the things the agent's own operation produces) rather than by borrowing a list of human viscera.
2. **Closed loop as a design requirement.** Each body variable must have both an afferent read and an efferent action that changes the variable's future values. A variable that only reads is a gauge — and the operator already stood down two designs for being "a few body gauges" (`foundations/designs.md`, D68). This paper's framework is the clearest statement of why that objection is right: without the descending half there is no interoception, only instrumentation.
3. **Two ascending routes with mutually inhibitory content** (vagal mechano/chemical versus spinal thermal/nociceptive, "may thus interact ... to inhibit each other") is a hypothesis, not a result, but it suggests a structure worth considering: the agent's own state arrives on two channels of opposite valence that compete, so that valence is a *competition outcome* rather than a scalar read-out — which would fit this project's requirement that valence be read out, not set (`barrett-constructed-emotion.md`, `hesp-2021-deeply-felt-affect.md`).
4. **Nothing about the partner.** The paper has no dyadic content at all: no social interoception, no coupling, no other agent. On the question this reading cycle was commissioned to answer — how a partner's influence can be structured rather than a gain — it is silent.

## Verdict
**watch (vocabulary and the closed-loop requirement only); reject as a source of a new computation.** The framework is ascending sensing + central integration + descending regulation, which is active inference / optimal control by another name, and the paper itself reports that the interoception–affect link is unknown and that models of the sensing and regulating halves are not yet unified.
