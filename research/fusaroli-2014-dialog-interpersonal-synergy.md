# Fusaroli, Rączaszek-Leonardi & Tylén 2014, "Dialog as interpersonal synergy" (New Ideas in Psychology 32:147-157, doi 10.1016/j.newideapsych.2013.03.005)

Source read, 2026-11-25: the full article as "in press" (2013 pagination 1–11), from the Elsevier PDF in the Aarhus University repository (pure.au.dk/ws/files/53683269). All sections, Tables 1-2 and the footnotes were read. The reference list was not read. This is a theory paper. Its evidence is a review of other studies, mainly Fusaroli et al. 2012, Mills & Gregoromichelaki 2010, Mills 2011, Goodwin 2011 and Dressler et al. 2009. None of these was read here.

## Claims
- **Interactive alignment rests on synchronization.** "two independent components continuously influence each other toward greater entrainment within a certain lag tolerance."
- **Taken to its extreme, alignment predicts the wrong thing.** "Brought to its extremes, the assumptions and mechanisms of interactive alignment would predict good dialogical coordination to progress toward obsessive mimicry … That does not match any systematic observation of conversation."
- **Complementarity, defined.** "the way components doing quite different things come to form a coherent whole. A structure initiated by one component is completed (rather than copied) by the other."
- **Three markers of a synergy** (Bernstein; Riley et al. 2011):
  - functional specificity: the task selects which dimensions couple;
  - dimensional compression: the collective variance has lower dimension than the components;
  - reciprocal compensation: a perturbation of one part is absorbed by the others so that function is preserved.
- **Different levels have different dynamics.** "While on the lower physiological levels of dialog (posture, visual attention, speech rate, etc.) good coordination seems to depend mainly on synchronization and alignment, on 'higher' linguistic levels, we observe quite the opposite."
  - Two speculative reasons are given. Low-level alignment is "a fail-safe coordinative mechanism". Conforming low-level behaviour is "a stable background on which minimal differences at higher levels can become highly salient".
- **Information, not just correlation.** Pure alignment makes one partner's behaviour "redundant" with the other's. In a complementary structure, "information on the overall, systemic level is irreducible to the individual contributions". The authors note that no study had quantified this yet.
- **Soft assembly.** Individuals keep their autonomy. They "can flexibly engage and disengage from it, as well as become part of other soft-assemblies".

## Evidence (all secondary, as reported here)
- **Fusaroli, Bahrami et al. 2012**, on the same oddball corpus as Fusaroli & Tylén 2016:
  - Indiscriminate lexical alignment (the probability that any word was used by the partner on the previous joint trial) was present in all dyads and was negatively related to collective benefit.
  - Selective alignment (the same measure for confidence expressions only) was positively related.
  - Convergence on a dominant set of confidence expressions was "an even stronger predictor".
  - Selective alignment decreased once the shared scale stabilised.
  - No coefficients are given in this paper.
- **Turn-taking.** "over 50% of the gaps are below the usual threshold for reactions: 300 ms", yet simultaneous starts are rare (Jefferson 1988).
- **Maze game** (Mills & Gregoromichelaki 2010): over 12 games, exchanges shrank "from more than 150 turns" to brief elliptical routines.
- **Mills 2011.** Pairing participants across "communities" disrupted interaction: more self-editing, more explicit acknowledgment, lower accuracy.
- **Motor compensation.** Remote parts of a synergy react "20–30ms after perturbation" in task-specific ways (Kelso et al. 1984, on speech articulators).
- **Aphasia.** Goodwin's patient Chil, with three words, sustains conversation through partners' completions. Dressler et al. 2009 report more fluent prosodic rhythm with familiar than with unfamiliar partners (no numbers given).

## Formal models
- No equations.
- The one mechanistic model cited is Wilson & Wilson 2005 on turn-taking. Each speaker has an oscillator at the shared syllable rate. The oscillators are "entrained in counter-phase", giving "a common rhythm … and complementarity: readiness to take the floor must be opposite at any given moment for speaker and hearer".
- Dimensional compression is to be measured as reduced dimensionality of the collective variance (via Riley et al. 2011 and Ramenzoni et al.). Recurrence and information theory are named as the planned tools.

## What it implies for a dyadic affective core
- **The counter-phase oscillator shows why one gain fails.** One coupling can be in-phase (copy) or anti-phase (complement), and the sign or phase of the coupling is a separate parameter from its strength. A single positive gain that runs flat→copy has no anti-phase regime. Turn-taking, which is near-universal, needs that regime on the "floor-readiness" variable while rate is aligned in-phase on another variable. So even the minimal dyad has at least two couplings with different signs.
- **Different channels need different coupling.** The paper predicts alignment on low-level channels (posture, rate; plausibly tonic arousal and mimicry of facial affect) and complementarity on higher ones (who leads, who acknowledges, question and answer). For the Bot this means a per-channel or per-level coupling structure, and the brief's mimicry principle (3) applies most naturally at the low level.
- **Reciprocal compensation is a testable signature that a gain model lacks.** When the partner's channel is perturbed or degraded (for example the user goes silent but still smiles), a synergy re-routes through other channels to preserve function. A gain model simply passes on less.
- **Function selects what couples.** Coupling should be conditioned on the current joint activity (comforting, joking, deciding), which is a profile change, not a gain change.
- **Not shown.**
  - No affect data.
  - No numbers of its own.
  - The low-vs-high-level split is asserted from a review, not measured.
  - The information-theoretic irreducibility claim is untested here. For the Bot it would map onto the synergistic-information caveat already noted under James et al. 2016.

## Verdict: watch (theory)
- It supplies the vocabulary used by the empirical paper (Fusaroli & Tylén 2016, adopted) and the anti-phase turn-taking example, which is the cleanest counterexample to a one-gain coupling.
- It has no data of its own and no model of its own, so it is not a mechanism source.
