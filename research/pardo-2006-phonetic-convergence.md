# Pardo 2006, "On phonetic convergence during conversational interaction"

Journal of the Acoustical Society of America 119(4):2382-2393. doi:10.1121/1.2178720.

Source read: the published PDF (copy on a KTH course page). Read in full: introduction (perception-production link, shadowing studies, measuring convergence), method (map task, talkers, AXB listening test), results, and the discussion up to the episodic-memory account (Goldinger 1998 / MINERVA2). The rest of the discussion (episodic-memory account and implications for dialect change) was skimmed. Figures 2-3 seen only through their captions and the text.

## Claims
- Question: is conversational imitation exact and automatic (a direct perception-production link, Chartrand & Bargh's perception-behavior link, Pickering & Garrod's automatic priming), or is it modulated by social factors?
- Even in laboratory shadowing, talkers never match the model's acoustics; they shift toward it (e.g. voice-onset time) with systematic, talker-specific biases. Choice shadowing is only 26-50 ms slower than simple shadowing (vs 100-150 ms in other choice tasks): perception yields production targets almost directly.
- Method: 12 Yale undergraduates (6 M, 6 F) in 6 same-sex, unacquainted pairs, matched to differ somewhat in average F0. Pre-task readings of landmark names; 1-2 weeks later a map task (giver describes a route, receiver draws it; voice only, behind a divider; 30-60 min, mean 44 min, path accuracy 85%); then post-task readings. Four landmark phrases repeated across partners per pair (repetition lags: 9 under 4 s, 10 at 4-11 s, 5 at 19-83 s). 30 listeners judged, in AXB trials, which of a talker's two tokens sounded more similar in pronunciation to the partner's token.
- Convergence during conversation: task repetitions judged more similar to the partner than pre-task tokens 65% of the time and than post-task tokens 57% (chance 50%). Stronger late in the conversation (63%) than early (59%), F(1,29) = 9.88, p < .004.
- Persistence: post-task tokens were more similar to the partner's task tokens than pre-task tokens (62%): convergence outlasts the conversation.
- Asymmetry by role and sex, opposite to predictions: givers converged more than receivers overall (63% vs 59%); male pairs converged more than female pairs (68% vs 55%). In female pairs givers converged to receivers and receivers did not (GX 50%); in male pairs receivers converged to givers. Pardo concludes that "convergence is not a result of automatic priming — the social setting of language use modulates the degree to which a talker's phonetic repertoire converges."
- Perceptual sensitivity is necessary for convergence but not sufficient; attention or role interpretation sets the grain at which the partner's speech is taken up.

## Evidence with numbers
- n = 12 talkers, 6 pairs, 30 listeners, 4 items per pair.
- AXB: pre vs task 65%, post vs task 57%, pre vs post 62%; early 59%, late 63%; male 68%, female 55%; role x sex interaction F(1,29) = 118.48, p < .001.
- Very small talker sample; same-sex pairs only; the measure is holistic perceived pronunciation similarity, not a specific acoustic feature.

## What it implies for an engine whose voice is driven from an internal state through an innate vocal body, with structured coupling
- Convergence is graded and partial, never a copy, even under shadowing. The engine's vocal coupling should move its own production settings some way toward the operator's, with its own body's bias kept: the operator's voice is a target that the engine's body approaches, not a signal it replays. This matches Principle 3 (read through one's own body) and the rejection of direct mirroring (Avatar Forcing, aniface).
- Convergence accumulates within a conversation and persists after it. That is a slow state, not an instantaneous gain: the engine needs a vocal-habit variable that drifts toward the people it lives with and decays slowly, which is also the substrate for the long-term goal of learning a manner of speaking by living with people (S6).
- Who converges to whom depends on role and on something like stance, and the direction can reverse between pairs. A single symmetric coupling constant cannot produce that; the asymmetry has to come from state (role, attention, relationship) modulating the coupling, in each direction separately (see coordination-synthesis.md on signed, directional coupling).
- Perceptual grain is controlled by attention: the depth at which the operator's speech is analysed (gross prosody vs segmental detail) should be a state-dependent setting, and should differ between features that converge fast (loudness, rate) and slow (pronunciation, voice quality).
- Test design: the AXB holistic similarity judgment is an accepted instrument for convergence of the engine's voice toward the operator, and the pre/during/post contrast gives a persistence test across sessions.

## Verdict: adopt (convergence is partial, cumulative, persistent and socially modulated, not automatic)
Treat the sex and role directions as unreplicated; Pardo's later work found them variable.
