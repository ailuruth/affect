# Likowski, Mühlberger, Seibt, Pauli & Weyers 2008, "Modulation of facial mimicry by attitudes" (Journal of Experimental Social Psychology 44(4):1065-1072, doi 10.1016/j.jesp.2007.10.007)

Source read: abstract only (from the CORE metadata record). The open copies on SSOAR, HAL and CORE all sat behind bot-check pages that I could not pass, and the browser extension was not connected. The design details below come from the co-authors' description in Seibt et al. 2015 (read in full), not from this paper. No numbers are given because I did not read them.

## Claims (abstract)
- Attitudes toward three characters (positive, neutral, negative), induced by written reports, changed facial reactions to their happy, neutral and sad faces.
- "Results revealed facial mimicry reactions to happy and sad faces of positive characters, but less and even incongruent facial muscular reactions to happy and sad faces of negative characters."
- Attitudes "formed in a few minutes, and only by reports and not by own experiences" moderated mimicry.

## Evidence (as described by Seibt et al. 2015)
- Women; computer-generated avatars introduced as game characters; trait adjectives (kind, nice, likeable vs reserved, serious vs malicious, aggressive, egoistic, deceitful) paired with hair colour; EMG zygomaticus and corrugator.
- Zygomaticus to happy faces stronger for positive than for neutral and negative characters; significant against baseline for positive and neutral, not negative.
- Corrugator to sad faces: congruent activation for positive characters, incongruent deactivation for negative characters (interpreted, tentatively, as schadenfreude).

## What it implies for the design
- A relation learned from a few sentences can flip the sign of the brow response to the same sad face. The partner model needs to carry a signed value, not a non-negative gain.
- Primary numbers unread: do not quote effect sizes from this paper until the full text is read.
