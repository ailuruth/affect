## Key claims
- **Mutual regulation model.** Infant and adult form "a dyadic, mutually regulated communicative system". Typical face-to-face interaction is "messy": it moves from matching to mismatched states and back via "an active, jointly carried out reparatory process". The "Fred-and-Ginger" synchrony model has "little support".
- **Numbers (as reported):** in Gianino & Tronick 1988, "mother–infant mismatches were repaired 70% of the time in the next interactive step, with new reparations occurring about every three to five seconds". Matches go with infant positive engagement, mismatches with negative affect and dysregulation (Tronick 1989).
- **What the infant learns from repair:** an implicit knowing that "we can repair mismatches", that its own dysregulated state "can be transformed into a positive state"; this is said to feed agency, trust and attachment. A history of failed repair gives "we can't repair mismatches".
- **The selection principle is blind and in the moment.** Open systems "select resources … to maximize complexity and coherence"; meaning-making "operates to maximize complexity by selecting what better fits together from what is available now", "blind to long-term consequences".
- **Culture-specific expression is shaped by the partner's responses.** Gusii mothers, asked to play face to face, "looked away from their infants just as their infants made eye-to-eye contact and smiled"; infants looked away and their smiles "vanished". Over time "infants come to shorten their looks and limit their affective expressions", assembled "via reiterated, complexity-governed selective processes of reparation".
- **Convergence on the partner's low state.** Infants of mothers with depressive symptoms show more sustained negative affect in interaction; "the infants may learn that 'we can be sad together'", a "way of being together" that becomes "stabilized" and "increasingly constricted and rigid in the long run". Field (1998, as reported): such infants show more negative patterns with a non-depressed adult "and may even induce a negative affect state in that adult".
- **Moods** are described as "memorial processes" carrying the past interaction history forward and biasing expectations; repair histories are relationship-specific ("unique 'psychic landscape'"), and mother and father dyads differ.
- **Still-face:** a prolonged mismatch; infants solicit, then show distress and withdrawal; reunion shows carry-over plus rebound (citing Mesman et al. 2009).

## What it means for the engine
1. **The infant's learning signal named here is its own state regulation, with the partner inside the loop.** The selector is the infant's own "coherence", not the caregiver's approval as such; but the caregiver's responses move that state, so in pass 7's terms (Proposition 1(b)) it is an own-state objective with a partner → own-state path: **open**.
2. **Human evidence that this open route shapes expression and can converge on the partner.** The Gusii case is partner-steered expression learning (the partner's withheld reciprocity trains shorter gaze and smaller displays). The depressed-mother case is convergence of the infant's held state on the partner's held state, stabilised by repetition and carried to a stranger. Under "every partner policy" (the conjecture's setting) this is exactly the copy-attractor the engine must exclude; humans do not exclude it. The protection infants have is the ordinary caregiver's policy (repairable, mostly positive matches), not a property of their own objective.
3. **Repair as the unit.** Mismatch → repair cycles every 3-5 s with 70 % next-step repair (as reported) is a usable temporal prior for the battery and for the "you responded to me" prediction error of pass 7, Direction 1: the still-face is a long run of unrepaired mismatch, i.e. a persistent own-state prediction error at the repair horizon.
4. **Relationship-specific slow state.** "Repair history with a specific person" as the carrier of trust is a learned per-partner slow statistic; the build core's relationship code is the place, and it must be learned from the agent's own regulation outcomes, not set.
5. Vocabulary: the paper names infant states with emotion words; nothing of that enters the engine. "Sad together" is quoted as the authors' phrase for a held low state shared by the dyad.

## Verdict
- **background (strong; cautionary).** A theory paper with reported numbers, no new data. It supplies the clearest statement that infants learn expression through an own-state signal the partner can move, with documented human outcomes of that route (culture-specific display shaping; convergence on a depressed partner). It argues for, not against, the per-expression ceilings (D93) and for typing what the engine learns from. Not a mechanism.
