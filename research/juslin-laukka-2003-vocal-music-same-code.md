## Claims (abstract)
- Review of 104 studies of vocal expression and 41 studies of music performance.
- The two channels are similar in (a) how accurately discrete emotions are communicated to listeners and (b) the emotion-specific patterns of acoustic cues used for each emotion.
- The patterns are "generally consistent with K. R. Scherer's (1986) theoretical predictions".
- This can explain why music is heard as expressive of emotion, and fits an evolutionary account of vocal expression.

## What it implies for an engine whose voice is driven from an internal state through an innate vocal body
- If speech prosody and instrumental performance share a cue code (tempo, loudness, pitch level and variability, spectral balance, articulation and timing microstructure), then that code is a property of a sound-producing body under a state, not of language. This supports driving the engine's voice from body settings (rate, intensity, pitch level and spread, timbre, onset sharpness) that are meaningful even without words, and it suggests the same settings could drive non-speech vocalizations.
- The agreement with Scherer 1986 is second-hand support for the appraisal-to-physiology route used in scherer-2003-vocal-communication-review.md and banse-scherer-1996-acoustic-profiles.md.
- The review is framed around discrete emotion categories. Only the cue dimensions transfer to the engine, never the categories (Principle 1).
