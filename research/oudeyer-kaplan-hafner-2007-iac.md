## Claims
- Error-maximizing curiosity ("Group 1") fails on noise: "a robot using this method will, for example, be stuck by white noise or, more generally, by situations which are inherently too complex for its learning machinery or situations for which the causal variables are not perceivable or observable by the robot."
- Rewarding the decrease of error ("Group 2") avoids that: "This ensures that the robot will not stay in front of white noise for a long time or in unlearnable situations because this does not lead to a decrease of its errors in prediction." But comparing error over time across different activities "will provide the robot with internal rewards when shifting from an activity with a high mean error rate to activities with a lower mean error rate", which "will push the robot towards instable behavior". Hence learning progress must be measured "in situations which are of the same kind", i.e. per region.
- IAC is framed as a drive, explicitly by analogy with homeostatic drives but not as one: "It is a motivation, or drive, in the same sense that food level maintenance or heat maintenance are drives, but instead of being about the maintenance of a physical variable, the IAC drive is about the maintenance of an abstract dynamic cognitive variable: the learning progress, which must be kept maximal."
- What it avoids, in the authors' words: "It is adaptive because the situations that are attractive change over time, indeed, once something is learned, it will not provide learning progress anymore." "It is called intelligent because it keeps, as a side effect, the robot away both from situations which are too predictable and from situations which are too unpredictable (i.e., the edge of order and chaos in the cognitive dynamics)."

## Evidence
Definitions (p. 270-271, read from the page image):
- Regions: start with one region R1; criterion C1, "when the number of exemplars associated with the region is above a threshold T = 250, then split." Criterion C2 picks a cut dimension j and value v_j on SM(t) so that "|Γn+1|·σ({S(t+1)|...∈Γn+1}) + |Γn+2|·σ({S(t+1)|...∈Γn+2}) is minimal", with σ(S) the mean squared deviation of the vectors in S from their mean. One expert per region; children inherit the parent's error list.
- Error: "e_n(t+1) = ||S(t+1) − S̃(t+1)||²", appended to the region's own list e_n(t), e_n(t−1), ..., where t "is specific to the expert, and not to the robot".
- Windowed comparison: "⟨e_n(t+1)⟩ = Σ_{i=0}^{θ} e_n(t+1−i) / (θ+1)" and "⟨e_n(t+1−τ)⟩ = Σ_{i=0}^{θ} e_n(t+1−τ−i) / (θ+1)", "where τ is a time window parameter typically equal to 15, and θ a smoothing parameter typically equal to 25." "D(t+1) = ⟨e_n(t+1)⟩ − ⟨e_n(t+1−τ)⟩" (1), and "L(t+1) = −D(t+1)" (2). Reward "r(t) = L(t)", which is "an internal reward (positive or negative)".
- Action selection is simplified to one-step greedy on the region's most recent L (eq. 3), with ε-greedy random actions; "In the following experiments ε is typically 0.35." (§VI.E later says "the 0.15 probability to do a random action"; the paper is inconsistent here.)
- Extrinsic rewards can be mixed as "r(t) = Σ_i α_i · r_i(t)" (footnote 5).

Experiment 1 (Webots simulation, §VI): a toy "moves randomly if the sound emitted by the robot has a frequency belonging to zone f1 = [0; 0.33]", "stops moving if the sound is in zone f2 = [0.34; 0.66]", "jumps into the robot if the sound is in zone f3 = [0.67; 1]". So f1 is unlearnable, f3 easy, f2 learnable but complex. Over 5000 steps: uniform at first, then "After the first 250 first steps, the robot concentrates on emitting sounds within f3", then on f2; "the robot consistently avoids the situations in which nothing can be learned, begins by easy situations, and then shifts autonomously to a more complex situation." Over 100 runs, the f2 test-error plateau was reached "at t = 4583 in average for IAC, with a standard deviation of 452, and at t = 11980 ... with a standard deviation of 561 for RANDOM"; final error printed as "e = 0.89" (IAC, SD 0.009) vs "e = 0.96" (RANDOM, SD 0.004). The same section earlier gives "around 0.09" and "0.096", so the printed 0.89/0.96 look like typos. "IAC allows the robot to learn the interesting part of the mapping about 2.6 times faster". MAX (error-maximizing) "spends times in uninteresting situations".

Experiment 2 (Playground, AIBO on a baby play mat, §VII): self-organized stages (looking, then biting/bashing, non-affordant before affordant), 200 runs on a learned model of the setup; the regularities "are only statistical". Not read in enough detail to report numbers.

No animal or human data. §II cites Berlyne (intermediate novelty most rewarding), Csikszentmihalyi (flow) and dopamine novelty studies as motivation only.

Yes (our judgment). The authors' own note: nearest-neighbour experts with T = 250 per leaf, "about 17 scalar comparisons ... and 250 distance computation" per step at 3 000 000 exemplars, "a few milliseconds on a personal computer".

## Verdict: adopt (the windowed, per-region learning-progress definition), watch (the "kept maximal" framing)
- This is the reference definition for D62: LP = −(mean error over the last θ+1 samples − the same mean τ samples earlier), per region. It answers both traps at once: an unlearnable region has flat error (LP ≈ 0), and so does a learned one.
- The per-region requirement is load-bearing. A global LP rewards switching from a hard channel to an easy one. D62's "how fast prediction error is falling on what it attends to" must be computed per channel or region with its own error history, not over the attended stream as a whole.
- On satiability: IAC is not set-point regulated. The authors liken it to food and heat maintenance, then say LP "must be kept maximal". LP is locally self-extinguishing (a learned region stops paying), but the drive as a whole never satiates while any region still pays. A set point on LP, and the "rest when learning runs too fast" behaviour in D62, is our addition with no support in this paper.
- IAC already has a negative LP reward when error rises (forgetting, environment change). A set-point version has to say what it does with negative LP.
