# Duschinsky 2015, "The emergence of the disorganized/disoriented (D) attachment classification, 1979–1982" (History of Psychology 18(1):32-46, doi:10.1037/a0038524, PMC4321742)

Source read: full text from PMC (APA Open Access, retrieved via NCBI E-utilities), including Table 1 (A/B/C criteria) and Table 2 (D indices, "From Main & Solomon (1990)"). Main & Solomon 1990, "Procedures for identifying infants as disorganized/disoriented during the Ainsworth Strange Situation" (in Greenberg, Cicchetti & Cummings, eds., *Attachment in the Preschool Years*), was NOT read. Everything about it below is Duschinsky's account and quotation.

## Claims
- D was "proposed for the Ainsworth Strange Situation Procedure by Mary Main and Judith Solomon" in 1986 and is "Often exhibited most strongly on reunion".
- Main & Solomon's seven indices of disorganization/disorientation (Table 2, verbatim):
  - "I. Sequential display of contradictory behavior patterns"
  - "II. Simultaneous display of contradictory behavior patterns"
  - "III. Undirected, misdirected, incomplete, and interrupted movements"
  - "IV. Stereotypies, asymmetrical movements, mistimed movements, and anomalous postures"
  - "V. Freezing, stilling, and slowed movements and expressions"
  - "VI. Direct indices of apprehension regarding the parent"
  - "VII. Direct indices of disorganization or disorientation"
- D is rated on a "1-to-9 scale of disorganization/disorientation". The scale grades "interpretive certainty", and only "at the highest ends of the scale" does it become a judgment of degree.
- Hand-to-mouth on reunion is "usually sufficient for D category placement" (Main & Solomon 1990, p. 140, as quoted).
- Main's 1982 rule: "behavior is not disordered simply because it is inappropriate, antisocial, asocial or because it appears infrequently".
- Ainsworth's four interactive measures, as summarized in Table 1. Example: "proximity-avoiding = the intensity and duration of behaviors that direct attention away from the caregiver as he or she approaches on reunion, such as averting the face".

## Evidence
Historical analysis based on archival manuscripts and personal communications with Main, Solomon and Hesse. It is not an empirical study. The D cut-off on the 9-point scale (the point at which D is assigned) is not stated in what I read.

## Verdict: reject (for this Bot)
D coding requires a certified coder judging bodily behavior on reunion: freezing, stereotypies, approach-avoid contradictions, hand-to-mouth. It is a human-infant clinical classification whose meaning depends on an attachment system "inferred from visible behavior", which Duschinsky stresses "the viewer can never know for sure". Applied to a Bot whose face is driven by a model, several indices would fire from rendering glitches ("mistimed movements", "freezing") and be uninterpretable. One useful caution carries over: anomalous behavior is not evidence of disorganization if the context explains it.
