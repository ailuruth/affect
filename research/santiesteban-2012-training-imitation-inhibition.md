# Training social cognition: from imitation to Theory of Mind (Santiesteban et al.)

Idalmis Santiesteban, Sarah White, Jennifer Cook, Sam J. Gilbert, Cecilia Heyes & Geoffrey Bird, *Cognition* 122(2):228–235, 2012, doi:10.1016/j.cognition.2011.11.004.

## What was read
**Not the paper.** The author-hosted PDFs (users.ox.ac.uk, samgilbert.net) were blocked by this session's network policy. What I had: the abstract, quoted in search excerpts, and descriptions of the design in later papers (one, Oliveros, Santiesteban & Ulloa 2024/2025, says it follows this design). **No means, test statistics or effect sizes were seen.** Do not cite beyond the abstract-level claims below.

## The computation, precisely
- **Design (abstract-level):** three training groups. (1) **Imitation-inhibition**: do the opposite finger movement to the one seen. (2) **Imitation**: do the same. (3) **General (non-imitative) inhibition** control. Training on day 1. Twenty-four hours later, tests of the automatic-imitation task, visual perspective-taking (the Director task) and Theory of Mind (Strange Stories).
- **Result (abstract):** "The group trained to inhibit their tendency to imitate showed improved performance on the visual perspective-taking test, but not the ToM test." The improvement was "specific to imitation–inhibition training": neither imitation training nor general inhibition training had it.
- **Hypothesis tested:** perspective-taking and ToM depend on *inhibiting* imitation (self–other control), not on imitation itself (the mirror-neuron account). Training the control of shared self–other representations should transfer to other tasks that need the agent to keep the other's representation separate from its own.
- **Caveats on record:** a ceiling effect on the Director task has been reported. Bukowski et al. 2021 and Oliveros et al. 2024/2025 failed to find training effects on self-reported perspective-taking or ToM. The 2012 group sizes are not confirmed here; a later replication used 37/36/36.

## What it means for the designs
- **One variable, two uses.** If the effect holds, the quantity that stops seen actions from being executed is the same one that lets the agent hold another's viewpoint without collapsing it into its own. For Principle 3 this is the right shape: reading the other through one's own body *requires* the self–other partition. It is not a brake added after the reading. A design should derive both "don't copy" and "infer the other's state" from one attribution operator, and predict that strengthening it improves both.
- **Trainable with a short dose.** One day of crossed (counter-imitative) practice changed a 24 h later outcome. With Cook et al. 2014 (already read: 50–90 min of crossed training reverses mirror responses), the partition is learned on minutes-to-hours scales, not fixed.
- **Not a whole-system guard**: the transfer is to perspective-taking, not to emotional contagion. Nothing here says affect routes are controlled.

## Verdict
**watch (abstract-level; contested replication).** It is the only causal evidence I found that imitation control and other-modelling share a variable, and it has failed to replicate on self-report measures.
