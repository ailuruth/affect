# Detecting Digital Chameleons (Bailenson, Yee, Patel & Beall, Computers in Human Behavior 2008)

Bailenson, J. N., Yee, N., Patel, K., Beall, A. C. "Detecting digital chameleons." Computers in Human Behavior 24(1):66-87, 2008. doi:10.1016/j.chb.2007.01.015. Author PDF from vhil.stanford.edu.

## What was read
- Full text (22 pages): introduction, Experiments 1 and 2 (method, results, discussion), general discussion, limitations. In Table 2 (standardized factor scores) the minus signs were lost in text extraction, so I quote only the directions and statistics stated in the prose.

## Claims
- Experiment 1 ("one-degree of freedom" Turing test with a button): "When the computer agent mimicked them, users were significantly worse than chance at identifying the other human."
- Experiment 2 (immersive VR, agent delivering a 195 s persuasive message): "participants were more likely to detect mimicry in an agent that mirror-mimicked their head movements (three degrees of freedom) than agents that either congruently mimicked their behaviors or mimicked those movements on another rotational axis."
- Detection is costly: "Explicit detection of the mimic caused the agent to be evaluated poorly in terms of trustworthiness and the warmth factors".

## Evidence
- Exp. 1 (26 students, 13 pairs, 16 trials each; mimic at a 4 s lag): effect of agent behaviour F(4, 48) = 3.85, p < .01, η²p = .24. Accuracy in the mimic condition was significantly below chance (t(12) = 2.21, p < .05). Confidence was *highest* in mimic trials (F(3, 36) = 3.57, p < .05). "Mimickers were more likely to pass the Turing Test than actual humans!"
- Exp. 2 (46 students; head motion copied at a 4 s delay in the mirror and congruent conditions; the axis-switch description gives no delay): detection was 80% for the mirror-mimic, 44% for the congruent (reversed) and 38% for the axis-switched condition (F(2, 41) = 3.22, p < .05). Detectors rated the agent less trustworthy (F(1, 41) = 6.02, p = .04, η² = .09) and less warm (F(1, 41) = 7.20, p = .03, η² = .11). Informativeness and agreement showed no detection effect (both F ≤ .33).
- Pool contamination: the 80% mirror-mimic rate was inflated by word of mouth from the 2005 study. A replication six months later with 53 naive paid participants found 6 detectors (11%).
- Authors' account of why: "Nothing in the natural world mimics perfectly ... digital chameleons mimic perfectly in terms of scope, scale and timing of any behavior." On credibility: "The mimicking agent is essentially lying with non-verbal cues, and this violation of conversation maxims may be what lowers its perceived credibility and warmth when detected."

## Failures documented
- **Exact, mirror-form mimicry is noticed.** Copying a partner's movement in the same form (left is left) was the most detectable transform. Up to 80% detected it in a primed pool, and 11% did in a naive one.
- **Noticed mimicry lowers trust and warmth**, the two social judgements that mimicry is supposed to raise.
- **Abstracted contingency is harder to detect** but still contingent. Reversed or axis-switched responses were detected under half the time.
- Limitation stated by the authors: "we have implemented mimicry strategies in the most rudimentary manner – exact mimics in terms of scale ... and frequency ... This is a poor approximation of how humans mimic one another".
