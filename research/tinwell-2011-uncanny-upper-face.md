# Facial expression of emotion and perception of the Uncanny Valley in virtual characters (Tinwell, Grimshaw, Abdel Nabi & Williams, Computers in Human Behavior 2011)

Tinwell, A., Grimshaw, M., Abdel Nabi, D., Williams, A. "Facial expression of emotion and perception of the Uncanny Valley in virtual characters." Computers in Human Behavior 27(2):741-749, 2011 (online 2010). doi:10.1016/j.chb.2010.10.018. Read from the authors' pre-print in the Aalborg University repository.

## What was read
- The pre-print, in full up to the reference list: introduction, method (design, participants, stimuli, AU table, procedure), results with Tables 2-3, discussion. It is a pre-print, so numbers could differ slightly from the journal version.

## Claims
- "even fully and expertly animated characters are rated as more uncanny than humans and that, in virtual characters, a lack of facial expression in the upper parts of the face during speech exaggerates the uncanny by inhibiting effective communication of the perceived emotion, significantly so for fear, sadness, disgust, and surprise but not for anger and happiness."

## Evidence
- Design: 3 (human actor / full-animation virtual character / "lack": upper-face movement disabled) × 6 basic emotions, repeated measures. N = 129 male students. Ratings of familiarity and human-likeness on 9-point scales. Animations were hand-keyed in FacePoser to copy the actor's AU intensities through each spoken line. In the "lack" condition, the upper-face AUs were switched off where used. For example, anger is AU4, 7, 17, 24 when full and AU17, 24 when lacking; surprise is AU1, 2, 5, 26 when full and AU26 when lacking (Table 1; the fear row is garbled in the extraction).
- Recognition: human 79.81%, full animation 73.75%.
- Means (Table 3), familiarity human / full / lack: anger 6.92 / 4.36 / 4.18; fear 6.03 / 4.30 / 3.60; sadness 6.54 / 5.05 / 4.57; surprise 6.46 / 4.66 / 4.29. Human-likeness, fear: 7.43 / 4.51 / 3.68.
- Character type × emotion interaction: familiarity F(10, 1280) = 3.281, p < .001; human-likeness F(10, 1280) = 6.065, p < .001.
- Discussion: the frozen upper face "may have evoked a sense of the virtual characters resembling the 'waxwork figures or automata'", and the ambiguity "presented an incongruity with the intonation of speech".

## Failures documented
- **Face–voice incongruence reads as uncanny.** When the upper face does not move with the emotional tone of the voice, ratings drop, most for fear.
- **Expert copying of human AU timing still falls short of human.** Full animation scored roughly 1.5-2.6 points below the human actor on familiarity for every emotion, although it was keyed by hand to the actor's own face.
- Scope: static, one-way clips of a speaking character, rated by observers. There is no interaction or listener behaviour. The evidence concerns perception of the face itself, not dyadic coupling.
