## Claims (from the 2004 abstract, verbatim)
- "(a) the amygdala mediates the memory-modulating effects of adrenal stress hormones and several classes of neurotransmitters; (b) the effects are selectively mediated by the basolateral complex of the amygdala (BLA); (c) the influences involve interactions of several neuromodulatory systems within the BLA that converge in influencing noradrenergic and muscarinic cholinergic activation; (d) the BLA modulates memory consolidation via efferents to other brain regions, including the caudate nucleus, nucleus accumbens, and cortex; and (e) the BLA modulates the consolidation of memory of many different kinds of information."
- Human imaging: "the degree of activation of the amygdala by emotional arousal during encoding of emotionally arousing material (either pleasant or unpleasant) correlates highly with subsequent recall."
- Conclusion: activation of neuromodulatory systems acting on the BLA "plays a key role in enabling emotionally significant experiences to be well remembered."

## Evidence (from the 2013 and 2005 proxies; not from the 2004 text)
- Time-limited: post-training stimulants, epinephrine and corticosterone enhance memory when given shortly after training but are "ineffective ... when administered several hours after training" (2005).
- Endogenous signal: amygdala norepinephrine (NE) "levels increase quickly and peak within 15-30 min after inhibitory avoidance training". NE "levels assessed within 90 min after training correlated highly with subsequent retention performance" (2005). Rats "that have greater increases in release subsequently display better retention" (2013).
- BLA neuron activity after footshock: "peaked at over half an hour later and did not return to baseline until over 2 h", and most cells peaked "∼40 min after the footshock" (2005). Inactivating the BLA "immediately or within several hours after training impairs memory" (2005).
- Gating: glucocorticoid enhancement "requires noradrenergic activation of the amygdala". It depends on arousal induced by training (2013, citing Okuda et al. 2004 and Roozendaal et al. 2006). β-blockade (propranolol) or glucocorticoid-receptor antagonists block the effects of arousal and stress hormones (2013).
- The modulator is separate from the store: amphetamine infused into the BLA after training enhanced both place and cued-response memory, while hippocampal infusions enhanced only spatial memory and caudate infusions only cued memory (2013). BLA noradrenergic activation raises hippocampal Arc protein, and BLA stimulation enhances hippocampal LTP (2013).
- Cortex: pairing a tone with BLA stimulation shifted auditory-cortex tuning toward the tone within 45 min, and the shift lasted weeks (2013).
- Humans (2013): propranolol before an arousing story with pictures blocked the memory advantage at 1 week. A pleasant or unpleasant video within 30 min after learning enhanced 1-week memory, and the effect was lost at longer delays (up to 45 min). Amygdala activity varies with arousal intensity and "the valence—positive or negative—is not critical". Arousal selectively enhanced memory for emotional over neutral words. Salivary α-amylase, a noradrenergic marker, correlated with memory for emotional pictures.
- The 2013 summary of the strategy: "Selectively remembering our more important experiences seems to be the best strategy."

## Mechanism
There are no equations in what was read. In outline: an arousing event releases adrenal epinephrine (which acts through vagal and brainstem routes; this detail is our background knowledge, not verified here) and glucocorticoids. Both converge on noradrenergic signalling in the BLA, which peaks about 15-40 min after the event and stays elevated for about 2 h. During that window the BLA does not store the memory. It scales consolidation in whichever structure does: hippocampus for place, caudate for response, cortex for features. It acts through efferents and by raising plasticity products such as Arc. The effect is modulatory and post-hoc. It applies to the trace being consolidated after the event and depends on arousal, not valence.

Our formalization: for a trace i encoded at t_i in store s, the consolidated change is Δw_i^s = η_s · (1 + β · ∫ A(t) K(t − t_i) dt) · e_i, where A(t) is an arousal-driven modulatory signal, K is a window lasting roughly hours, and e_i is the encoding change. A gates storage in every store but carries no content.

## Implications for the engine (our inferences)
- **Arousal at encoding is the replay weight, and it must come from the core.** McGaugh supplies the biological case that the persistence of a day's episode should scale with a scalar modulatory signal that is (i) produced by the agent's own body response to the event, (ii) independent of valence, (iii) effective for minutes to hours after the event, and (iv) applied to whatever store holds the content. For S6, each replay-buffer segment should carry the integrated arousal over a post-event window, not just at the instant. Under Principles 2 and 10, arousal must be a readout of the learned latent state, for example the magnitude of homeostatic deviation or of its rate of change. It cannot be a hand-set variable.
- **One modulator for many stores.** The BLA result (one site enhancing place, response and cortical memory) means a single tag can weight replay into the fast episodic store, the slow temperament parameters and the partner-response mapping alike. One arousal tag per segment is enough. Separate tags per store are not needed.
- **The window favours significant moments with the operator.** Arousing interactions consolidate strongly, and so does ordinary material encoded just before or after them, combined with tagging-and-capture. Of a few hours a day, the arousing minutes and their surroundings are what will last. That is a strong prior for scarce data: replay is weighted by arousal and by recency within the window, and the neutral remainder is allowed to fade.
- **Ablation prediction.** Remove the arousal weight from replay (uniform replay, or β = 0). The agent should still learn average operator statistics, but it should lose the selective, long-lasting changes tied to emotionally significant exchanges. Relationship-specific asymmetries, such as wariness after a rupture or warmth after a repair, should then form more slowly or not at all. This is our derivation of a testable S6 contrast, not McGaugh's.
- **Valence-free weighting matters for Principle 6.** Weighting by arousal and not by valence means replay does not preferentially keep moments when the operator was pleased. That protects against a flattery bias in what the agent remembers.
- **Guardrail.** The PTSD-relevant findings (propranolol or morphine after trauma reducing later symptoms) show the same mechanism can over-consolidate. An engine with unbounded arousal weighting could fixate on one bad exchange. The weight should saturate, and the satiable homeostatic objective is the natural bound.

## Verdict: adopt (arousal-weighted, post-event, store-agnostic consolidation as the replay weighting); watch (re-read the 2004 text if access is obtained)
- The mechanism is one of the best-supported in the field, and the abstract plus two later reviews by the same author agree on it. The full 2004 review, with its dose-response and lesion details, remains unread. Specific quantitative claims attributed to "McGaugh 2004" should not be made from this note.
- It fits Principle 10 as a gain on consolidation derived from the core's arousal readout. It is not a separate module.
