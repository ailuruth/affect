# The role of the right temporoparietal junction in the control of imitation (Sowden & Catmur)

Sophie Sowden & Caroline Catmur, *Cerebral Cortex* 25(4):1107–1113, 2015 (online 2013), doi:10.1093/cercor/bht306 (PMC4380005).

## The computation, precisely
- **Design (abstract):** neuronavigated rTMS over right TPJ and over a control site (mid-occipital), during a task that manipulates **imitative compatibility and spatial compatibility simultaneously and independently**. This removes the confound in the classic finger task, where the imitative and the spatial match coincide.
- **Result (abstract):** "Both imitative and spatial compatibility effects were apparent on response times; however, the effect of imitative compatibility was significantly stronger, indicating less control of imitation, during stimulation of the rTPJ relative to the control condition." Later reviews state that spatial compatibility was not changed.
- **What the control depends on:** disrupting rTPJ selectively increases interference from the *other's body-part-specific action*. It does not increase interference from a merely spatial stimulus feature. So the controlled variable is "this activation comes from another agent's body", not "a stimulus conflicts with my response". That fits a self–other attribution, not generic conflict control. (Caveat: Darda & Ramsey 2019 find no consistent rTPJ activation across fMRI studies; Hogeveen et al. 2015 find the TPJ role to be task-dependent.)

## What it means for the designs
- **The attribution operator is selective to body-mapped input.** The guard should act on the component of motor drive that arrives *through the self–other body map* ($P(o)$), and leave ordinary stimulus–response mapping alone. In a face model: the partner-to-own face correspondence is the only route that needs a provenance tag. Generic spatial or temporal cues (the partner moved, the partner looked) do not.
- **Knock it out and imitation rises.** Removing the operator does not abolish perception of the other. It increases copying. This is the ablation a design should reproduce: delete the attribution step, and the agent's face copies more while its reading of the partner is unchanged.
- **Time scale:** single-trial RT, sub-second. TMS effects last minutes.

## Verdict
**watch (abstract-level).** It is the cleanest causal dissociation of imitative from spatial control. Numbers must be taken from the original.
