## Claims
- "Feelings are mental experiences of body states." They signify need, injury, optimal function, threat or social interaction, and they "constitute a crucial component of the mechanisms of life regulation".
- **Box 1 vocabulary.**
  - **Action programmes** are innate, largely stereotyped sets of physiological actions.
  - They come in two kinds. **Drives** (hunger, thirst, libido, exploration and play, care of progeny, attachment) mostly have interoceptive triggers. **Emotions** (disgust, fear, anger, sadness, joy, shame, contempt, pride, compassion, admiration) are "mostly triggered by the perception or recall of exteroceptive stimuli".
  - The changes in body state that a programme causes are mapped, and "may remain non-conscious or may be experienced consciously as feelings".
- **Body maps came before feeling.** Central maps of body state are enough to detect and correct deviations without feeling. Feeling was added because "body states are necessarily valenced — they are either good or bad from the point of view of homeostasis". As a result, "feelings are powerful proxies of ongoing biological value". They also "facilitate learning of the circumstances surrounding a change in body state and ... prediction of future situations".
- **Four engineering elements of a homeostatic action programme:**
  1. a competent stimulus (an internal deviation, or an external object perceived or recalled);
  2. a detector;
  3. an execution site that coordinates a set of corrections;
  4. a detector of completion that halts the corrections.
- **Learning extends** homeostatic goals to "money, power or drugs".
- **Locus.**
  - Feelings arise in the upper brainstem (parabrachial nucleus, PAG, NTS, superior colliculus), which holds topographic body maps.
  - The insula and SI/SII "contribute to but are not essential for the emergence of feelings".
  - The insula's role is "more of a modulatory than a generative role". It provides finer-grained maps and is perhaps "essential for the introduction of feelings into the flow of cognitive processes".
- **Evolution.** The brainstem is conserved, so "the parsimonious assumption should be that feelings are present" in other species.
- **Cellular hypothesis.** The crucial cells are unmyelinated interoceptive axons. Ephaptic signalling among them may play a part. In humans about 80% of fibres in the main vagal trunk are unmyelinated.

## Evidence
- **Patient B:** complete bilateral insula destruction (herpes simplex encephalitis). He keeps pain, pleasure, itch, tickle, happiness, sadness, caring, compassion, hunger, thirst and distension feelings. "Immediate comfort appears to be their main concern, fairly unbridled by cognitive constraints."
- Children born without cerebral cortex show behaviours compatible with feeling.
- Lesions of the posterior upper brainstem cause coma or a vegetative state; ventral lesions cause locked-in syndrome with feeling preserved.
- Electrical stimulation of the brainstem evokes valenced responses that animals will learn to switch on or off.
- Decorticated mammals keep coherent, goal-oriented behaviour.

## Mechanistic commitments
- **Body-state maps** are the substrate of feeling.
- **Valence is fixed by homeostatic value**: whether a state is good or bad for the organism decides its sign.
- **Programmes are closed loops** with an explicit stop condition. This is satiation (Principle 6).
- **Feeling's function is learning and anticipation.** It extends regulation to new triggers and future states.
- A **subcortical generative locus**, with cortex refining and connecting feeling to cognition.

## What it implies for a single formal core
Damasio's account is closest to the brief's homeostatic core:
- valence is the sign of change in homeostatic value, a quantity the core already has, such as the drive gradient or costate;
- feeling's stated function, learning and anticipation, is what a learned value function adds to a reflexive regulator.

Element 4 (stop on completion) means **every programme must terminate when the deviation is corrected**. A core based on drive reduction has this; a core that maximizes reward does not.

The "drive versus emotion" split by trigger source (interoceptive versus exteroceptive) does not need two mechanisms in a single core. An exteroceptive cue that predicts body change and an internal deviation should both enter as changes in predicted value. That is a derivation obligation.

## What an ablation could separate
- **Map resolution, insula-style.** Coarsen the body-state representation available to the higher layer while keeping the fast regulator. On Damasio's account, valenced behaviour and regulation survive and only fine discrimination and the link to cognition drop. Patient B's profile is "comfort-driven, cognitively unbridled". In the engine: Claude's account of the episode loses specificity while the face and voice keep their valenced dynamics.
- **Stop condition.** Remove completion detection, so drives continue after their need is met. Overshoot should follow, which is Principle 6's flattery and overconsumption risk.
- **Learning extension.** Freeze value learning. Only innate triggers should move valence, and the agent should never come to value cues for the partner's presence.
- **Subcortical versus cortical locus.** See emotion-elements-synthesis.md. Damasio predicts that the fast core alone carries valence and learning, and that losing the conceptual layer leaves them intact.

## Verdict: adopt (homeostatic valence; four-element programme; subcortical-locus pole)
This is the subcortical counter-position to LeDoux & Brown 2017. The cellular (ephaptic) hypothesis is speculative and has no use in an engine.
