# Kuhl, Tsao & Liu 2003, "Foreign-language experience in infancy: Effects of short-term exposure and social interaction on phonetic learning" (PNAS 100(15):9096-9101, doi:10.1073/pnas.1532872100, PMC166444)

Source read: the full text on PubMed Central (both experiments, methods, results, general discussion); Figures 1-2 by caption and text.

Read for search pass 9 as the nearest reachable primary source on social gating (Goldstein, King & West 2003 was not reachable). Note: this is a study of **perception** learning, not of the infant's own output.

## Key claims
- **Experiment 1 (live).** 32 American infants from 9.3 months (21 completed): 12 sessions of 25 min over 4 weeks (10 min book reading, 15 min toy play) with four native Mandarin speakers using infant-directed speech, eye contact and the infant's name; control group the same with English. About 33,120 Mandarin syllables heard (25,989-42,184); the test syllables were 6.5 % of them.
- **Test.** Conditioned head turn on a synthetic alveolo-palatal affricate/fricative contrast (differing only in amplitude rise time, ≈30 vs ≈100 ms), 30 trials. Mandarin-exposed 65.7 % (SE 2.40) vs English controls 56.7 % (SE 2.29), F(1,19) = 7.34, p < .05; equal to Taiwanese infants raised on Mandarin, t(57) = 0.67; no loss with 2-12 days' delay; native /ra-la/ unaffected.
- **Experiment 2 (recorded).** Same speakers and materials on DVD, audiovisual (n = 15) or audio only (n = 13); more syllables heard (M = 49,866). No learning: neither group differed from English controls; both differed from live Mandarin (p < .01); F(3,45) = 3.94 across the four groups.
- **Attention does not explain it.** Rated attention: live 3.53-3.59, AV 2.79, audio 1.48 (F(3,45) = 101.76); AV attended more than audio but learned no more.
- **Authors' account.** A live person supplies social cues that "attract infant attention and motivate learning" and referential cues (gaze to the named object, followed by the infant); analogies to songbirds that learn from live but not taped tutors.

## What it means for the engine
1. **A social gate on learning that is not a reward for the learner's output.** What the partner's liveness changes is whether the infant's perceptual statistics are updated, not what the infant produces. In pass 7's terms this is a gate on a past-term (representation) objective, which is approval-closed for expression as long as the gate does not depend on the agent's own expression (see Proposition 3 of new-core-search-9-synthesis.md).
2. **The gate is not attention.** More looking (AV vs audio) did not give learning; whatever live interaction adds, it is not captured by a gaze or attention score. That fits Principle 6 (no reward for attention) and warns against using the user's attention as the gate.
3. **What the paper does not separate.** Live sessions contain contingency, referential gaze and social presence together; the study cannot say which one gates. A gate that reads "the partner answered my act" is contingency and inherits the approval route; a gate that reads only the partner's liveness or referential gaze toward a shared object does not.

## Verdict
- **background (social gating of perceptual learning; the gate is neither exposure nor attention).** Small groups (10-15 completers per cell); one contrast; perception only.
