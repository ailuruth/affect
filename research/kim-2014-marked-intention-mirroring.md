## Key claims
- **Marked mirroring, as stated from Gergely:** the mother shows her understanding of the infant's state "while concurrently signaling that she is not experiencing the same state herself", by a schematic, exaggerated, slowed display with some features made salient and others dropped, sometimes mixed with another state. Markedness "serves to mitigate the potentially arousing effect of direct imitation" while making aspects of the infant's state salient. **Ostensive cues** (eye contact, head tilt, infant-directed intonation, the infant's name) signal that the display "concerns him and what unfolds within him".
- **Two codes:**
  - *direct mirroring*: imitation of the infant's facial/gestural or vocal behaviour in the same modality, no words;
  - *intention mirroring*: non-imitative, marked, ostensive **verbal** reference to the infant's state, judged accurate by an independent coder.
- **Sample:** 50 first-time mothers (Adult Attachment Interview in the third trimester); analyses on 25 secure vs 16 insecure/dismissing; modified still-face at 7 months (side by side, seen through a mirror; 3 × 2 min).
- **Results:**
  - direct mirroring did not differ (facial/gestural 0.033 vs 0.034 per unit time; vocal 0.025 vs 0.020; β_AAI ≈ 0, p = .97 and .37);
  - intention mirroring was more than twice as frequent in secure mothers (0.043 vs 0.016; β = −.08, z = −2.73, p = .006), and rose from phase 1 to the reunion in both groups (p = .005) while facial direct mirroring fell (p = .01);
  - direct and intention mirroring were uncorrelated (facial r = .021);
  - during the still face, infants of secure mothers looked at the mother more (proportion of fixations .359 vs .268, t(39) = 2.38, p = .02) and away less (.289 vs .382);
  - **the mediation test failed:** intention mirroring did not predict infant gaze (b = −.33, p = .49).
- **Limits stated:** middle-to-upper-class sample; preoccupied group too small; **contingency of mirroring not measured**; valence of the mirrored infant signal not coded.

## What it means for the engine
1. **Markedness is a source tag carried by the form of the response.** A marked display is contingent on the infant's own state but deviates from how the partner looks when the state is the partner's own (exaggerated, schematic, slowed, mixed). The infant can use the deviation to refer the display to itself rather than to the partner. Formally: the partner's response u is decomposed into a component predicted by the partner's own baseline (their state) and a component predicted by the agent's own recent expression (about the agent), and the second is read as information about the self.
2. **That reading is a perception and self-model route, not an expression one.** If the engine used the partner's marked response as a target for its own state latent, the partner would write the agent's self-model (pass 7: partner features must be context, not targets). If it used it as reward, it is the approval route outright. The safe use is evaluation: whether the agent's partner model separates "about me" from "about them" components.
3. **Weak evidence.** The paper shows that a verbal, marked form of responding is more common in secure mothers and that their infants look more during the still face, but not that marked mirroring causes anything in the infant (mediation n.s.). The model's learning claim remains untested here.
4. **The engine's own display toward the user:** Q24 and the concern-posture history (removed in revision 9 under Q29) were about the agent showing the partner's state back. Markedness says a human caregiver's useful display is explicitly not a copy; it is a deformed, ostensive one. That is consistent with D93 (a copy is not what helps), but it gives no learning signal for the agent.
5. Vocabulary: the coded verbalisations name states; in the engine such words exist only in Claude's text, never below it.

## Verdict
- **background (the first operational coding of markedness; the learning claim not supported by its own mediation test).** Useful for the decomposition in point 1 and as a caution against using partner responses as self-model targets.
