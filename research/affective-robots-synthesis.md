# Synthesis: embodied affective robots and agents, what was built and how it failed

Scope: the robots and virtual agents that gave a body or face an internal affective state, read for (1) architecture, (2) what was innate and what was learned, (3) whether anything was ablated, (4) documented failures, and (5) what survives the Principles in brief.md. Each claim names its note. The notes say how much of each source was actually read. Three were read only in part or through substitutes: Cañamero 1997 (abstract only), Boucenna IJSR 2014 (read through its two conference versions), and BabyX (the 2014, 2015 and 2021–23 technical papers are closed, so no BabyX equations were read).

## 2. What the table shows

**Every system is assembled, and every one hand-names its emotions.** Kismet has nine emotions in a designer's [A, V, S] space. BabyX has six basic-emotion circuits and eight named neurochemicals, with temperament as a dial. Watanabe uses Russell's axes, Boucenna five expression labels, Cañamero's line named hormones, and Churamani a valence–arousal regressor. BabyX's authors chose assembly on purpose ("large functioning sketch", "Lego-like"). Moerland's survey describes the whole RL line the same way: emotions "hooked on characteristics of the MDP". No system derives its components from one objective (Principle 10), and none lets emotion structure emerge (Principle 2).

**What was learned is narrow, and none of it is the emotion itself.** Three kinds of learning appear:
- an associative map from the partner's face to the agent's own state or motor pattern (Watanabe, Boucenna, BabyX);
- a policy or value function with emotion as a reward term or a modulator (Moerland's 52 papers, Churamani);
- within-session statistics of the partner (Hoffman's transition counts, Hiolle's responsiveness estimate).

Only 2 of Moerland's 43 rows learn the links between situation and emotion.

**Nothing persists.** No system reports internal state carried across sessions. Churamani's mood resets each session and its memory spans seconds. Watanabe's learning rate decays to zero. BabyX reports no persistence, and its use was restricted to developers and lab studies. The goal "persists across turns and days" has no precedent here.

**Ablations are rare, small and never predicted.** Lewis & Cañamero compared fixed and modulated gain (10 runs per cell, no statistics). Churamani lesioned the affective core and found nothing in user behaviour or impressions. Hoffman compared reactive and anticipatory robots. The RL papers compare emotion against no emotion. BabyX proposed lesions and reported none. No paper derives an ablation's effect from equations before running it (Principle 5 and Principle 10 ask for both).

**Recurring failures.**
1. *Few hand-named axes break even for a hand-designed face.* Kismet needed a third axis, and special-case postures, to keep anger and fear apart. This is the brief's "one-dimensional attempts fail" in its oldest form.
2. *A reward on the partner's approval trains fawning.* Churamani's users steered the robot by smiling. BabyX releases dopamine on praise. Lewis & Cañamero found pleasure tied to the abundant resource made survival worse. Three independent cases support Principle 6.
3. *The mirroring signal was there and went unused.* Kismet's subject mirrored it into an escalating loop. Keepon's operator copied a child, and the child began sharing play. Neither robot learned anything from it.
4. *Learning a mimicry map needs a mirroring partner, and lag breaks it.* Gaussier's argument (Boucenna note): if both agents display independent states, the map cannot form; "the parent is no more than a mirror". Boucenna had to instruct people to imitate for 2–10 min. The 0.8–1.6 s human lag, paired at zero lag, taught the wrong map. Watanabe's pure co-occurrence Hebbian rule would bind the partner's habitual face to the most frequent state, where LMS drops features present in every state.
5. *Self and other are merged.* Watanabe injects the other's state into the robot's own. BabyX's joint action works because "the 'agent' field of event representations is ignored". Gold & Scassellati show why this is hard: a partner who follows you looks like your own effect.
6. *State that does not reach the face does not reach the person.* Churamani's lesion left user impressions unchanged, and the authors say the expressive channels did not depend on the state.
7. *Timing beats appearance.* Keepon's 4 degrees of freedom engaged children where the 29-degree-of-freedom Infanoid overwhelmed them. Hoffman's anticipation changed perceived fluency and commitment without changing mean task time. Both argue that a purely reactive agent cannot move in phase with a partner.
8. *Evaluation is demonstration.* Typical evidence is single runs, n of 5–17, no statistics, private test worlds never reproduced, operator-controlled robots, and human–human data standing in for the agent (BabyX 2023).

## 3. The brief's BabyX claim, checked

Now read (CACM 2016, AI and Ethics 2022, J R Soc NZ 2023), with the 2014, 2015 and 2021–23 technical papers still unread:
- "Muscle-driven face": confirmed. The real-time face interpolates a precomputed FEM simulation, which is the same route as the brief's S4 plan with ArtiSynth.
- "Subcortical circuits": confirmed. The brief should add that the emotional core is named basic emotions plus named neuromodulators. Principle 2 is a sharper difference than the subcortical framing.
- "No conceptual layer": qualify it. By 2021–23 BabyX has event representations, episodic memory, plan inference, word learning and a self-report mode ("I like dogs"). It has no language model, and its self-report reads the emotion state directly, which Principle 4 forbids.
- "No long-term living": consistent with everything read.
- "No controlled evaluation": confirmed for BabyX as an agent.
- "Ablations were planned and never run": write it as "lesions proposed (2016) and not reported since". Three papers cannot prove "never".
- Missing from the brief: BabyX already proposed the learned self–other face map (babbling, caregiver mirroring, a dopamine-gated delayed association). The engine's claim over BabyX for this map is not the idea. It is (a) measuring the map at all, (b) learning it without a reward for praise, and (c) keeping track of who did what.

## 4. Ideas that survive the Principles

- **Two-sided drives** (Kismet): a body variable with both an under- and an over-stimulated side, each producing its own regulatory behaviour. This is satiable by construction (Principle 6).
- **Deficit-gated cue salience** (Lewis & Cañamero, Eq. 1): m_i = d_i(1 + α·cue_i), so a satiated need ignores its cue. This is the scalar ancestor of the brief's need-conditioned attention. The engine's version must make the gate content-dependent and per-need, not one shared α.
- **Pleasure as a gain, not a reward** (Lewis & Cañamero): a signal released on improvement in the rate of need reduction modulates perception and persistence and is never maximized. Their own data warn where it fails: tied to an abundant source, it hurts. For the engine, the user's smiles are the abundant source.
- **The identifiability condition** (Gaussier, via Boucenna): the self–other face map forms only if the partner's display depends on the agent's display. The formal core should state this as a proposition, with its ablation: replace the operator's responses with a time-shuffled copy and the map does not form.
- **The developmental sequence for the map** (BabyX, Boucenna, Watanabe): spontaneous facial babbling, contingent mirroring by the partner, and an association between the agent's own efference and the partner's face, delayed by a learned lag and trained by an error-driven rule (LMS or prediction error, not co-occurrence Hebbian learning). All of it comes without a dopamine bonus for praise.
- **Expression before action** (Kismet's lower expression threshold): the face shows the state before behaviour commits. This fits the affective face route in face-development-synthesis.md.
- **Feeling as predicted action readiness corrected by the body** (Lowe & Ziemke): it yields an ablation. Cut the feedback from the produced face and voice back to the state, and expressions should overshoot or fail to recover after a perturbation.
- **Anticipation and fluency measures** (Hoffman): concurrent motion, human idle time and functional delay, as objective timing measures next to coupling. Acting ahead of the partner, not faster task completion, is what people perceive.
- **Responsiveness reshapes decay, never reward** (Hiolle 2014, earlier note).
- **Moerland's satiability point**: of the reward schemes surveyed, only homeostatic drive reduction satiates. Appraisal and novelty bonuses do not, so any curiosity term must carry its own satiation.

## 5. What this engine must do that none did

1. **Derive every component from one objective, and derive each ablation's effect from it before running it.** No system did either. Lewis & Cañamero and Churamani ran lesions, but neither predicted the result from equations.
2. **Let emotion structure emerge from body variables in a learned latent; name nothing.** Every system above names its emotions, axes or neurochemicals. Kismet's stance patch shows the hand-named route breaking.
3. **Learn the self–other face map from contingent mirroring, and measure it.** That means map accuracy against the number of mirroring episodes, contingent vs yoked or time-shuffled mirroring, and a lesion of the map that degrades reading of the operator's face. Nobody has published this curve or control for a face (BabyX: demo only; Boucenna: 51–61% with instructed imitators, no control; Watanabe: one run).
4. **Handle the lag and the follower confound explicitly.** Learn the response lag rather than pairing at zero lag (Boucenna). Keep an efference record separate from the agent's sensed face. Weight map learning toward the agent's spontaneous displays, and keep "partner answers me", "I follow the partner" and "partner acts on its own" as competing whole-history hypotheses (gold-scassellati note). Do not assume the operator mirrors: measure how much partner dependence each session holds, since an adult will not imitate at Boucenna's instructed rate.
5. **Keep self and other apart while sharing the body.** Read the other through one's own generative model (Principle 3) without injecting the other's state into one's own (Watanabe) or dropping the agent field (BabyX).
6. **Persist state across sessions and days, and show it.** S6's claims (temperament, operator-specific relationship surviving restarts, the replay-tagging ablation) have no precedent at all in this literature.
7. **Never reward the partner's approval.** Churamani's fawning, BabyX's praise dopamine and Lewis & Cañamero's abundant-resource result are three warnings. Responsiveness and contact may reshape decay constants and gains, never a maximized quantity.
8. **Make the state visible in the face and voice, with timing that anticipates.** A state that does not shape expression does not change the human (Churamani). Phase, lag and anticipation matter more than degrees of freedom (Keepon, Hoffman).
9. **Evaluate on published instruments and on the human,** with pre-registered ablation predictions and statistics, not demos, single runs or a gridworld the project wrote. The bar the field left is low: no system here has a long-term, controlled, ablated evaluation of an affective agent with a face.
