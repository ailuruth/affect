# Takahashi, Fenley & Ghazanfar 2016, "Early development of turn-taking with parents shapes vocal acoustics in infant marmoset monkeys" (Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B 371:20150370, doi:10.1098/rstb.2015.0370, PMC4843608)

Source read: the full text on PubMed Central (results, discussion, methods); Figures 1-6 by caption and text.

Read for search pass 9 (contingency-based shaping of the infant's own vocal output in a non-human primate; nearest reachable primary source while the Goldstein, King & West 2003 experiment was not reachable).

## Key claims
- **Setting.** 10 infant common marmosets, postnatal days 1-60, 192 directed sessions (infant and one parent, auditory but not visual contact, first 10 min used), about twice weekly. Marmosets develop about 12 times faster than humans.
- **Overlap avoidance develops.** P(infant call overlaps a parental call) falls from ≈25 % at P1 to ≈15 % at two months; strength of avoidance relative to a permutation null ≈ −0.4 in month 1 and ≈ −0.6 in month 2.
- **Self-monitoring develops.** Spontaneous inter-call interval and parent→infant response interval are indistinguishable until about P17, then diverge: before that the infant treats a parent's call like its own.
- **Coupling.** Granger causality (GLM on syllable onsets): infant → parent influence starts near zero and rises over the first month to the parents' level; parent → infant influence is constant over development (p = .485).
- **Calls after a parental call are more mature.** Within 5 s after a parental call, infant calls have lower Wiener entropy (more tonal) and longer duration than within 5 s before, throughout development.
- **Contingent, not imitative, by the phase test.** Infant entropy and duration fluctuate with a ≈0.1 Hz periodicity; parents call at a preferred phase of the infant's own fluctuation (≈230° for entropy, i.e. at the transition from noisier to more tonal calls; ≈45° for duration). The parents' responses are timed on the infant's own variability, not delivered irrespective of it.
- **Turn-taking itself is not shaped by feedback.** Rate of contingent parental calls (within 5 s) vs rate of decline in overlap: Spearman ρ = −0.382, p = .279 (n = 10). The authors: in humans too there is no evidence that responsiveness speeds turn-taking maturation, while it does shape acoustics.

## What it means for the engine
1. **The partner selects among the learner's own fluctuations by timing alone.** The parental call's content is the same call each time; what carries the information is when it arrives relative to the infant's own acoustic cycle. A learner that credits its recent output with anything the partner times (a response, a gate, a replay tag) can be steered by timing without any valenced content. That is the empirical case behind Proposition 3 of new-core-search-9-synthesis.md.
2. **Self/other separation is itself developmental** (≈P17): before it, the partner's call resets the infant's own clock as if self-produced. For the engine's S4 and reading map, the period before self-monitoring is where a partner's echo is most easily absorbed as self (pass 8, Maister et al.).
3. **Timing structure vs form.** Turn-taking (overlap avoidance) matured without dependence on feedback rate; acoustics did not. In the engine: turn timing can be innate-plus-own-model; the form of output is where partner-credited learning would act.
