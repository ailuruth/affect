# Htet, Jimenez-Rodriguez, Gagliardi & Prescott 2024, "A coupled-oscillator model of human attachment dynamics evaluated in a robot dyadic interaction" (Living Machines 2024, LNCS 14930:52-67, doi:10.1007/978-3-031-72597-5_4)

Source read: the author-accepted version from White Rose eprints (CC BY 4.0). Read: abstract, introduction and related work, the experimental setup and robot results (Section 5), and the conclusions. The oscillator equations and phase-space analysis (Sections 2-4) were skimmed, not checked.

## Claims
- Attachment is modeled as "a behavioural coupling between motivational oscillators". Attractive coupling gives ambivalent tendencies and repulsive coupling gives avoidant ones.
- "coupled robot dyads generate dynamical patterns that resemble caregiver-child interactions", "associated with quantitative and qualitative observations of the 'strange situation' procedure".

## Evidence
What was adapted. The paper's Fig. 5 calls it a "robot-robot SSP":
- Two small robots: a "child" that explores an arena and approaches a "caregiver" on a box.
- Care is exchanged only by vocalization: "Once the child is close to an April tag it starts asking for care by vocalising."
- No separation, reunion or stranger episodes are described. The text says "We tested the model system over multiple episodes of X minute duration", with the duration left as the placeholder "X" in the accepted version.
- Secure, avoidant and ambivalent are not classified from behavior. They are set by parameters ("three regions of parameter space corresponding to putative secure, ambivalent and avoidant regimes"; "ϵAv ≈ 0.6"; "ϵAm > 0.6").

How it was scored:
- Visually, by comparing plots of distance-from-base and vocalizations with a published plot of human reunion movement (Fig. 1, from Prince et al. 2021). "We show in Figure 6, a pattern of oscillatory behavior, for the secure attachment scenario ... that resembles that illustrated in figure 1".
- No Ainsworth scale, no classification criteria, no statistic comparing to human data.

## Verdict: reject (as an evaluation precedent)
It borrows the Strange Situation's name and its exploration and return pattern, not its procedure or scoring. The "attachment style" is the parameter the authors set, so the evaluation cannot fail. It confirms that no robot work found so far has scored an artificial agent with Ainsworth's scales or classification rules.
