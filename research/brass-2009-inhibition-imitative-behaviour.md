## The computation, precisely
- **Paradigm** (as described by Darda & Ramsey and Sowden & Catmur, both read in full): the finger-lifting stimulus–response compatibility task (Brass et al. 2000). Lift index or middle finger to a number cue while watching a task-irrelevant hand lift the same or the other finger. Incompatible minus compatible RT is the "general compatibility effect". This classic effect mixes an imitative and a spatial component. When the two are separated (Sowden & Catmur, control-site data), the imitative part is ~7 ms and the spatial part ~64 ms, on RTs of ~430–500 ms.
- **How the papers read in full summarise it.** Darda & Ramsey: "Brass and colleagues further proposed a dissociation of roles for the mPFC and rTPJ during imitation control - the rTPJ distinguishes between self- and other-generated actions, and the mPFC enforces the self-generated action when faced with conflict from an action representation generated by another agent (Brass et al., 2009)." Wang & Hamilton: "Brass et al. (2009) suggest that both [inhibition of mimicry and mentalising] require good self-other distinction". They also report Spengler et al. 2010b: self-focus (seeing oneself in mirrors) *strengthened* inhibition of mimicry. Hogeveen et al.: "the core neurocognitive function of TPJ and mPFC in the social domain is to control the degree to which the self or another is represented (Brass et al., 2005, 2009)".
- **Regions (excerpt-supported):** inhibiting imitation engages anterior fronto-median cortex (aFMC) and temporoparietal junction (TPJ), the same regions engaged in mentalizing and in judging agency. The paper's keywords: imitation, inhibition, prefrontal cortex, temporo-parietal junction, mentalizing.
- **The claim, as cited by everyone after it:** controlling imitation is not generic response inhibition. It is a **self–other distinction** over a *shared representation*. Seeing an action activates the same motor code as intending it, so the system holds two candidate sources for one code. Control means deciding which activation is *mine* and enforcing it. TPJ distinguishes self- from other-generated actions; aFMC/mPFC enforces the self-generated one (this division of labour is confirmed by Darda & Ramsey's paraphrase above).
- **Patient link** [unverified]: frontal patients show "imitation behaviour" (Lhermitte), compulsively copying the examiner. In the shared-representation account, this is the self–other control failing, not a hyperactive mirror system.
- **What the control signal depends on:** who is the source of an active motor representation. It does not depend on the content of the action. The same computation is proposed to serve imitation control, perspective-taking and agency.

## What it means for the designs
- **The operation is attribution, then selection, on one shared code.** Perceived partner movement $P(o)$ and own intention $u$ both land in motor code $m$. The guard is not a gain on $P(o)$. It is a source tag $a \in [0,1]$ (self vs other) carried with each component of $m$, and an output rule that executes only what is tagged self: $m_{\text{out}} = \Pi_{\text{self}}\,m$. "Perceive through my own motor code but don't copy" is then a **partition of a shared code by provenance**, not a subtraction of the partner.
- **Is it the whole-system guard?** Only for the direct route. The account is about motor activation. A partner face that changes the agent's *affect*, which then drives the agent's face, arrives at motor code tagged self. Brass's computation would pass it. The tag would have to be carried through the latent state to close indirect routes (see the synthesis).
- **Time scale:** within a single response, sub-second.

**Later tests of the account (read in full):** the stimulation evidence supports a TPJ role (Sowden & Catmur 2015: rTMS raised imitative interference 7 → 20 ms; Hogeveen 2015: anodal tDCS improved inhibition, d = 0.58). The fMRI meta-analysis does not: no mPFC overlap for any contrast, and no rTPJ overlap for imitative compatibility (Darda & Ramsey 2019, 12 studies, 300 participants).
