# Lundqvist 1995, "Facial EMG reactions to facial expressions: a case of facial emotional contagion?" (Scand J Psychol 36:130-141, doi 10.1111/j.1467-9450.1995.tb00974.x)

Source read: published version via DiVA (Örebro repository), abstract, Experiment 1 method and results, references. Experiment 2 skimmed. Read as a substitute for Dimberg 1982, which I could not obtain; this is the same Uppsala paradigm and cites Dimberg 1982 for the procedure.

## Claims
- Viewers' facial EMG over corrugator supercilii, zygomaticus major, lateral frontalis, depressor supercilii and levator labii regions differs by the expression shown (sad, angry, fearful, surprised, disgusted, happy) in a way "to large extent ... consistent with the hypothesis that facial expressions are contagious."

## Evidence
- Electrodes placed "according to Fridlund and Cacioppo's (1986) 'Guidelines for Human Electromyographic Research'"; impedance "reduced to less than 10 kΩ"; "low-pass filters set at 1000 Hz and high-pass filters set at 10 Hz"; 50 Hz notch; "contour following integrators with the time constant set at 0.02 s"; "digitized on-line at 100 Hz per channel".
- Scoring (long-window variant): "scored as phasic responses; i.e., the mean EMG during 1 s immediately before stimulus onset was subtracted from that recorded during the stimulus exposure." Exposure "8 s in 6 adjacent trials", intertrial interval 25-45 s.
- Cover story (sweat glands) "(Dimberg, 1982)"; 2 of 48 subjects who guessed the purpose were excluded.
- Statistics: MANOVA (Pillai-Bartlett V) then per-muscle ANOVAs with Geisser-Greenhouse correction, Tukey HSD at α = .05. Exp. 1: Stimulus V = 0.3569, F(12, 411) = 4.62, p < .0001; corrugator F(2.7, 126.1) = 7.63, p = .0002; zygomatic F(2.0, 93.0) = 8.69, p = .0004, happy > angry and fear.
- Not clean mimicry: corrugator rose to all stimuli, and surprised faces drew more zygomatic than angry faces, which the author attributes to valence.

## Verdict: adopt (as the long-window scoring rule)
- Confirms the scoring convention: change from a 1-s pre-onset baseline, per muscle, compared across expressions within subject. Pairs with Dimberg & Thunberg 2012 for the sub-second window.
- Shows that some responses track valence rather than the specific expression; an operator-mimicry claim must show the match is expression-specific, not just "more smile when the agent looks positive".
