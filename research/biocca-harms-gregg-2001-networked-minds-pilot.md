# Biocca, Harms & Gregg 2001, "The Networked Minds Measure of Social Presence: Pilot Test of the Factor Structure and Concurrent Validity" (4th International Workshop on Presence, Philadelphia, May 2001)

Source read: the full 9-page PDF from the ISPR proceedings archive (matthewlombard.com/ISPR/Proceedings/2001/Biocca2.pdf). It is the same file the 2004 paper cites at astro.temple.edu. The two-column text extraction interleaves columns, so the per-factor item lists below were reassembled by reading. Pairings are as printed, and two items appear garbled in the source (noted).

## Claims
- Definition: "Mediated social presence is the moment-by-moment awareness of the co-presence of another sentient being accompanied by a sense of engagement with the other (i.e., human, animate, or artificial being)."
- Three theoretical dimensions (co-presence, psychological involvement, behavioral engagement), giving eight pilot factors: isolation/inclusion, mutual awareness, mutual attention, empathy, mutual understanding, behavioral interaction (labelled "behavioral interdependence" in the text), mutual assistance, dependent action.
- Format: "An initial set of 69 seven-point Likert scale items"; 38 retained.
- Standardised item alphas: mutual awareness .74, mutual attention .82, empathy .76, mutual understanding .87, behavioral interdependence .75, mutual assistance .69; isolation and dependent action are "Insf." (two items each). "The average Cronbach alpha reliability is α =.77. The range is between .69 and .87."
- Empathy items (pilot wording) include "When I was happy, the other was happy", "When the other was happy, I was happy", "The other individual was influenced by my moods", "I was influenced by my partner's moods", and "The other's mood did NOT affect my mood/emotional-state" (reverse-keyed).
- The dependent-action pair is printed as "The other could not act without me." / "I could not act with the other." The second looks like a typo for "without"; unverified.

## Evidence
- N = 76 undergraduates in 38 pairs, mixed design (order between, face-to-face vs NetMeeting video within); Desert Survival ranking task, questionnaire after each half.
- All scales were higher face-to-face. Significant for mutual awareness (F = 40.2), mutual attention (F = 10.2) and mutual assistance (F = 4.1, p = .05). Not significant for empathy (F = 0.6, p = .43), mutual understanding (main effect; an order interaction instead), behavioral interaction, or dependent action.
- Caution: "The factor analysis is limited by the inadequate ratio of items to subjects and the small subject sample, and, therefore, should be interpreted cautiously."

## Verdict: reject as the version to run; keep as provenance
This is a superseded pilot. Its 8-factor, 38-item form was replaced by the 6-factor, 36-item form in Harms & Biocca 2004 (see harms-biocca-2004-networked-minds.md), which is the one to administer. It is still worth citing for two things. First, the authors defined social presence to cover "artificial" beings, which licenses (but does not validate) using the measure with an agent. Second, its Empathy factor showed no face-to-face advantage in an unemotional task, a warning that affect-related subscales may not move unless the interaction itself carries emotion. A within-subject ablation must therefore use an emotionally loaded task, or a null result says nothing about the affect system.
