# Normalization as a canonical neural computation (Carandini & Heeger)

Citation: M. Carandini, D. J. Heeger. "Normalization as a canonical neural computation." Nature Reviews Neuroscience 13(1):51–62, 2012 (online 23 Nov 2011). doi:10.1038/nrn3136. Author manuscript PMC3273486.

Source read: the PMC author manuscript, HTML text-extracted. Read in full: introduction, the sections on invertebrate olfaction, retina (light adaptation and contrast normalisation), V1 (contrast saturation, cross-orientation suppression, the drive-to-suppression transition, surround suppression, winner-take-all), higher and non-visual cortex (MT, ventral stream, A1, MST multisensory, LIP value), attention, circuits and mechanisms, behavioural evidence, discussion, Box 1 (the normalisation equation and its variants) and Box 2 (coding rationales). Figures were seen only through their captions. Primary data papers cited in the review were not read.

## The computation
- Box 1: R_j = γ D_j^n / (σ^n + Σ_k D_k^n); general form R_j = γ(Σ_k w_jk I_k)^n + β over σ^n + (Σ_k α_jk I_k^m)^p. The numerator's summation field sets selectivity; the denominator's broader suppressive field sets the pool. "The normalization signal can be averaged over a period of recent time to model adaptation."
- **The drive-to-suppression transition (eq. 9), the result most relevant here:** R = (w₁c₁ + w₂c₂)/(σ + c₁ + c₂) with w₁ ≫ w₂. With c₁ = 0, stimulus 2 drives the neuron; with c₁ high, "c₂ appears only in the denominator, where it exerts a purely suppressive effect." The same input changes the sign of its effect with context, with no change of weights.
- The same form fits fly olfaction (a mask odour shifts the curve right on a log axis), photoreceptor light adaptation, LGN contrast normalisation, V1, MT, A1, MST visual–vestibular integration ("multisensory enhancement decreases with stimulus intensity", and the non-preferred modality "can both drive the cell when presented alone and suppress it when presented with a stimulus of the preferred modality"), and LIP value coding (relative, not absolute, value).
- Attention multiplies the drive before normalisation; whether it acts as contrast gain or response gain is predicted by stimulus size relative to the attended region.
- **Mechanism ties gain to time constant:** under shunting inhibition, raising conductance g lowers both the response gain and the time constant (V = I/g; τ = C/g), confirmed in retina where higher contrast makes impulse responses "weaker and faster". The authors stress the mechanism differs across systems (GABA needed in the fly antennal lobe, not for V1 cross-orientation suppression); what is shared is the computation.
- Box 2 rationales: dynamic-range use, invariance to one dimension at the expense of another, decodability, linear discriminability, max-pooling versus averaging depending on input balance, redundancy reduction. Deficits are linked (by citation only) to amblyopia, epilepsy, depression and schizophrenia.

## What it means for the design
1. **A per-channel ceiling that is a weight, not a gain.** Put the agent's own drive and the partner's mapped drive for expression k in one normalised unit: R_k = (w_k^s d_k^s + w_k^p d_k^p)/(σ_k + Σ_j α_kj(d_j^s + d_j^p)). Then R_k is a weighted average of the w's scaled by a saturating factor, so the partner's contribution can never exceed w_k^p however strong the partner is (my derivation from eq. 9 with n = 1). An innate w_k^p < 1 is a ceiling that no partner intensity reaches.
2. **Copy is excluded by curvature, not by a bound alone.** R_k is concave and saturating in the partner's level, so it cannot equal the partner's level over any interval of levels (identity is linear): whole-range copying is impossible for every parameter value. Partial matching inside one operating range remains possible.
3. **Sign flips by context with no learned sign:** when the agent's own drive on channel k is high, the partner's same-channel input moves only the denominator and suppresses; when the agent is quiet, it drives. This is the shape of D83's "within each ceiling, the strength … can flip sign", obtained from one equation. It is the same qualitative shape as the dCaAP anti-coincidence unit (gidon-2020-human-dcaap-anticoincidence.md), but with a measured, system-general law behind it.
4. **Time scales from the pool:** in the conductance form, each channel's time constant is C/(g_L + pool), so the face is fast when much is happening and slow when quiet, with no time constant to tune. This is the liquid-time-constant steady state already recorded (hasani-2021-liquid-time-constant.md); the review supplies the biological evidence and the context sign flip.
5. **Limit:** this is an existing canonical computation, and in ML it is layer normalisation and the LTC neuron. It is a constraint layer, not a new core.

## Verdict
- [Normalization as a canonical neural computation (Carandini & Heeger)](carandini-heeger-2012-normalization-canonical.md) — 2012 — adopt (as constraint layer) — one equation across fly, retina, V1, A1, MST and LIP; an input that drives alone suppresses when a preferred input is strong (sign flip by context), the non-preferred input's weight is its ceiling, and shunting ties gain to time constant; not a new core (it is LN and the LTC steady state).
