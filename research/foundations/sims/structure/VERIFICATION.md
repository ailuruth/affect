# Verification of the expressive-structure test bed (ES1–ES4)

Independent check, 2026-11-25. I did not write this work. I edited no existing file. Reruns and probes ran in a copy at /tmp/esverify, and probe scripts are in /tmp/esverify/probe/ (not committed).

## Summary verdict

- **Reproducible.** All five scripts and both figures reproduce byte for byte. Two outputs, out/es1_per_seed.txt and out/es_paired_exploratory.txt, have no script that generates them. I regenerated both exactly from probes.
- **Mostly honest, with four label errors:**
  - ES1-3 is called "confirmed" even though its conjunction failed.
  - The ES1 participation ratios for the independent-tables and oracle arms are measured on the wrong values.
  - ES2-4 holds by construction and is not labelled as such.
  - The status headers of both candidate documents contradict their own new sections.
- **Expressive structure: neither candidate meets the brief yet.**
  - **Costate is closer.** In its *myopic limit* it shows content-dependent selection over people, memory-enabled actions and person × topic actions, all in a learned space. Still missing:
    - attention over perceived cues;
    - needs structured as a field over a learned space (here the needs are a global 2-vector);
    - time;
    - any *pre-registered* confirmation that its distinctive anticipation term helps.
  - **The Robust regulator** does not meet it. The selection rule, the shared map, the memory "seek" action and the relation map are not in its formal statement (robust-regulator.md:59-69). Its behaviour in ES2 never uses relations, so ES2 does not show relationships for it.

**ES1 (sim_es1_social_space.py)**

- **Participation ratio, line 34.** The panel values use the shared map `X[Pn] @ Wh.T` for *every* arm. For the independent-tables arm (a map it never uses) and for the oracle, the reported participation ratios of 1.48 and 1.64 (RESULTS.md:9-10) are therefore not those arms' own values. Measured on the arms' own values:

  | Oracle | 1.66 |

  Only the full arm's 1.67 is correct as specified.
- **Learning data.** PREDICTIONS.md:24 says "the learning data are identical". In fact only the learning *rule* is shared, because the data depend on each arm's choices. This is harmless.
- **Median and runaway columns.** These, and the per-seed file, are not produced by the committed script.

**ES2 (sim_es2_relations_memory.py)**
- **Drift (line 20), presence (K = 3), companions (line 10) and arrival probability (line 13) match PREDICTIONS.md:66-78.**
- **Episodes are collapsed.** The spec says "retrieve the stored episode with the largest e·Δb_obs" (PREDICTIONS.md:87). The code ranks the *per-person mean* of the stored effects (line 31). Episodes are therefore per-person tables with no content beyond who the person was.
- **Anticipation term (line 27).** It uses the *current* e, not the forecast e at the companion's arrival. It uses max(0, E[·]), not E[max(0, ·)]. This is not in the spec. It is a heuristic (see the Identification section).
- **ES2-5.** The pooled r = 0.93 multiplies both sides by e, which shares a ‖e‖ factor. The result survives removing it (probe3):
  - r after dividing both sides by ‖e‖: **0.86**;
  - per-need r of the predicted against the true companion effect: **0.88 and 0.84**.

**ES2b (sim_es2b_anticipation.py)**
- **The string patch on line 5 has no assert.** It is verified indirectly. The companion/nomem costs equal ES2's, and the rr_nomem costs (about 0.18) show the memory is off.

**ES3 (sim_es3_reliability.py)**
- **SST.** The code's SST is the sum of squares about the *current* mean, SXX − SX²/n (line 8). The spec says "about their running mean" (PREDICTIONS.md:127).
- **Start of the weight.** The code sets w = 0 until n_arr > 10, which means 11 arrivals. The spec says 10.
- **Effect.** Both deviations are minor, and neither changes a verdict.

**ES4 (sim_es4_topics.py)**
- **Matches PREDICTIONS.md:141-163:** 40 seeds, feature dimensions 81 and 17, ridge regression with λ = 1, exploration, the agreement window, and the paired statistic.

**Random streams (all scripts)**
- **The world (seed) and the encounter stream (seed + 1000) are shared across arms.** Presence and arrival draws are precomputed and do not depend on the arm.
- **Exploration and observation noise (seed + 2000) are seeded identically, but they are consumed differently per arm.** The `and` short-circuits and the draws are conditional. They therefore desynchronise, which adds variance to the paired comparisons but no bias. The comparisons are fair.

## Fairness of the ablations

**ES1 scalar gauge.** This is the most degenerate low-dimensional version: it discards *which* need is short. It is a legitimate example of "one-dimensional attempts", but a weak test of "the same candidate, low-dimensional". I ran two probes (probe1):
- **Winner-take-all query.** One gauge plus the index of the most depleted need: value = max(e)·Δb̂_{i, argmax e}.
  - Agreement with the oracle is 0.79 (full 0.835), and 0.72 on first encounters (full 0.78).
  - Median cost is 0.156 against 0.092. The paired ratio full/winner-take-all is **0.37 (CI 0.17–0.78)**, with full lower on 15/20 seeds. There were 2 runaways.
  - So the vector query survives a fairer ablation on cost. Most of the gap to the scalar arm in choice agreement, however, comes from knowing *which* need is short, not from the full vector.
- **Rank-1 shared map.** 17/20 runaways. This is a straw man (the learned space is smaller than the number of needs), so it is uninformative.

**ES1 independent tables.** A fair "no shared space" control. Its fallback to the population mean is sensible.

**ES2 scalar arm.** It regulates as well as Costate: the paired ratio is 0.99 (CI 0.70–1.40) in the companion world and 0.82 in the random world. So content-dependent memory retrieval (ES2-3) has **no demonstrated behavioural benefit** in ES2. Disclosed at RESULTS.md:52.

**ES2 Robust regulator arm.** It differs from Costate only in dropping the companion term. That is fair as "Costate minus anticipation". It is not a test of the Robust regulator's own formalism (see below).

**ES4 additive and tables arms.**
- **Additive.** A fair low-capacity control. Note that it has *fewer* parameters than bilinear (34 against 162), so ES4-2 is a parameter-increasing win and does not support item 4.
- **Tables.** Cannot generalize by design.
- **A missing middle control:** a low-rank person × topic factorization, or person tables plus topic tables.

## Identification with each candidate

**Costate**
- **The test bed uses the myopic limit.** Costate's μ = −∇_b V, with V a discounted rollout (K5, costate.md:95). The test bed uses μ = e = −∇d(b). That is the one-step, myopic limit, which costate.md:137 itself names as the *rival* baseline: "a deficit field, homeostatic RL with no anticipation".
- **RESULTS.md:31 says "myopic". costate.md:288 drops that word.**
- **The drift schedule in ES1 and ES4 is deterministic and predictable.** A true μ would anticipate the block switches, and the test bed's μ does not.
- **The drive differs from the formal one.** The test bed's d = ½‖max(−b, 0)‖² is flat above the set point. That violates costate.md:48 ("unique minimum at b*") and robust-regulator.md:79 ("overshoot raises d"). Overshooting one need while fixing another is free here.
- **Anticipation.** The companion term is a heuristic stand-in for the anticipatory term −∇_xV·E[Δx|i] (costate.md:86): one step ahead, using the current e, and max(0, E[·]). The reliability weight w (a prequential R²) is not derived from V. It is an add-on.
- **Memory "seek"** priced by μ·E[Δb] *is* consistent with costate.md:121-122.

**Robust regulator**
- **The identification "e·ĉ_i = μ·E[Δb|i]" is not in its formal statement.**
  - Its ĉ_p is a disturbance gain on the reconstructed face ŷ_p, learned by dĉ_p/dt = γ⟨e, Hŷ_p⟩ − σĉ_p (robust-regulator.md:66).
  - It is not an action-effect map, it is not fitted by ridge regression on static appearance, and it does not drive a discrete argmax selection.
  - Its action law is a continuous cancellation, u = −K∇d − K_I m − Σĉ_p Hŷ_p (robust-regulator.md:64).
- **A derivation is available but not written.** Greedy control-Lyapunov selection (choose the item maximising −ḋ = e·Δb) would derive the rule. Until §2.2 states it and K2 is amended for a shared map, the ES results are an add-on for this candidate.
- **None of the observer ξ_p, the integral mood m or the mimicry K1 appears in the test bed.**

**Add-ons for both candidates (not derivable from either as written):**
- ε-greedy exploration at 0.1, gated at ‖e‖ > 0.1;
- the reliability weight w;
- the max(0, ·) companion form.

## Principles

- **Principle 2 (no named emotion variables): no problem.** Needs are generic.
- **Principle 6 (satiable drives): the drives are satiable.** The drive is zero at or above the set point and the agent rests. The 0.06% approach rate at ‖e‖ ≤ 0.01 is threshold leakage.
- **Principle 6 (no reward for attention or time): a risk, not a violation in the toy.**
  - Δb here is a fixed property of the person and topic, not their response.
  - In the engine, if "contact" fill is measured from the user's engagement, then ES4's machinery learns which topics elicit engagement. Below the set point that is a flattery gradient, and satiation caps it but does not remove it.
  - Costate's K2 do-conditioning protects only the λ term (costate.md:91), not b_c.
  - What is needed: define contact fill from the agent's own exposure, not from user responses, or extend K2 to Δb learning. Then add a toy that checks that topic choice gains nothing from a partner's smile or dwell time.
- **Principle 10 (one formal core).** The add-ons listed above, and the Robust regulator's adopted rule.
