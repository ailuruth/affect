# v3 sketch: dyadic loop regulation, from enactivism and developmental robotics

Lens: enactivism (sensorimotor contingencies; participatory sense-making), perceptual crossing and its evolved-agent models, homeokinesis and DEP, morphological computation. Written 2026-11-25 without reading the other sketches.

## 0. The idea in one paragraph

De Jaegher and Di Paolo define social interaction as "the regulated coupling between at least two autonomous agents, where the regulation is aimed at aspects of the coupling itself ... without destroying in the process the autonomy of the agents involved" (2008 chapter, p. 39). Read literally as a computation:
- The agent does not learn a map from the partner's face to its own face. It regulates a property of the **round trip through the partner**: my face changes their face, and their face changes mine.
- The regulated property is the stability margin of the joint (dyadic) linearised dynamics. The agent adjusts only its own leg of the loop.
- It measures the partner's leg with its own body: its own innate micro-motion serves as a probe, so a non-responsive partner (a recording, a "shadow") gives a leg of zero.
- The partner's share of the agent's expression is then a fixed point of this regulation. It depends on how responsive the partner is to the agent, and how persistent both bodies are, not on a gain the designer sets.

## 1. Mechanism (formal statement)

**Signals.**
- Agent motor output y_t ∈ ℝ^m: m = 24 AU intensities + voice channels (speech on/off; pitch, energy and rate when live).
- Partner perceived in the agent's own motor coordinates: x_t ∈ ℝ^m (body route).
- Conversation events e_t: own and partner VAD, word onsets.
- Lag embedding at 10 Hz over d = 10 lags (1 s): X_t = [x_t; …; x_{t−d+1}], Y_t likewise.

**Body (morphology, innate).** Each AU is a first-order muscle-like plant with its own time constant. A = diag(α_i), with α_i set from that AU's own autocorrelation in training faces. Then

  y_{t+1} = A y_t + (I − A) u_t + ξ_t,  ξ_t ~ N(0, σ_ξ² I) innate micro-motion, known to the agent by efference.

**Self (latent, learned slowly).** A CTRNN with log-spaced time constants (33 ms to 30 min), N ≈ 256:

  τ ⊙ ḣ = −h + W σ(h) + U [e_t; y_t],  u_t = P h_t + K_t X_t.

- W, U and P are trained offline only on the agent's own face given its own events: the self-model never sees the partner.
- The partner enters through **K_t** alone.
- K_t is a fast plastic operator in ℝ^{m × md}.

**The partner's leg, read through the agent's body.** The agent estimates how its own probe changes the partner's next frames. Exponentially weighted instrumental-variable regression, with τ_B ≈ 20 s:

  x_{t+1} ≈ Λ̂_p X_t + B̂_t Ξ_t,  B̂_t = Ĉov(x_{t+1} − Λ̂_p X_t, Ξ_t) Ĉov(Ξ_t)^{−1},

- Ξ_t is the lag-embedded ξ, constrained to reduced rank r_B ≈ 4.
- ξ is generated by the agent and is independent of everything before it, so B̂ is an interventional estimate: common drivers such as topic, turn and laughter do not bias it.
- A recording or a shadow has B̂ = 0 by construction.
- Λ̂_p, the partner's own persistence, is initialised from the agent's own self-model Jacobian (the "like me" prior) and refined online.

**Joint operator.** In lag-embedded coordinates,

  J_t = [ Λ_a     (I−A)K_t ]
        [ B̂_t     Λ̂_p     ]

Λ_a = A + (I−A) P ∂h/∂y is the agent's own one-step Jacobian: body plus latent.

**Regulation of the coupling (the core update).** Let μ_1..μ_r be the r largest-modulus eigenvalues of J_t (r ≈ 3), with right and left eigenvectors v_j and w_j, partitioned into agent (a) and partner (p) blocks. Eigenvalue perturbation gives ∂μ_j/∂K = (I−A)^T w̄_j^a v_j^{pT} / (w_j^* v_j). The update is

  τ_K K̇ = Σ_j (1 − |μ_j|) Re[ (μ̄_j/|μ_j|) ∂μ_j/∂K ] − λ K,      (★)

with τ_K ≈ 2 s; write η := 1/τ_K.
- Each regulated joint mode is driven toward the unit circle: the edge between dying out and running away.
- The leak λ removes coupling that the joint loop does not need.

**Slow traces.**
- Per-partner relationship operator: K̄_p ← (1−ε)K̄_p + ε K_t, with ε set for hours to days. K_t starts at K̄_p when partner p returns.
- Session-level mean of the mode spectrum (mood-scale).

**Readouts.**
- The face is y.
- The voice is the voice channels of y.
- Claude is coupled as in §4.

**The computation.** The agent performs adaptive regulation of a dyadic loop's stability margin. Its own leg is shaped to the measured leg of the other.

### Two derived properties (they drive §3)

**P-a (scalar dyad).** Take J = [[a, k], [b, p]] with 0 ≤ a, p < 1.
- The eigenvalues are μ± = (a+p)/2 ± √(((a−p)/2)² + kb).
- μ+ = 1 if and only if **kb = (1−a)(1−p)**.
- The fixed point of (★):
  - For |b| small, the gradient ∝ b is overwhelmed by λ, so k* ≈ (η/λ)(1−μ₀) b/|a−p| with μ₀ = max(a, p) (for a ≠ p): **reciprocity** (couple to whoever couples to you).
  - For |b| large, k* → (1−a)(1−p)/b: **compensation** (do not double a loop the partner already closes).
- So k*(b) is an **inverted U**.
  - As η/λ → ∞, k* stays bounded at (1−a)(1−p)/|b|.
  - The mirror value k = 1 is reached only in the coincidence b = (1−a)(1−p).

**P-b (rank bound).** At any fixed point of (★), λK* = Σ_j (1−|μ_j|) Re[rank-1 term].
- Hence **rank K\* ≤ 2r**, whatever η, λ, σ_ξ or the data.
- A delayed mirror is a shifted identity of rank m = 24 (or 25).
- So for r ≪ m, the mirror is **not a fixed point** of the mechanism.
- Where the coupling concentrates (which AUs, which lags) is chosen by the eigenvectors of J. That is, by where the partner responds to the agent (B̂) and by which modes both bodies' bandwidths can sustain (A, Λ̂_p).

## 2. Novelty test (honest)

| Part | Existing mechanism it is |
|---|---|
| AU plant with per-channel time constants | linear filter; morphological computation in the Pfeifer sense |
| CTRNN self-model, offline training | RNN/CTRNN (Beer); self-model as in homeokinesis |
| K_t fast plastic coupling | fast weights |
| B̂ from own micro-motion | system identification with a dither / persistent excitation; instrumental variables; dual control |
| "Like me" prior for Λ̂_p | inverting one's own model (Yoshida & Man; Meltzoff) |
| (★) eigenvalue-gradient regulation of the spectral radius to 1, with leak | self-organised criticality by plasticity: homeokinesis (Der), DEP's κ "edge of chaos", Aguilera & Bedia 2018 (criticality learning rule; abstract only), spectral-radius control in reservoirs |
| k ∝ 1/b at high b | gain scheduling / indirect adaptive pole placement (self-tuning regulators: Åström & Wittenmark 1973, abstract only; the Lund TR is a scanned image) |
| Per-partner K̄ | slow weights / EMA |
| Mode readouts | eigendecomposition (DMD-like) |

**Closest prior work, read in the primary source.**

1. **Der & Martius 2016, "Dynamical self-consistency leads to behavioral development and emergent social interactions in robots" (ICDL; read in full).** This is the nearest by far.
   - Two DEP-controlled humanoids see each other's joint angles: "x = (x_own, x_oth)", with the learning signal x′ "replaced with the sum x′_own + x′_oth". DEP is τΔC = M ẋ′ ẋ^T − C, with "a normalization with a factor κ (meta-parameter)".
   - Result: "rapid convergence (in seconds) toward a synchronized motion"; "the sighted robot learns to imitate the motions of the blinded robots"; with delayed vision "in-phase or antiphase motion patterns".
   - DEP PNAS 2015 (abstract, rule, Methods normalisation read): "With κ small ... activity breaks down ... With κ sufficiently large ... ending up in full chaos for large κ."
   - **So the closest prior work shows both target failures openly:**
     - its social outcome is synchrony and imitation (risk a);
     - one scalar κ runs it from rest to chaos (risk b).
   - Its partner coupling is Hebbian on velocity correlations of all sensors, partner included. It has no estimate of the partner's responsiveness to the agent. It cannot tell a partner from a recording; its robots imitate a blinded "sender" that does not respond to them.
2. **Der 2008, "Artificial life from the principle of homeokinesis" (read in full).**
   - Time-loop error E = vᵀv, "Δc = −ε ∂E/∂c ... fast synaptic dynamics which is constitutive for the behavior".
   - Two wrestling humanoids: "the combined dynamical system is driven by the time-loop error gradient flow towards a working regime characterized by high activity while still avoiding the strongly chaotic region".
   - Loop regulation through another robot therefore exists, but over the undivided loop, with no partner leg singled out.
3. **Di Paolo, Rohde & Iizuka 2008 (CSRP 589 preprint of the New Ideas in Psychology paper; read in full).**
   - "sensitivity to contingency arises from processes of circular causality that characterise the collective dynamics."
   - Live versus recording: "the mutual coupling results in a feedback loop where coordination (crossing) is robust to perturbations. But, the feedback is broken in the one-way interaction."
   - Agents are evolved CTRNNs, with no learning rule at all.
   - Auvray, Lenay & Stewart 2009 (behind a bot wall) is known here only through this paper's account: subjects cannot tell partner from shadow by stimulation pattern, yet "two-way mutual scanning is the only globally stable condition."
4. **De Jaegher & Di Paolo, "Making sense in participation" (2008 IOS chapter, the short form of the 2007 PCS paper; chapter read in full).**
   - The 2007 article itself could not be retrieved (paywall; mirror unreachable).
   - The definition quoted in §0 and the autonomy condition — "individuals as interactors must not lose their own autonomy" — are the specification that (★) and P-b turn into mathematics.

**What does not exist in what I read:**
- An online rule whose regulated quantity is the stability margin of the **joint** operator.
- In that rule, the other's leg is identified **interventionally from the agent's own spontaneous micro-motion**.
- Its consequences, which follow from the equation:
  - the inverted-U, reciprocal-then-compensatory share (P-a);
  - the hyperparameter-free rank bound that excludes the mirror (P-b);
  - zero coupling to a recording.

**Verdict: recombination.**
- Every part is an existing mechanism. The objective is homeokinesis- and criticality-style plasticity aimed at a differently cut loop. The identification is textbook dither-based system identification. The high-gain law is adaptive pole placement with the partner as plant.
- A critic can fairly say "indirect adaptive control of a dyad toward marginal stability".
- I claim no new primitive. The candidate's value is that the specific cut of the loop gives the anti-copy and anti-dial properties as theorems, and makes a new, testable prediction about humans (P1 below).

## 3. Answers to the shared risks

**(a) Collapse into a delayed copy.**
- Three independent reasons:
  - P-b: a full-rank shifted identity is not a fixed point for r ≪ m. The coupling is confined to ≤ 2r input–output directions.
  - P-a: the more responsive the partner (a mirroring partner has large b), the **smaller** the agent's coupling. Mutual mirroring is self-limiting, the opposite of DEP's synchronisation.
  - Coupling exists only through B̂. A recording, a shadow, or a speaker who is not attending to the agent gives B̂ → 0, so K decays under the leak. The REACT "Mirror" policy is a shadow of the speaker; this mechanism would not maintain it.
- Mimicry of particular channels (a smile answered by a smile) can still appear when the partner's responsiveness concentrates there. That is a channel-specific consequence, not a default.

**(b) One hidden dial from flat to rigid imitation.**
- The scalars are η/λ, σ_ξ, r and the time constants.
- Sweeping η/λ runs the agent from flat to **critical reciprocity**. That endpoint is bounded and set by the partner (k* → (1−a)(1−p)/b per mode), not the copy.
- σ_ξ changes only the variance of B̂, not its expectation.
- r bounds the rank. Its endpoint at r = m is the only route toward full-rank coupling, and even then the target is the margin, not the identity.
- So no scalar's sweep passes through imitation.
- The partner's share of expression, Var(K X)/Var(y), is a function of (B̂_t, Λ̂_p, Λ_a). It varies by channel, by lag and over time within one session with one hyperparameter setting. This is checked directly in P2.

**(c) A lookup table of past faces.**
- No face is stored.
- The memory is the operators: K (rank ≤ 2r), B̂ (rank r_B), K̄_p per partner. That is a few thousand numbers, refit online from about 20 s of interaction.
- The self-model is trained on the agent's own face only.
- Nearest Neighbour stays a baseline, not a component.

**Tension with D66, for the critics.** D66 excludes the agent's expression from every learned map. Here, B̂ uses the agent's innate micro-motion ξ, not its learned expression. (★) contains no term in the partner's expression magnitude, so it has no gradient toward drawing engagement. Whether that is enough is for the critics to check.

## 4. Emotion as structure; reading the other; face, voice, Claude

**Structure, no labels.** At each moment the dyad has r regulated joint modes. Each mode is described by:
- |μ_j|, its distance from the edge;
- arg μ_j, its rhythm (nods, back-channel cycles, laughter bursts);
- its AU and voice loadings;
- its agent/partner participation π_j = ‖v_j^a‖²/‖v_j‖²;
- the phase lead between the two blocks.

Hypothesis, stated as such and not built in: recurring constellations of these behave like what we call emotions. Examples:
- a near-critical, low-frequency mode with π ≈ ½ on CheekRaiser and LipCornerPuller (shared amusement);
- modes that stay subcritical while B̂ collapses, with π → 1, so the agent carries the mode alone (bids, then withdrawal: the still-face sequence; De Jaegher's breakdown and repair).

Valence- and arousal-like quantities are only read out for analysis, for example:
- the trend of Σ(1−|μ_j|)², repair versus breakdown;
- Σ|μ_j|, loop energy.

Nothing below Claude has a named emotion variable. Homeostatic body variables, if kept, enter e_t as inputs to h; they are not the core.

**Reading the other through its own body.**
- The partner is perceived in the agent's own motor coordinates.
- The partner is known as B̂: what the agent's own face does to them, probed by its own micro-motion.
- The partner's autonomous dynamics are modelled first with the agent's own body model.
- To know the partner is to know the round trip one makes through them.
- Liveness (partner versus recording) is decided by the loop, not by a classifier, as in perceptual crossing.

**Face and voice.**
- y drives the AU face and the voice parameters directly.
- The agent's own speech (on/off, timing, prosody) is part of y, so B̂ also measures how its speaking moves the partner's face.
- Turn-taking rhythms appear as complex joint modes.

**Claude** never sees numbers or state words. The state changes what reaches Claude and when:
- **Cue selection:** the partner's recent transcript segments that coincide with peaks in the partner-side loading of the near-critical modes go into the context, in order. Others drop out. This changes what Claude talks about.
- **Episodic retrieval:** past conversation excerpts are keyed by similarity of K̄ and of the mode constellation, which changes what Claude recalls.
- **Timing and length:**
  - reply onset is set by the phase of the vocal joint mode;
  - the length budget is short when π → 1 (the agent carries the loop alone: brief repair bids);
  - the length budget is longer when the modes are shared.
- Claude's words become acts in y. Their effect on the partner returns through B̂.

## 5. Falsifiable test on Seamless Interaction features only

**Data.** V00 only (FAUValue v1, is_valid, VAD, transcript word times):
- naturalistic pairs, familiar and stranger;
- improvised repeat actor pairs (195 pairs).

Setup:
- Person-level splits from the shared harness. A pilot of 300 interactions (< 1 GB), downloaded only after Q11 is resolved.
- 10 Hz for operator estimation; baselines scored at 30 Hz.
- Invalid frames masked.
- Column order checked first (the LowerLipDepressor = LipsParts duplicate is dropped).

**Honest limit: a recording is a shadow.** Replayed partners never respond to the agent's ξ, so a free-running agent's K must decay. This is reported as **Part B** (measured: K and the partner share fall to the leak floor within about 3τ_B; a ridge or mirror listener does not).

Scoring uses **Part A**:
- The agent "wears" person X's body: ξ := X's self-model residuals. B̂ is then a Granger-style, not interventional, estimate of Y's real responsiveness to X; this is a stated weakening.
- K is regulated causally.
- X's face is forecast at a 1-s horizon, re-anchored to X's real state each second.

**P1 (human data, no model; the primary falsifier).**
- In 20-s windows (50% overlap), fit reduced-rank (r = 3) cross-lagged regressions in both directions and take the gains g_{X←Y}(w) and g_{Y←X}(w).
- (★) predicts an **inverted U** of g_{X←Y} against g_{Y←X} within sessions:
  - positive slope at the low end;
  - negative quadratic term;
  - the peak inside the observed range;
  - the log product more stable (lower variance) than either log factor.
- Inference: random effects over sessions, against 1,000 segment-shuffle and 1,000 data-sliding surrogates (E3 rules), which also absorb shared-window estimation artefacts.
- **Falsified if** the relation is monotone increasing (mutual contagion, the picture behind every one-dial design), or flat, or the product is no more stable than its factors beyond surrogates.
- Secondary: familiar pairs sit closer to joint criticality (higher fitted |μ_1|) than strangers.

**P2 (against the published simple baselines).** Baselines on listening segments (VAD: Y speaks, X silent) and on all frames:
- Median, Random, Nearest Neighbour, Mirror and Delayed Mirror (15 frames), as in REACT;
- Persistence, required because Part A re-anchors;
- a **scalar-dial family**: the same self-model plus a full-rank ridge partner map, swept over 20 λ values.

Metrics:
- per-AU CCC and L1 against the real X;
- FRVar, FRDiv (10 samples via ξ) and FRSyn (TLCC peak offset to Y, ±2 s), as in the REACT metric code, on AU series;
- copy index max_{δ∈[0,1 s]} corr(ŷ_t, x_{t−δ});
- per-AU partner-share profile (R² on partner lags).

Pass requires all three:
1. CCC ≥ best simple baseline (Mirror and Delayed Mirror included), with paired bootstrap over persons.
2. Copy index inside the human per-session 95% interval, where Mirror and Delayed Mirror fall outside.
3. At matched CCC, the per-AU partner-share profile correlates with the human profile (Spearman) better than **every** λ of the dial family, and its within-session variation over windows matches the human variation better.

**Falsified if** any single λ of the dial family matches the mechanism on criterion 3: then the partner share is a one-dial phenomenon after all.

**P3 (rank).** The validation-selected rank of human cross-person operators is ≤ 2r = 6, far below 24. A human operator near full rank (copy-like) contradicts P-b's fit to people.

**P4 (carry-over, repeat actor pairs).** Sessions are held out one at a time; session order is undocumented, so this is not chronological. Start K at:
- K̄ from the same pair's other sessions;
- K̄ from X with other partners;
- zero.

The same-pair start must give lower L1 in the first 30 s. **Falsified if** it is not better than the same-person start.

**Lesions, each predicted from (★):**
- λ → 0: unbounded K, oscillation, copy index rises.
- B̂ := I: compensation disappears, mirroring returns.
- r = m: P-b lost; the dial family is approached.

**Budget.** CPU and numpy. Eigen-decomposition of a 500 × 500 matrix at 1 Hz; under 1 GB of disk; under 4 GB of RAM.
