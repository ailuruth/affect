# Known failures of listener-reaction, dyadic face and conversational-avatar systems

Written 2026-11-25 for operator note Q15 (research/questions.md). It collects failures that papers, challenge reports, surveys and user studies have documented. Nothing here was re-measured. Each row cites the source note in research/, and every number is quoted as that source reports it. Where a note says a part of a source was not read, nothing from that part is used.

Read with the evaluation rule in Q15: the right-hand column states what the core must do. It does not say how well a face should match human video. Surface scores appear here only as evidence that the published criteria themselves fail.

## How the rows hang together

The published systems sit at one of two ends of a single dial. At one end they copy the partner (row 1), which the metrics reward and humans notice when it is exact (row 8). At the other end they produce an average face (row 2), which distance metrics also reward. Between the two, nothing in these systems carries a state that belongs to the agent: no emotion of its own (row 6), no memory of the partner (rows 4, 5), no tie between its words and its face (row 7). This is the flat-to-copy dial that Q15 names in the sketches. The documented failures say the dial is a property of the formulation (map partner input to face output), not of any one architecture. In the REACT tables, the diffusion (PerFRDiff), graph-network (REGNN) and transformer-VAE (Trans-VAE) baselines all land on it, at one end or the other.

## Not covered, or not read

- **BabyX (Sagar et al.).** Still unread. The CACM 2016 paper returns 403 (babyx-autonomous-facial-animation.md), and the 2023 J. Royal Society of NZ paper on BabyX turn-taking (doi:10.1080/03036758.2022.2098781) also returned 403 here. The brief's description of its failures (no conceptual layer, no long-term living, ablations not run) remains the operator's statement, unverified.
- **Huang, Morency & Gratch 2011, "Virtual Rapport 2.0".** Every copy I tried was blocked (DTIC, USC ICT). Not used.
- **Dyadic talking-and-listening head papers with role switching** (INFP, DualTalk) and dedicated talking-head surveys: not read in this pass.
- **No dedicated survey of listening-head or facial reaction generation was found.** The Sookha et al. survey is the closest.
- **Uncanny-valley evidence** is one study (Tinwell 2011) of speaking faces, not of listening or interaction.
- **The "human dial" question in Q15** (does a person's reaction to a familiar partner only scale, or change shape?) is not addressed here.
