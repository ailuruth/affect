# Gu, Dao, Ermon, Rudra & Ré 2020 (HiPPO) and Gu, Goel & Ré 2021 (S4): optimal polynomial projection memory and structured state spaces

Citations:
- A. Gu, T. Dao, S. Ermon, A. Rudra, C. Ré. "HiPPO: Recurrent Memory with Optimal Polynomial Projections." NeurIPS 2020. arXiv:2008.07669v2.
- A. Gu, K. Goel, C. Ré. "Efficiently Modeling Long Sequences with Structured State Spaces." ICLR 2022. arXiv:2111.00396v3 (5 Aug 2022).

Source read:

- **S4:** abstract, sections 1-5 and Appendix A in full. Not read: Appendices B-D (the LSSL instability proofs, the NPLR derivations, the Cauchy-kernel algorithms, experiment details).
- **A discrepancy in S4 v3:** its introduction reports Path-X at "88% accuracy", while its Table 4 shows 96.35. The table was evidently updated without the text, and the paper tells readers to see Appendix D.5 before citing the table.

## The failure or bottleneck it answered

- **HiPPO.** The bottleneck is a memory with bounded size that must summarize the whole history online. The paper names three failures of earlier work:
  - recurrent nets have "a limited memory horizon";
  - "existing methods generally require priors on the sequence length or timescale and are ineffective outside this range";
  - most lack gradient guarantees.

  Measured examples:
  - Online reconstruction of a 10⁶-step signal with 256 units: LSTM MSE 0.25 ("fails with even sequences of length 1000"), LMU 0.05, HiPPO-LegS 0.02.
  - Under a test-time change of sampling rate on Character Trajectories, every baseline collapsed while LegS held (100 Hz → 200 Hz: LSTM 31.9%, GRU 25.4%, GRU-D 23.1%, ODE-RNN 41.8%, NCDE 44.7%, LMU 6.0%, LegS 88.8%).
  - The sliding-window variant depends on its window: LegT reached 98.0% pMNIST at θ = 200 but 91.75% at θ = 20.
  - Random A and B matrices gave 69.93%, "to confirm that the precise derived dynamics are important."
- **S4.** The bottleneck is computational. The LSSL, a deep state-space model with the HiPPO matrix, needed "O(N²L) operations and O(NL) space". Its proposed fast algorithm was "numerically unstable": the HiPPO matrix is "highly non-normal", and diagonalizing it needs entries up to about 2^{4N/3} (Lemma 3.2). On LRA Path-X (length 16,384) all earlier models scored at chance (50%).

## The new computation, in one equation

c(t) = argmin_{g ∈ P_N} ‖ f_{≤t} − g ‖_{L²(μ⁽ᵗ⁾)} ⇒ dc/dt = A(t) c(t) + B(t) f(t); for LegS, μ⁽ᵗ⁾ = (1/t)·𝟙[0,t] and dc/dt = −(1/t)A c + (1/t)B f

- **HiPPO.** The memory state is *defined* as the coefficients of the best degree-N polynomial approximation of the entire past, under a measure μ⁽ᵗ⁾ that says how much each past moment counts. Differentiating the projection in t gives a linear ODE in the coefficients. The memory update is derived from the definition, not learned or guessed. The scaled measure (LegS) weights all of [0, t] uniformly. This makes the operator timescale-equivariant (Prop. 3: "if h(t) = f(αt), then hippo(h)(t) = hippo(f)(αt)") with gradients Θ(1/t₁) (Prop. 5).
- **S4.** S4 computes the same kind of operator. It writes the A matrix as normal plus low-rank (A = VΛV* − PQᵀ), evaluates the convolution kernel K = (CB̄, CĀB̄, …, CĀ^{L−1}B̄) through its generating function at roots of unity, applies the Woodbury identity, and reduces the result to a Cauchy kernel. The cost is Õ(N + L).

## New computation or recombination?

Parts:
1. Linear time-invariant state-space models x′ = Ax + Bu, y = Cx + Du, from control theory.
2. Orthogonal polynomials and online sliding transforms: sliding DFT, DCT, Hadamard, Chebyshev moments (HiPPO App. A.1).
3. The Legendre Memory Unit (Voelker et al. 2019), which HiPPO-LegT recovers exactly: "Equation (1) proves the LMU update."
4. The Fourier Recurrent Unit, also recovered as a special case.
5. Bilinear and zero-order-hold discretization; the Woodbury identity (1950); Cauchy kernels and fast multipole methods; FFT convolution.
6. For S4, the LSSL (Gu et al. 2021), of which S4 is a reparameterization.

**HiPPO is a change of what the state is.** Before HiPPO, a recurrent state was whatever training made it. HiPPO's state is the solution of a stated optimization problem (optimal projection of the history under a measure), and its update is derived. LegS's scaled measure is new by the authors' account: "We are not aware of any previous works addressing this scaled setting". The parts are classical, so by the project's test this is borderline, but it is a change of what is computed rather than of a parameter. HiPPO also explains LSTM and GRU gates as its N = 1 case. With "the discretization step size ∆t … chosen adaptively … this becomes exactly a gated RNN."

**S4 is not a new computation.** It computes the LSSL's operator faster and stably. Its own ablation says so: "random NPLR matrices still do not perform well, which validates that S4's effectiveness primarily comes from the HiPPO initialization, not the NPLR parameterization." With A trained, all initializations reach perfect training accuracy but their validation accuracies differ by "over 15%". S4's contribution is algorithmic (numerical linear algebra), and it made HiPPO's computation usable.

## What it suggests for an affective core

- **Partner influence as a measure, not a gain.** In HiPPO, what the state keeps of a signal is fixed by a *measure over the past*: a function, not a scalar. One option is that the agent's state keeps an optimal compression of the partner's (or the dyad's) history under a measure that the agent's own state selects. The partner's share is then a vector of projection coefficients, and how far back the partner counts is a shape, not a level. A measure tilted by the agent's need would let the same partner history count differently in different states, without any single gain.
- **No timescale dial.** LegS shows that a memory can have no timescale parameter at all. The step-size and forget-bias dials are removed by construction (Prop. 3), not by tuning. That is the kind of result the project needs for the partner gain: a structural property that makes the dial absent, not set.
- **The harness question in this vocabulary.**
  - An LTI state-space filter y = K ∗ u has a **fixed response profile** K. Scaling C or B scales its gain. This is the "gain on a fixed profile" case in the D75 dial analysis.
  - A profile that changes with the situation needs time-varying dynamics: A(t), as LegS already has (A/t), or Δ and B that depend on the input. Input-dependent selection is in later work (Mamba, not read here).
  - So the class of model decides which outcome the dial analysis can even show.
- **Where it collapses into the dial.**
  - The readout is linear. If C is trained to predict the agent's expression from coefficients that include the partner's history, the partner's share is the norm of C on the partner coefficients, which is one gain.
  - LegS never forgets ("remember all history"). An affect built on it could not recover from an episode without a decaying measure, and a decaying measure brings back a timescale θ or Δt, which is a dial again.
  - Linear superposition makes self and partner contributions additive (y = y_self + y_partner). Any additive partner term can be scaled from zero to dominance.

## Verdict

watch. HiPPO is the clearest case in this set of a memory whose content is *defined* by an optimization problem and derived, not tuned. Its measure is a functional knob, which is the kind of object that could replace a scalar partner gain. S4 is an efficient way to compute it, not a new computation. In LTI form both give only a fixed response profile, so on their own they cannot produce a situation-dependent partner profile.
