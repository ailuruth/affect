## The object, exactly

**Algebra.**
- (V, q) is a quadratic space of dimension n over a field F with char F ≠ 2. The paper's layers use F = R. Its bilinear form is b(v_1, v_2) = ½(q(v_1 + v_2) − q(v_1) − q(v_2)).
- The Clifford algebra Cl(V, q) is the unital associative algebra generated by V with v² = q(v). This gives the fundamental identity v_1v_2 + v_2v_1 = 2b(v_1, v_2) (Lemma D.3).
- With an orthogonal basis e_1, ..., e_n, the products e_A = ∏_{i∈A}^{<} e_i for A ⊆ [n] form a basis, and dim Cl = 2^n.
- Grade m: Cl^(m) = span{e_A : |A| = m}, of dimension C(n, m). Thm 3.1 / D.27: this does not depend on the orthogonal basis chosen.
- The extended form is b̄(x, y) = (β(x)y)^(0), with β the reversal anti-involution, and q̄(x) = b̄(x, x).
- Thm D.26: (e_A) is b̄-orthogonal, and q̄(x) = Σ_A x_A² ∏_{i∈A} q(e_i). Hence Cl = ⊕_{m=0}^n Cl^(m) is an orthogonal sum (Cor D.30), and x = x^(0) + ... + x^(n).
- Parity: Cl^[0] = ⊕_{m even} Cl^(m) and Cl^[1] = ⊕_{m odd} Cl^(m).
- The main involution is α(x) = x^[0] − x^[1].
- Remark D.32: the multivector grading is a grading of vector spaces only, not of algebras (vv = q(v) has grade 0).

**Group and action.**
- Adjusted twisted conjugation, eq. 5: ρ(w)(x) = w x^[0] w⁻¹ + α(w) x^[1] w⁻¹, for w ∈ Cl^×.
- Clifford group, eq. 6: Γ(V, q) = {w ∈ Cl^× ∩ (Cl^[0] ∪ Cl^[1]) : ρ(w)(v) ∈ V for all v ∈ V}. These are the invertible, parity-homogeneous elements that map vectors to vectors.
- For w, x ∈ V with q(w) ≠ 0: ρ(w)(x) = −wxw⁻¹ = x − 2 b(w, x)/b(w, w) · w, which is the reflection in the hyperplane normal to w (eq. 7).

**Layers (section 4), with F = R and channels c.**
- Linear layer, eq. 13: y_{c_out}^(k) = Σ_{c_in} φ_{c_out c_in k} x_{c_in}^(k). This is one real scalar per (output channel, input channel, grade), plus an invariant bias on grade 0 only.
- Geometric product layer, eq. 14: P_φ(x_1, x_2)^(k) = Σ_{i,j} φ_{ijk} (x_1^(i) x_2^(j))^(k).
- Eq. 15: the fully connected version sums these products over input channels, after a linear mixing.
- Normalisation, eq. 16: x^(m) ↦ x^(m) / (σ(a_m)(q̄(x^(m)) − 1) + 1).
- Nonlinearity: ReLU on grade 0, and x^(m) ↦ σ(q̄(x^(m))) x^(m) for m ≥ 1. This is a gate by an invariant scalar.
- Data enter as scalars (grade 0) and vectors (grade 1). Invariant outputs are read from grade 0.

**GATr (skimmed).**
- GATr uses the projective algebra G_{3,0,1} (e_0² = 0, e_i² = 1), with 16-dimensional multivectors. It embeds planes as vectors, lines as bivectors and points as trivectors (their Table 1).
- It acts by the sandwich product ρ_u(x) = u x u⁻¹ (u even) or u x̂ u⁻¹ (u odd), for u ∈ Pin(3, 0, 1), the double cover of E(3).
- Equivariant linear maps (their Prop. 1, proof not read): φ(x) = Σ_{k=0}^{d+1} w_k ⟨x⟩_k + Σ_{k=0}^{d} v_k e_0 ⟨x⟩_k.
- Bilinears: the geometric product and an "EquiJoin" z_0123 (x* ∧ y*)*.
- Nonlinearity: GatedGELU(x) = GELU(x_1) x, with x_1 the scalar component.
- LayerNorm: x / sqrt(E_c ⟨x, x⟩).
- Attention, their eq. 5: Attention(q, k, v)_{i'c'} = Σ_i Softmax_i( Σ_c ⟨q_{i'c}, k_{ic}⟩ / sqrt(8 n_c) ) v_{ic'}. Here ⟨·,·⟩ is the invariant inner product, which is the R^8 dot product on the 8 components without e_0.
- Symmetry breaking (for example gravity) is supplied as extra inputs, "similar to how position embeddings break permutation equivariance". Rotary position embeddings go in auxiliary scalar channels.

## The key property and its proof, step by step

**Theorem (Thm 3.2 with Cor 3.3, 3.4; appendix Lemma E.8, Thm E.10, Thm E.16, Cor E.18, Thm E.29).**
- Hypotheses: (V, q) is finite-dimensional with char F ≠ 2 (degenerate q allowed), w ∈ Γ(V, q), x, x_1, x_2 ∈ Cl(V, q), c ∈ F.
- Conclusions:
  - (i) ρ(w) is linear and multiplicative, ρ(w)(x_1x_2) = ρ(w)(x_1) ρ(w)(x_2), with ρ(w)(c) = c;
  - (ii) ρ(w_2) ∘ ρ(w_1) = ρ(w_2w_1);
  - (iii) ρ(w)(x^(m)) = (ρ(w)x)^(m) for every grade m;
  - (iv) q̄(ρ(w)x) = q̄(x);
  - (v) for every polynomial F ∈ F[T_1..T_ℓ] and every grade k, ρ(w)(F(x_1..x_ℓ)^(k)) = F(ρ(w)x_1, ..., ρ(w)x_ℓ)^(k).
- Further, when q is non-degenerate, Γ/F^× ≅ O(V, q) (Thm 3.5). So (v) is O(V, q)-equivariance, with O acting on every multivector by acting on each vector factor (eq. 12 / 533).

Proof as numbered steps:
1. **Why the twist (Motivation E.1).**
   - The reflection r_w(v) = −wvw⁻¹ follows from the fundamental identity (eq. 339-343).
   - The naive extension ρ_1(w)x = α(w)xw⁻¹ is not multiplicative. For odd w, ρ_1(w)(v_1v_2) = −ρ_1(w)(v_1)ρ_1(w)(v_2) (eq. 350-353).
   - The fix is to twist by parity: ρ(w) = ρ_0(w) ∘ α^w, with ρ_0(w)x = wxw⁻¹ and α^w(x) = x^[0] + η(w)x^[1], where η(w) = w⁻¹α(w).
2. **Algebra automorphism (Lemma E.8).**
   - If w is parity-homogeneous, then η(w) = (−1)^{prt(w)} ∈ {±1}, and α^w is either id or α. Both are algebra automorphisms that preserve parity.
   - ρ_0(w) is always an algebra automorphism, and it preserves parity because prt(w) = prt(w⁻¹) and Cl is Z/2-graded.
   - Their composition is therefore an algebra automorphism, with inverse ρ(w⁻¹).
   - This gives (i).
3. **Homomorphism (Thm E.10).**
   - Expand ρ(w_2)ρ(w_1)x = w_2 α^{prt(w_2)}(w_1 α^{prt(w_1)}(x) w_1⁻¹) w_2⁻¹.
   - Use α^{prt(w_2)}(w_1) = (−1)^{prt(w_1)prt(w_2)} w_1. The two signs cancel, and α^{p}α^{p'} = α^{p+p'}.
   - This gives (ρ(w_2w_1))(x), which is (ii).
4. **Grade preservation (Thm E.16).**
   - Let b_i = ρ(w)(e_i). These lie in V by the definition of Γ.
   - Apply ρ(w) to 0 = e_ie_j + e_je_i (i ≠ j) and use multiplicativity: 0 = b_ib_j + b_jb_i = 2b(b_i, b_j). So (b_i) is an orthogonal system.
   - ρ(w⁻¹) shows linear independence, so (b_i) is an orthogonal basis.
   - Then ρ(w)(e_A) = b_A. By basis-independence of the grading (D.27), b_A ∈ Cl^(|A|).
   - Linearity extends this to all x. This gives (iii).
5. **Basis independence of grades (D.27; the simpler D.37 for char 0).**
   - Define Cl^(m) = span of antisymmetrised products [v_1; ...; v_m] of vectors. This definition uses no basis.
   - Expanding each v_k in any orthogonal basis kills the terms with repeated indices, so [v_1; ...; v_m] ∈ span{e_A : |A| = m}.
   - Conversely, e_A = [e_{j1}; ...; e_{jm}].
   - (The char ≠ 2 proof in D.27 does the same by induction, using b(b_j1, b_j2) = 0 to cancel the repeated-index terms. I read the m = 2 case and the setup of the induction.)
6. **Polynomials (Cor E.18).**
   - A polynomial is built from sums, products and scalar constants. ρ(w) preserves each: additivity, multiplicativity, and ρ(w)(c) = c (c ∈ F lies in grade 0, which is even).
   - Induction on the expression tree gives equivariance of F.
   - Step 4 lets the grade projection commute with ρ(w). This gives (v).
   - The paper also allows coefficients from the exterior algebra of the radical, ∧(R) (Cor E.24), because ρ(w) fixes ∧(R) pointwise (Lemma E.23).
7. **Orthogonality (Thm E.29).** Write x = Σ_A x_A e_A, so ρ(w)x = Σ_A x_A b_A. Then q̄(ρ(w)x) = Σ x_A² ∏ q(b_i) = Σ x_A² ∏ q(e_i) = q̄(x), using q(b_i) = ρ(w)(e_i²) = q(e_i). This gives (iv).
8. **Link to O(V, q) (Thm E.25, Cor E.26).**
   - ρ(w)|_V preserves b by the computation in step 4 and fixes the radical R pointwise, so ρ(w)|_V ∈ O_R(V, q).
   - Surjectivity: write Φ = (O, 0; M, I). Decompose O into reflections by Cartan-Dieudonné, which needs the non-degenerate part. Realise each elementary shear M_{ij} by γ = 1 + c e_i e_{m+j} (Example E.20).
   - The kernel is the invertible, parity-homogeneous part of ∧(R) (Cor E.22).
   - Short exact sequence: 1 → ∧^{[×]}(R) → Γ → O_R(V, q) → 1. For non-degenerate q, R = 0, and this is Γ/F^× ≅ O(V, q).
9. **Layers are equivariant (section 4).**
   - Eq. 13 is a degree-1 polynomial with grade projections.
   - Eq. 14-15 are grade-projected degree-2 polynomials.
   - Eq. 16 and the gates multiply by functions of q̄(x^(m)), which is invariant by step 7.
   - So every layer commutes with ρ(w), and so does their composition.

Where it is loose or incomplete:
- **A typo in Thm E.29.** Eq. 516-522 write x_A where Thm D.26 (eq. 175) has x_A². The argument is unaffected.
- **No completeness result.** The paper proves that eq. 13 and the polynomial layers are equivariant. It does not prove that eq. 13 is the most general equivariant linear map. The abstract's "several non-equivalent subrepresentations" is asserted; I did not find a proof of irreducibility or non-equivalence in the parts read. GATr's Prop. 1 claims the full characterisation for Pin(d, 0, 1), but its proof is in an appendix I did not read.
- **No universal approximation.** Neither paper proves universality. GATr says so: "GATr is not yet shown to be a universal approximator".
- **Experiments.** All results are empirical, on synthetic volumes, O(5) regression, n-body (MSE 0.0039 ± 0.0001 against SEGNN's 0.0043) and top tagging (matching LorentzNet).
- **GATr attention.** Its equivariance is immediate from the invariance of ⟨·,·⟩ and the equivariance of the value maps. GATr reports trying geometric-product attention and finding "worse performance in practice".

## What it would mean for a dyadic affective core (my inference)

The theorems give equivariance under O(V, q) for whatever V is. The question is what V and the group would be for two agents with per-expression channels. There are two natural mappings, and they fail in different ways.

**Mapping 1: "what" as directions of V.** Let V = R^d with one basis vector per expression (action unit), let x_A, x_B ∈ V be the agent's and the partner's expression vectors, and let G = O(d).

*Claim (my derivation from the paper's facts).* Any CGENN network whose multivector inputs are only x_A and x_B (plus any number of scalar inputs, such as partner-history scalars) outputs a grade-1 value of the form

  y = φ_A · x_A + φ_B · x_B,

with φ_A, φ_B real functions of the invariants (|x_A|², |x_B|², ⟨x_A, x_B⟩ and the scalar inputs).

Proof:
1. Let W = span{x_A, x_B}. Choose an orthogonal basis of W and extend it to one of V. Every product of elements of W lies in span{f_A : A ⊆ basis of W}.
2. By D.27, the grade-1 part of that span is W.
3. The linear layers (eq. 13) multiply each grade by scalars. The products (eq. 14) stay in the subalgebra Cl(W). The gates and norms multiply by scalars. Polynomial constants are in F, so the pseudoscalar is never available as a constant.
4. So every hidden multivector lies in Cl(W), and every grade-1 output lies in W.
5. If x_A and x_B are independent, the coefficients are unique, and equivariance forces them to be invariant.

The same holds for GATr's linear maps and attention, since its equivariant linear maps are grade-wise scalars, apart from the extra e_0 terms. I did not check its dual-based join, which can leave Cl(W).

*Consequence.* The partner's influence on the agent's face is φ_B × (a copy of the whole partner vector). That is **exactly the excluded mechanism**: a single scalar gain from flat (φ_B = 0) to copy (φ_B = 1). The gain may depend on state and history and may go negative (anti-copy of the whole face).

The symmetry forbids:
- per-expression gating (return the smile but not the disgust);
- specific off-diagonal coupling (answer anger with a smile);
- different signs for different expressions.

Each of these needs a preferred axis, and O(d) has none. The reflection ρ(x_A)(x_B) = x_B − 2⟨x_A, x_B⟩/|x_A|² · x_A is signed and state-dependent, but it still lies in span{x_A, x_B}.

O(d) is also not a symmetry of faces: action units are not interchangeable by rotation. Mapping 1 is therefore both wrong and collapsing.

**Mapping 2: "whose" as directions of V, "what" as channels.** Let V = R² = span{e_s, e_o} (self, other). Each expression channel c carries a multivector x_c ∈ Cl(R²) with four components: a scalar s_c, a_c e_s (self intensity), b_c e_o (partner intensity), and β_c e_s e_o (bivector).

- **Linear layers (eq. 13)** give y_c^(1) = Σ_d φ_{cd1} x_d^(1). This is a full signed, off-diagonal per-expression matrix Φ¹, which is what (a) asks for. But Φ¹ is applied identically to the self and the other components. The self component of the output depends only on the self components of the inputs, so there is no cross-agent influence at all.
- **The geometric product supplies the cross terms.**
  - For vectors: x_c x_d = ⟨x_c, x_d⟩ + x_c ∧ x_d, with ⟨x_c, x_d⟩ = a_c a_d + b_c b_d (self-self plus other-other matching) and x_c ∧ x_d = (a_c b_d − b_c a_d) e_s e_o.
  - The bivector is a **signed, per-expression-pair, antisymmetric self×other coupling**: my c against your d, minus your c against my d. It flips sign under the role swap e_s ↔ e_o, so it is the component that carries who-leads asymmetry.
  - Multiplying the bivector back into a vector, (β_c e_s e_o)(a_d e_s + b_d e_o) = β_c(b_d e_s − a_d e_o), routes the partner's expression d into the agent's channel c with a sign, gated by β_c. This is a bilinear, signed, off-diagonal and state-gated coupling. It is the closest thing in these papers to (a).
- **But the symmetry destroys (b).** O(2) rotations of the whose-plane mix self and other, and an O(2)-equivariant network cannot tell e_s from e_o.
- **Breaking it does not help.** Break the symmetry GATr-style by feeding e_s as a reference input. The residual group is the stabiliser of e_s, {id, e_o ↦ −e_o}. Equivariance under it makes the agent's self output an even function of the partner's components jointly: f(a, −b) = f(a, b). So the partner can influence the agent only through even terms such as b_c b_d and |b|², and **linear partner-to-self influence is forbidden**. With non-negative action-unit intensities this costs little in practice, since b ↦ b² is invertible on b ≥ 0. Mathematically, though, it means the residual symmetry encodes nothing about the dyad.
- **Dropping the group entirely** leaves Cl(R²) as a structured bilinear parameterisation. Grade 0 is agent-neutral, grade 1 is whose, the bivector is the relation, and the products are fixed. Then none of Ruhe's theorems is doing any work. Any property such as no collapse to copy must be proved separately.

**Where the papers do apply.** Head pose and gaze are genuinely SE(3) quantities. Three consequences follow:
- Expressing the partner's head pose and gaze in the agent's body frame is a sandwich product by the versor of the relative pose T_{A←B} (GATr's projective algebra). That is a literal, equivariant version of (d) for the geometric channels.
- Invariance to the room frame (where the dyad stands) is a real symmetry.
- Under E(3), all action-unit and voice channels are grade-0 scalars. The group says nothing about how they couple. So the per-expression coupling problem (a) is untouched by equivariance.

**What invariance forbids, precisely.**
- Under O(d) on expression space: everything except "invariant-scalar gain × copy + invariant-scalar gain × self".
- Under O(2) on the whose-plane: any distinction between self and other.
- Under its stabiliser: linear partner-to-self terms.
- In GATr attention on a 2-token dyad: the weights are softmax-normalised, so the output is α v_B + (1 − α) v_A with α ∈ [0, 1], computed from invariant inner products. Shared value maps make this a convex mix: scalar gain from flat to copy, and diffusion to consensus if iterated with shared weights. Sign flips can come only from the value maps, and those are grade-wise scalars, so they apply to the whole grade at once.

**Theorem a design would need.** For a bilinear dyad layer h_A^(c) = Σ_{d,e} T_{cde} ⟨u_d(m), x_B^(e)⟩ (Clifford-structured or not, where m is the partner-history state), show that:
- (i) the induced Jacobian ∂h_A/∂x_B is not of the form φ(m)·M_0 for one fixed M_0;
- (ii) it can be singular on a chosen subset of expressions while being non-zero elsewhere (gating);
- (iii) the closed-loop dyad does not contract x_A − M x_B to 0.

The Clifford theorems would supply none of (i)-(iii). They would only guarantee room-frame equivariance of the geometric channels.

## Verdict

reject as the core mechanism; adopt for geometric channels only. Clifford-group equivariance is a clean, fully proved result: grade-preserving, product-preserving, so every grade-projected polynomial is O(V, q)-equivariant. But applied to expression space it provably reduces partner influence to an invariant scalar gain on a copy. Applied to the self/other plane it erases "whose". The bivector self∧other is a nice signed relational term, but it carries no theorem once the group is dropped. Use GATr-style versors only to bring the partner's head pose and gaze into the agent's body frame.
