# Man & Damasio 2019: Homeostasis and soft robotics in the design of feeling machines

Source status: **unread**. Nature Machine Intelligence 1:446-452, doi:10.1038/s42256-019-0103-7. The article is paywalled. Unpaywall reports `is_oa: false` with no repository copy, and no arXiv or author copy was found. Only the abstract and figure captions on the nature.com article page were read.

## Claims (abstract only)
- Intelligent agents should hold their own meta-goal of self-preservation, as organisms do through homeostasis.
- Machines that implement something like homeostasis "might also acquire a source of motivation and a new means to evaluate behaviour, akin to that of feelings".
- The authors propose a class of machines, drawing on soft robotics and multisensory abstraction, that would (1) exhibit equivalents to feeling, (2) improve functionality across environments, and (3) serve as a platform for studying consciousness, intelligence and feeling.
- Figure captions: Fig. 1 is Ashby's 1954 homeostat; Fig. 2 shows artificial and natural soft materials.

## Verdict: watch (unread)
I cannot judge it against the Principles without the text. The abstract is consistent with Principles 2 and 6, since it treats homeostasis as the source of value. It is the theory source that Yoshida & Man cite for "vulnerable body", but their operational definition, read in their own papers, is the usable one: vulnerability is "the circular causality by which homeostatic states can affect the agent's ability to regulate those states". Get the full text through a library or from the authors before citing anything beyond the abstract.
