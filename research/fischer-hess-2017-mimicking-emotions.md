# Fischer & Hess 2017, "Mimicking emotions" (Current Opinion in Psychology 17:151-155, doi 10.1016/j.copsyc.2017.07.008)

Source read: full article PDF (UvA repository, final published version), all sections and the annotated reference list. Short review; the studies are described as the authors describe them.

## Claims
- Restates the Contextual Model of Emotional Mimicry: people mimic only with a minimal potential for affiliation, when they share the perspective that produced the emotion; and they mimic the meaning of the movement, not the movement. "Emotional mimicry thus is goal driven, rather than stimulus driven. While mimicry is automatic, it only occurs in the presence of an affiliation goal."
- The reverse also holds: people "(unconsciously) refrain from mimicking others' emotions if we want to keep emotional distance". Congruent responses to non-affiliative signals (disgust) are a "reverse function" that increases social distance.
- Smiles and sadness, which signal affiliation, should be mimicked preferentially; anger and disgust should not.
- Mimicry is shaped by learned associations and reward value of faces (top-down, grounded in prior interaction).

## Evidence (as reported)
- Under cognitive load, smiles of people described as likeable were mimicked, but not smiles of people described negatively or neutrally; without load, neutral others' smiles were mimicked too.
- Social exclusion increases mimicry.
- Mauersberger et al. 2015 (diary study after a lab EMG task): mimicry of sadness related to social competence; congruent responses to disgust related to social incompetence and predicted negative interactions. (I read that paper's abstract: N = 108, 10-day diaries.)
- Aguado et al. 2013: responses learned to a person's expressions transfer to that person's neutral face. Heerey & Crossley 2013: genuine smiles are more rewarding, and rewarding with genuine smiles increases smile mimicry and speeds learning.
- Aragon et al.: after unfair treatment, mu suppression ceased for purely kinematic but not goal-directed hand movements.

## What it implies for the design
- Withholding mimicry is a signal in its own right. An agent that always copies cannot express distance; one that can selectively not couple can.
- The model says the relation value acts before the motor output (automatic, not deliberate), and that it is learned from the history of rewards and expressions with this specific person. That fits a per-person learned state in the fast loop, not a label passed from Claude.
- Cognitive load narrows mimicry to people already liked: under load, the relation prior dominates. An agent's coupling should depend on the confidence of its partner model, not only its sign.

## Verdict: adopt (as the theory update; check primaries before relying on any single effect)
