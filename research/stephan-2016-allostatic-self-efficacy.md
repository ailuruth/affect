## What the paper does
- **A three-layer hierarchical Bayesian account** of interoception, regulation and metacognition, proposed to explain fatigue followed by depression under chronic bodily disturbance. It is a theory paper: one toy simulation, no data fitted.
- **Layer 1: homeostatic reflex as a clamped prior ("Bayesian reflex").** (Equations reconstructed from text.)
  - Perception: a Gaussian belief (μ_t, π_t) over a bodily state x, with input y = g(x) + noise of precision π_data, updated by a precision-weighted prediction error (the HGF form: update ∝ π_data/π_posterior × (y − g(μ_t))).
  - Reflex: **clamp the prior** at the setpoint (μ_prior, π_prior) and ignore the data for belief updating; define action as the gradient of the log evidence under the homeostatic model with respect to x, so x descends interoceptive surprise S at a rate set by a time constant λ. Action vigour scales with the prediction error **and with the prior precision**; it vanishes at the setpoint.
  - "Dyshomeostasis" is defined formally as chronically elevated interoceptive surprise (high entropy of viscerosensory channels), equivalently low evidence for the body model.
- **Layer 2: allostasis** is a higher level changing the reflex's prior over time, ϕ_i(t): either its **mean** (a predicted perturbation of known sign: shift the setpoint first) or its **precision** (a perturbation of unknown sign: tighten the range, so the same deviation produces larger surprise and faster correction). Simulation (Fig. 6): perturbations of 1.5; setpoint shifted 1 → 2 before an expected push; precision raised 1 → 4 before an unsigned one gives a visibly faster correction, at the cost of more noise-driven action (sensory noise SD 0.25). "A modulation of precision of top-down predictions is sufficient to switch from learning to acting."
- **Layer 3: metacognition, "allostatic self-efficacy".** A layer that monitors the performance of layers 1–2 and holds a belief about one's capacity to regulate the body.
  - **The signal:** if interoceptive surprise is not falling (its gradient is flat or positive) while action is taken, regulation is failing. The authors name this the metacognitive diagnosis; **fatigue** is its first, possibly adaptive, response (save energy, wait, learn by watching others).
  - **Tiredness vs fatigue:** after exertion, rest makes surprise fall (negative gradient), so mastery is experienced; in fatigue rest does not, so the belief cannot recover.
  - **Generalisation:** if dyshomeostasis persists, low self-efficacy spreads to other domains (learned helplessness), giving depression. Protective factors (preserved mastery in other domains, social support) can block the spread.
  - Two forms are sketched without maths: another hierarchical level whose beliefs are predictions for the visceromotor areas; or a monitor of the log evidence of the whole circuit integrated over a window ("free action").
- **Circuit (proposed).** Visceromotor areas (anterior insula, ACC, subgenual, OFC) at the top issue allostatic predictions to hypothalamus, brainstem and spinal effectors and send the same prediction to posterior/mid insula as efference copy; effectors return no prediction error, so allostatic predictions are fulfilled by action, not revised. A metacognitive layer (candidate: anterior prefrontal cortex) sits above.

## The mathematical object
**A hierarchy over each body variable: a reflex that descends interoceptive surprise under a clamped prior; an allostatic level that sets that prior's mean and precision over time; and a metacognitive level whose belief is updated by whether the agent's own regulation is reducing surprise (the sign of dS/dt under action).**

## What it means for the engine
1. **A slow latent defined by self-reference, not by input content.** The self-efficacy belief is fed by the trend of the agent's own surprise under its own actions. That is the cleanest stated way to build a slow affective coordinate that is about the agent (D81's "the state is about the self") rather than about the partner's face. It is the slow-level counterpart of Joffily & Coricelli's valence = −dF/dt and Hesp et al.'s affective charge (both already adopted in the index).
2. **Precision as the allostatic knob.** Tightening a prior's precision both speeds correction and raises surprise for the same deviation. An arousal-like readout falls out as the precision the upper level places on the reflex, not a named variable.
3. **Structure over domains.** "Self-efficacy can be preserved in some domains and lost in others" and "generalises" gives the brief's structure-over-needs requirement a concrete form: one efficacy belief per body variable (or per need and context), with a learned coupling that spreads failure across domains.
4. **Q18 and the partner route.** A partner who helps regulate the agent's body (comfort, a calm voice) makes the agent's surprise fall, which raises efficacy-in-their-presence. That is contingency kept in the state; its expression is bounded by the ceilings (conjecture-five-passes Corollary 1b). But if efficacy also biases what the agent does, it becomes a selection route toward the partner's regulating responses (the dwell-selection hazard of pass 5), and it must be audited for occupation shift.
5. **Hazard of the theory itself.** The model makes helplessness an attractor of the metacognitive layer under persistent surprise. An engine that adopts it inherits a depressive fixed point by design; it needs an exit, which the paper gives only as "protective factors".
6. **Labels.** None: setpoints are body variables, the higher levels are beliefs about them. The theory uses clinical names (fatigue, depression) only as its targets.

## Verdict
- **adopt (the three-level object: clamped-prior reflex, precision/mean allostasis, efficacy from the agent's own surprise trend) as material; background as evidence (theory, no data).** It is hierarchical active inference / the HGF applied to the body; the metacognitive level is described, not formalised.
