# Van Kleef & Côté 2022, "The Social Effects of Emotions" (Annual Review of Psychology 73:629-658, doi 10.1146/annurev-psych-020821-010855)

Source read: the full published review (UvA-DARE open-access copy, pure.uva.nl file annurev_psych_020821_010855.pdf). I read in full: the abstract, introduction, definitions, theory history, the affective-reactions and inferential-processes sections, Key Insights, Challenges, Summary Points and Future Issues. The domain sections (close relationships, groups, customer service, negotiation, leadership) I only scanned for numbers and moderators. It is a narrative review with no meta-analytic effect sizes. Cited studies are reported as the review describes them.

## Claims
- **EASI, updated:**
  - Affective reactions are now split into reciprocal reactions (same emotion), complementary reactions (a different emotion "with matching social-motivational implications": anger → fear, sadness → compassion, disappointment → guilt, pride → envy), and sentiments (liking).
  - The social-relational moderator is now stated as the perceived appropriateness of the expression.
  - The relative impact of inference rises with motivation and ability to process and with perceived appropriateness. The relative impact of affective reactions rises with low processing or perceived inappropriateness.
- **Primitive contagion is in doubt.** Mimicry followed by afferent feedback is "mixed at best":
  - Hess & Blairy 2001: mimicry of anger, disgust, happiness and sadness occurred, and feelings matched for happiness and sadness, but mimicry did not mediate contagion.
  - Afferent feedback has replication problems (Wagenmakers et al. 2016).
  - Contagion happens without faces: by phone (Coyne 1976), through text in negotiations, and on Facebook (Kramer et al. 2014).
  - Contagion depends on the relationship: Lanzetta & Englis 1989 found opposite facial responses under expected competition. Out-groups are mimicked less, and lower-power partners converge toward higher-power partners.
- **Social appraisal and perspective taking** are the proposed non-primitive mechanisms. Observers use the other's expression to appraise the shared situation, and come to feel similarly because they now appraise it similarly. "Direct causal evidence for these mechanisms is scarce."
- **Complementary reactions are gated by dependence and care:**
  - Low-power people felt less compassion at a partner's distress, in self-report and physiology (Van Kleef et al. 2008).
  - Low-power negotiators felt more fear at a high-power counterpart's anger (Lelieveld et al. 2012).
  - Disappointment elicited more guilt from in-group than out-group counterparts (Lelieveld et al. 2013).
  - Sadness elicited compassion only with a collaborative relationship or anticipated future interaction (Sinaceur et al. 2015).
  - The reciprocal and complementary literatures are "mostly independent." When observers feel the same emotion and when they feel a different one is an open question (Future Issue 2).
- **Inferences are drawn about three things:**
  - the expresser: traits, dominance, intentions; de Melo et al. 2014 reverse appraisal maps happiness → goal-conducive, sadness → goal obstruction, anger → obstruction plus other-blame, regret → obstruction plus self-blame;
  - the situation: anger leads observers to construe the situation as less cooperative (Van Doorn et al. 2012); anger teaches norms (Hareli et al. 2013);
  - the observer.
- **Inferences are stronger under ambiguity.** Observers drew stronger inferences from two team members' expressions when the team's future was more ambiguous, and when the expressions were more likely to reflect team processes than dispositions (Homan et al. 2016).
- **Summary point 4:** "There is no systematic relationship between the valence of emotional expressions and the favorability of behavioral responses to those expressions."
- **Functional equivalence across channels.** Face, voice, body, text and symbols have qualitatively similar social effects. The authors call for direct within-study comparisons.
- **Gaps.** Almost all the evidence comes from short laboratory interactions. How the routes interact "between people and over time," when one person's response becomes the other's stimulus, is unknown.

## Evidence
Narrative review. Numbers it gives:
- People are about 9% more accurate at recognising expressions from their own culture (Elfenbein & Ambady 2002).
- In work groups, moods converged for eight mood categories, with high-arousal moods (cheerfulness, anxiety) spreading more than low-arousal ones (Bartel & Saavedra 2000).
- Lundqvist & Dimberg 1995: facial mimicry for every expression except fear, and matching feeling for every expression except surprise.

The review reports no pooled effect sizes, so every claim rests on single studies of the kind listed in the 2010 note.

## Formal model
None, verbal only.

## What it implies for the design
- **The strongest support for the hypothesis under examination.** The review's conclusion: "Accumulating evidence casts doubt on the process of primitive emotional contagion but aligns with nonprimitive processes such as social appraisal." The same review states that direct causal tests of social appraisal are scarce. The hypothesis is the field's best current account, not an established mechanism.
- **The complementary reactions are the off-diagonal entries.** Anger → fear, sadness → compassion and disappointment → guilt are the off-diagonal entries that mimicry-context-synthesis.md says any partner map must have. Their gating (power, group, future interaction) is the per-expression gating. Under the hypothesis they need no separate map: they are the agent's own appraisal of what the partner's inferred state means for its concerns. Anger aimed at me predicts a costly act toward me, and that is threat to me. Distress in someone whose welfare enters my concerns is loss to me. A complementary reaction appears when the partner's appraisal and mine diverge. A reciprocal one appears when they coincide.
- **The gate that decides reciprocal vs complementary should be the same inference.** "How much the partner's outcome is my outcome" (care, dependence, shared group, future interaction) decides when sadness elicits sadness and when it elicits compassion, and whether it elicits anything at all. If that is learned per partner from interaction history, no global gain sets it.
- **A hidden dial remains in the Bayesian reading.** Homan et al. 2016 ("stronger inferences under greater ambiguity") is the human analogue of precision weighting. The partner's expression moves the agent's belief about the shared situation more when the agent's prior over the situation is broad. In a Bayesian implementation the scalar is the ratio of likelihood precision on the partner's expression to prior precision on the situation.
  - Turning that ratio up makes the agent's *situation belief* follow the partner's expression more closely.
  - The agent's *face* follows only through its own concern-relative appraisal of that belief. So the end-point of the dial is "believes whatever the partner's face implies about the world", not "copies the partner's face".
  - It collapses into copying only if the agent's concern model is the partner's (identical concerns, identical stakes). The design should make that impossible: the agent's needs are its own body variables.
  - This argument is mine and should be checked by derivation, as the critiques did for the 15 designs.
- **Appropriateness without labels.** It can be read as how well the agent's current situation model predicts the partner's expression. Low appropriateness (an expression the situation does not explain) should move the agent's model of the *partner* (hostile, unreliable, or an unseen cause) rather than its model of the situation. That routes inappropriate anger to retaliation or withdrawal without a second route.
- **Time is the open problem.** The review says no one knows how the routes play out in ongoing, reciprocal interaction, which is the agent's only regime. Any claim that the inferential design reproduces human dyadic dynamics must be tested on live dyadic data (Seamless, CANDOR), not borrowed from these one-shot lab studies.

## Verdict: adopt (as the current state of the field)
This is the most recent and most explicit statement that social appraisal, not mimicry plus feedback, best explains emotional convergence. It introduces complementary reactions as a class the transfer designs cannot produce without off-diagonal entries. It is a narrative review without effect sizes, and it names the absence of dynamic, ongoing-interaction evidence as a gap.
