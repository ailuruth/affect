## Key claims
- **Two processes in marmoset vocal development:** change in usage (twitters and trills move to the visual-contact context) and transformation of immature contact calls (cries, phee-cries, subharmonic phees) into mature phees within the first two months (longer, lower dominant and AM frequency, lower entropy).
- **Growth explains part, not all.** Body weight doubles in about a month; vocal tract and fold growth account for a portion of each acoustic change but none entirely.
- **Contingent, not total, parental calls.** The timing of the cry-to-phee transition varied from ≈10 to 40 days; it correlated with the number of contingent parental responses (calls a few seconds after the infant's), not with the total number of adult calls heard (an exposure account).
- **Closed-loop twin experiment (as reported).** Dizygotic twins, from day 1 to two months, computer-driven playback of parental contact calls at high vs low contingency, randomly assigned: the high-contingency twin produced mature-sounding calls sooner. (The companion summary, not read in the original: spontaneous output was not changed by contingency.)
- **Feedback is necessary for normal outcome.** Offspring reared with limited parental contact still produced infant-like vocal behaviours at over a year.
- **Receiver predisposition.** Parents of both humans and marmosets answer more-adult-like calls more often; the authors suggest vocal learning evolved to reach the sounds that "exploit the receiver predispositions" faster, i.e. the sounds that draw caregiver attention.

## What it means for the engine
1. **The infant's learned output converges on what the partner answers.** The target is set by the receivers' predisposition, and the learning signal is the contingent response. Proposition 1(a), open: in an engine the user's response bias would set the direction of any output learned this way, and "sounds that draw caregiver attention" is the Principle 6 failure stated as an evolutionary account.
2. **Contingency, not exposure, carries the learning.** The twin design is the cleanest causal form of the yoked control: equal exposure, different contingency, different learning. A battery partner for the engine can copy it: two agent copies with the same partner input stream, one contingent on the agent's output and one yoked; any difference in learned output is the approval route (see the extinction-burst probe, goldstein-schwade-bornstein-2009).
3. **Isolation outcome.** Without feedback marmoset output stays infant-like; in the engine the corresponding fact is that an approval-closed output learner gets no partner-shaped form at all (pass 7's dichotomy; Proposition 3 of new-core-search-9-synthesis.md).

## Verdict
- **background (review; reports the causal closed-loop contingency experiment and the receiver-predisposition account).** Secondary for the twin experiment.
