# Fast response times signal social connection in conversation (Templeton et al.)

Emma M. Templeton, Luke J. Chang, Elizabeth A. Reynolds, Marie D. Cone LeBeaumont & Thalia Wheatley, *PNAS* 119(4):e2116915119, 2022, doi:10.1073/pnas.2116915119 (PMC8794835, CC BY-NC-ND).

## What was read
The full PMC text: significance, abstract, the three studies, the self/partner analysis, discussion with limits, and methods. The SI (bin-size robustness, factor loadings, the replication sample) was not read.

## Claims and evidence
- **Design.** Study 1: 66 undergraduates in six 11-person same-gender round-robins, 322 unstructured 10-min conversations, mostly strangers; after each, a survey and a **continuous moment-by-moment connection rating** while rewatching. Response time = start of a turn minus end of the previous turn (negative = overlap); expected mean about 200 ms. Study 2: 22 returned with 3 close friends each (65 conversations). Study 3: 450 online listeners heard ~10-turn clips in which every gap was shortened to 1/5 or lengthened to 2×.
- **Across conversations:** faster mean response time predicted enjoyment (standardised b = −0.35, SE 0.05) and connection (b = −0.28).
- **Within conversations** (20 bins of 30 s): connection rose over the 10 min (b = 0.27); controlling for that, faster bins were more connected (strangers b = −0.03, p = .002; friends b = −0.07). Robust to bin size and to a circular-shift null.
- **Across people:** a person's average response time across 10 partners predicted how connected those partners felt (b = −0.63).
- **Whose speed matters: the partner's.** My connection was predicted by how fast *you* answered me (b = −0.22, p < .001), not by how fast I answered you (b = −0.08, n.s.); the contrast held within conversations for strangers and friends. "When someone responds quickly it signals to their partner that they had been actively listening."
- **Causal, and read by eavesdroppers:** the same clip with gaps at 1/5 length was rated more enjoyable and connected than the original, and the 2× version less enjoyable (condition effect F = 49.4 enjoyment, 28.5 connection). Listeners were never told to attend to timing.
- The authors call sub-250 ms responses an "honest signal" because they are too fast for deliberate control. In conflict, fast overlapping turns may mean the opposite (not tested).

**Limits:** WEIRD students, polite get-to-know-you talk with a known 10-min end; no conflict or distress conversations; the paper cannot say what caused any individual response time.

## What it means for the designs
1. **The agent's latency is the strongest lever on felt connection the user has, and it is the agent's latency, not the user's.** The user's felt connection depends on how quickly the agent answers. Revision 2's R15 (the onset waits for a 1–3 s Claude draft) puts every agent turn at roughly 5–15× the human mode, worse than the 2× manipulation that lowered ratings here. A fast-loop onset (breath, "mm", gaze to the user, brow) within about 250 ms is needed regardless of the draft; the words can follow.
2. **Responsiveness, not initiative, is what this paper measures.** It gives no evidence that speaking first raises connection. What it does supply for the "never speaks first" gap: when the agent does take the floor after a silence, the quality the user will judge is the contingency of its next responses, so an initiative bid that is followed by slow replies would undo itself.
3. **Connection grows over minutes within one conversation** (b = 0.27 across 10 min). The agent's warmth/approach drive (the missing positive affect in revision 2) has a natural input here: fast mutual exchanges are a positive event the body can appraise. A satiable approach variable that rises with the partner's quick contingent replies to the agent, and saturates, would give revision 2 a positive state that does not come from matching the partner's face.
4. **Timing is an affect output that partners read without noticing.** If the agent's latency becomes a state-dependent output, slower agent replies will be read as less connection; tying slower latency to the agent's sadness or low energy is honest and legible, but it must stay within a small range or the agent will seem disengaged exactly when it is concerned.

## Verdict
**adopt (evidence and a measurement):** partner response time is a causal, sufficient, unconsciously read signal of connection; the agent must answer fast from the body. Says nothing directly about initiative or distress.
