## Claims (abstract, verbatim)
- Background: "Imitation has long been assumed to occur from birth [2-4], with influential theories (e.g., [5-7]) placing an innate imitation module at the foundation of social cognition (potentially underpinned by a mirror neuron system [8, 9])." Prior support is "mixed", "potentially because previous investigations have relied heavily on cross-sectional designs with relatively small samples and with limited controls".
- Design: "We presented infants (n = 106) with nine social and two non-social models and scored their responses at 1, 3, 6, and 9 weeks of age."
- Result: "Longitudinal analyses indicated that the infants did not imitate any of the models, as they were just as likely to produce the gestures in response to control models as they were to matching models."
- On earlier positives: "Previous positive findings were replicated in limited cross-sections of the data, but the overall analyses confirmed these findings to be mere artifacts of restricted comparison conditions."
- Conclusion: "Our results undermine the idea of an innate imitation module and suggest that earlier studies reporting neonatal imitation were methodologically limited."

## Evidence
Longitudinal within-subject design, 4 ages, 11 models (9 social, 2 non-social), n = 106. Which gestures, how responses were coded, how many infants were retained per wave and the test statistics were not read. Search-result summaries mention 64 infants in the longitudinal analysis, but that was not read in the paper.

Not read, seen only as search-result titles and snippets: a re-analysis of these data titled "Re-examination of Oostenbroek et al. (2016): evidence for neonatal imitation of tongue protrusion" (Meltzoff, Murray, Simpson et al., Developmental Science 2018), and a reply by Oostenbroek et al. Read both before treating the question as settled either way.

## Verdict: adopt (as the reason not to hard-wire innate mimicry), provisional on reading the full text
For the foundation: the agent's sign map must not depend on an innate copy-what-you-see module. The largest longitudinal test found matching gestures no more likely after a matching model than after control models. That fits the Gergely & Watson route, in which the mapping between one's own states and another's displays is learned from contingent, marked mirroring by the partner, not given. Mimicry in the agent, if any, should be an emergent or learned outcome that is tested, not a built-in reflex. Caveats: only the abstract was read, and the re-analysis dispute (tongue protrusion) is unresolved here. Read the full text and the 2018 exchange before citing exact numbers.
