## The computation, precisely

**The claim the review actually makes, which is weaker than the popular one.** Oxytocin is not a prosocial drive signal; it is a modulator that changes signal-to-noise for social input, and whether the result is prosocial depends on context:

> "oxytocin can enhance the salience of social stimuli and increase signal-to-noise ratios by modulating spiking and synaptic plasticity in the context of circuits and networks."
> "Activating PVN oxytocin neurons in an enriched social environment increased both prosocial and agonistic behaviors depending on context, consistent with the hypothesis that oxytocin is an attentional modulator acting to enhance social salience rather than being inherently prosocial."
> "The oxytocin peptide is not intrinsically social, and so the maternal/social phenotype of oxytocin must instead come from the inputs that activate these cells, that is, the (social) receptive fields of oxytocin neurons."

**Three orders of modulation — the structural taxonomy, and the useful part.**

1. **First-order (direct on principal cells).** In hippocampal CA2, receptor activation "depolarized these cells, which led to spike bursts ... due to M-type K+ channel (KCNQ) closure via Gαq and Gαi G protein coupling through PLC. Thus, brief episodes of oxytocin modulation can **dramatically change the CA2 firing mode** and alter information processing through the hippocampal circuit". The review's own assessment of this mode: "first-order modulation is effective at enhancing all inputs to a neuron or network, thereby increasing sensitivity to any signal but potentially at the cost of discriminating between similar signals with different behavioral significance." *That is a gain, and the authors say it has the defect of a gain.*

2. **Second-order (via local inhibitory interneurons) — the dominant mode.** In CA1 oxytocin does **not** depolarise pyramidal cells; it drives parvalbumin interneurons, and the consequence is two-sided:
   > "First, increased tonic GABA release in the absence of external input suppresses spontaneous firing of CA1 pyramidal neurons, **reducing background noise** in the circuit. Second, this weakens evoked (phasic) disynaptic inhibitory postsynaptic currents (IPSCs) but not excitatory postsynaptic currents (EPSCs) when Schaffer collaterals are stimulated, likely through the depletion of GABAergic vesicle pools from enhanced spontaneous interneuron activity ... leads to a heightened net excitatory response to incoming stimulation, thus **increasing the signal strength**."
   One modulator, two opposite-signed effects on two different components (tonic down, evoked up), giving a change in *ratio* with no single multiplicative factor. "Regulation of synaptic inhibition seems to be a major, if not the most common, mode of oxytocinergic modulation in the brain, although specific effects vary across regions"; "Modulation of inhibitory circuits enables more selective signal enhancement for specific cell types, synapses, and sensory inputs than does the direct excitation of principal cells."
   Region-specific sign differences are documented: in somatosensory but not prefrontal cortex oxytocin increased mEPSC frequency; in prefrontal cortex the receptor-bearing cells are somatostatin interneurons.

3. **Third-order (modulation of other modulators).** The receptors sit on *other* neuromodulatory terminals: in nucleus accumbens "The relevant oxytocin receptors are believed to reside on serotoninergic terminals of raphe inputs ... such that presynaptic oxytocin receptor activation enhances serotoninergic modulation". In midbrain the net effect is a **routing change**: oxytocin increases spontaneous firing of VTA dopamine neurons but suppresses substantia nigra dopamine neurons, "with the net effect of oxytocin in the midbrain dopamine system **biasing overall activity away from substantia nigra and toward VTA**", because the receptor is on VTA (but not SNc) dopamine cells while GABAergic cells in both are driven. And "oxytocin-induced oxytocin release" closes a positive-feedback loop on its own source.

**The temporal structure: a transient modulator that installs a permanent change.**
> "oxytocin transiently decreased call-evoked IPSCs in virgin auditory cortex, leading to excitatory LTP within minutes. In this way, oxytocin modulation acts as a social salience signal to almost immediately boost neural responses, which are then consolidated via mechanisms of long-term plasticity ... so that auditory cortex remains responsive to pup calls perhaps for the lifetime of the animal."
> "Once plasticity has occurred, blocking cortical oxytocin receptors did not substantially affect pup retrieval abilities."
So the modulator is a **write-enable**, not a carrier of content: it opens a window; what gets written is whatever the input happens to be; afterwards the modulator is dispensable.

**Where the social content comes from: the receptive field of the oxytocin neurons, learned socially.** The Carcea et al. 2019 result quoted here is directly a dyadic-learning finding: virgin females' oxytocin neurons were not driven by pups; mothers "began shepherding virgins toward the nest, agonistically chasing them toward the pups, and this shepherding behavior and virgin nest entry activated a fraction of PVN and the identified oxytocin neurons"; later "Observation of maternal retrievals activated virgin oxytocin neurons, and some of these same neurons were reactivated when the virgin retrieved pups", with "bursts of activity in the hypothalamus produced bigger cortical responses, which increased just prior to individual virgins beginning to retrieve pups."

## Evidence with numbers
Sparse, as it is a review of others' work: oxytocin is a "nine amino acid–long cyclical peptide"; "only a fraction of neurons seem to express oxytocin receptors"; IPSCs evoked in "over 90% of principal cells" after optogenetic stimulation of receptor-expressing prefrontal interneurons; receptor expression "low-to-moderate levels in virtually every brain area examined", higher in piriform cortex, CA2, and **female left auditory cortex** (a lateralisation matched behaviourally); virgin cortex has an initial sensitivity to "the most common feature of pup calls (~5 Hz tempo or intersyllable interval)"; plasticity emerges "within minutes to hours". Species differences in receptor distribution are emphasised ("remarkable species diversity"), as is genetic variation in prairie vole accumbens receptor density, where "Repeated neonatal social isolations (modeling neglect) disrupt adult pair-bonding in voles with low accumbens receptor density but not in those with high receptor density".

## Is this a new computation relative to the excluded frameworks?

Parts: (i) multiplicative gain on principal cells (**excluded**); (ii) tonic-down/evoked-up inhibition change = a signal-to-noise or normalisation change (**excluded** as a gain, albeit a two-component one); (iii) a plasticity write-enable gate (**excluded**: this is a learning-rate modulation / three-factor rule, and "fast weights" with a gating term covers it); (iv) routing between two downstream targets by expressing the receptor on only one of them (**not clearly on the list**).

Honest verdict: **mostly a gain, and the review itself frames it that way** ("analogous to turning up the volume"). The one piece that is not a gain is (iv) plus the second-order structure:

- **The receptor's *placement* is the computation.** The same released peptide suppresses SNc and boosts VTA because of *which cells carry the receptor*; it raises signal and lowers noise because the receptor is on interneurons rather than principal cells. The modulator's effect is determined by an **expression map over cell types** which is slow, learned/genetic, and not a scalar. Two agents with identical peptide levels and identical circuits compute different things if their receptor maps differ — and the prairie vole result makes that a measured fact about individuals ("robust individual genetically determined variation in oxytocin receptor density").
- **The modulator carries no content and becomes dispensable.** Blocking receptors after plasticity does not impair the behaviour. So the social signal's role is to *mark* an episode as worth writing, not to specify what is written.

## What it suggests for an affective core where the partner is structured, not a gain

1. **A write-enable, not an influence.** Let the partner's signal (via the agent's own appraisal) gate *whether the current episode is written to slow parameters*, and not what is written or how much is expressed now. Then the partner's influence on the agent's face at time $t$ is identically zero, and its influence is entirely on what the agent becomes. This removes the dial from the expressive path by construction — and it is testable by the dispensability prediction: after the learning window, removing the social signal leaves behaviour unchanged.
2. **Influence as a receptor map, not a coefficient.** Make the social variable's effect depend on a slowly-changing map from the variable to *which* of the agent's own sub-systems carry its receptor. Changing the map changes which computation is boosted; changing the level cannot. "Level × map" factorises the dial into an amount that is bounded and a structure that is discrete — and per-channel differences, sign flips and partner-specific patterns all live in the map.
3. **Two-sided modulation gives a ratio, not an amplitude.** Lower the agent's spontaneous (self-generated) expressive noise *and* raise its evoked response in one move, as oxytocin does in CA1. The observable is a change of signal-to-noise with the mean unchanged — which is a precise, measurable alternative to "the partner made the face bigger", and it matches the project's requirement that influence change structure rather than size.
4. **Third-order modulation names a real design option** that none of the seventeen designs used: the social variable acts on *the agent's other modulators* (its need signals, its curiosity), never on the expressive path. The partner then changes how the agent's own drives are weighted against each other, which is "structured" in the strict sense: the effect is a reweighting within the agent's own motivational system, with no term reaching the face.
5. **The social content must be learned on the input side.** The Carcea result (oxytocin neurons acquiring responses to pups through watching an experienced mother) is this project's "learning by living" in its correct location: not learning how to express, but learning *what counts as a social event*. For the Bot: the slow social variable's receptive field is what experience shapes; the expressive machinery is not.
6. **A direct warning for this project.** The review is explicit that this system is "an attentional modulator ... rather than being inherently prosocial", and that activating it raised *agonistic* behaviour too. Any Bot variable modelled on it must not be described or implemented as "warmth" — if it increases salience, it will increase negative responses as well, and the brief's Principle 6 (no flattery path) requires that this be the case rather than be designed away.

## Verdict
**adopt (two mechanisms: the modulator as a write-enable that is afterwards dispensable; influence as a receptor/expression map over subsystems rather than a coefficient).** The headline computation — salience as signal-to-noise — is a gain and therefore excluded; the review's own framing concedes it, and its "first-order modulation ... at the cost of discriminating between similar signals" is a clear statement of why a gain is the wrong instrument.
