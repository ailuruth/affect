## Key claims
- **Two kinds of solution to the correspondence problem.** Transformational (AIM, Meltzoff & Moore): an innate intermodal code that computes similarity between seen and done acts. Associative (ASL): "direct connections between visual and motor representations of action" that produce the matching act "'blindly', without explicitly representing the relationship".
- **ASL in four points:**
  - **vertical associations** (visual ↔ motor of the same act) solve the correspondence problem; **horizontal associations** (within modality) give sequences;
  - vertical associations "are built by contingent visuomotor experience"; **"Reinforcement for MO is not necessary for the establishment of a vertical association"**;
  - sources of that experience: mirror self-observation, **being imitated by others**, synchronous action to a common stimulus, and acquired equivalence (e.g. the same sound from one's own and another's act);
  - contingency, not contiguity: the learner must experience seeing-and-doing "in a predictive relationship … where the probability of the second event is higher when the first event has occurred than when the first event has not occurred".
- **Erosion answered by context.** Doing an act without seeing it (eyes on hands or toys) does not erode the association; it attaches it to the context in which the contingency holds, "a face occupying a large proportion of the visual field". Prediction: the urge to imitate an act is stronger when the act is shown on a face.
- **No elementally novel imitation** is demonstrated in infants or adults (TPside: one study, one day of three, not reliable against TPmid, no reliability data); "head-touch" may be effector matching, not movement matching.
- **Goal-directed imitation fits ASL via R–S\* learning.** Once a vertical association exists, observation activates the motor representation as "a conscious or unconscious 'urge'", which "can be inhibited or facilitated according to what the observer knows — via R–S* learning … about their situation and the likely consequences of performing the action". Quail imitated pecking or stepping only when they had seen it rewarded for the model.
- **Imitation does not improve without visual feedback.** In Cook et al. (as reported), adults improving a facial imitation over trials improved only with visual feedback of their own attempts; without it, "no improvement at all". So no similarity-computing mechanism guides imitation.

## What it means for the engine
1. **Three separable things that are easily conflated:**
   - the **correspondence map** (vertical association): learned from contingent seeing-and-doing, no reward; a past-term (representation) objective in pass 7's terms, approval-closed for expression learning;
   - the **urge** it produces: a direct seen → motor path, i.e. the mimicry route, which in the engine is bounded per channel by the innate ceilings (D93);
   - the **R–S\* gate**: learned knowledge of what doing the act leads to, which inhibits or facilitates the urge. If the outcome S\* includes the partner's reaction, this gate is exactly the approval route (Proposition 1(a)); if S\* is restricted to the agent's own body variables, it is Proposition 1(b).
2. **Context-gated correspondence is the "per expression, by context" of Q18, learned without labels.** Heyes's contextual control says the map is attached to the context where the contingency was experienced. That is a mechanism for per-channel, context-set strength that needs no rule; whether it can stay under a ceiling is the ceiling's job, not the association's.
3. **Being imitated is a teaching signal that the partner controls.** A partner who mirrors crossed (Cook et al. 2014, already noted: 50-90 min of crossed training reverses mirror responses) rewires the map. In the engine that is a perception attack (misreading the partner), not an expression one, as long as the map does not train expression.
4. **No similarity computation** in the mechanism means no internal "match score" to maximise: there is nothing in ASL that rewards matching. That is the useful negative for the engine: correspondence learning does not by itself create a copy drive; the drive appears only when a reward or an outcome gate is added.

## Verdict
- **adopt (theory, consistent with the evidence read; the decomposition map / urge / outcome gate is the useful product).** ASL is an associative learning account, already the engine's working theory of mimicry (Ray & Heyes, Cook et al.). This paper adds the explicit no-reinforcement statement, contextual gating and the R–S\* gate where the approval route would enter.
