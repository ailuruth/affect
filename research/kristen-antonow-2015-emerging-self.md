# A longitudinal study of the emerging self from 9 months to 4 years (Kristen-Antonow, Sodian, Perst & Licata)

Citation: S. Kristen-Antonow, B. Sodian, H. Perst, M. Licata, "A longitudinal study of the emerging self from 9 months to the age of 4 years", Frontiers in Psychology 6:789 (2015). doi:10.3389/fpsyg.2015.00789.

## Design
- 89 full-term German infants; still-face (SF) task at 9 months (three 30-s normal-interaction phases alternating with a neutral-face phase and an ignore phase); **social mirroring task** at 12 months (Meltzoff 1990 paradigm); mirror self-recognition (MSR, rouge) at 24 months; delayed self-recognition (DSR, sticker on a 2-min-old video) at ~50 months; WPPSI verbal IQ at 4 years.
- **Social mirroring task details.** Two experimenters with identical toys; one imitates the infant's action, the other performs a predefined control action (shake vs slide, pound vs poke, mouth vs touch body). Both "started and stopped acting at the same time as the infant", so *temporal contingency is equated* and only the form of the response differs. Coded: smiling and looking at each experimenter, and **testing behaviour**, "sudden and unexpected actions (sudden stop and restart) on the toy while eyeing the experimenter".

## Results (as reported)
- Descriptives: SF smile effect in 88%, gaze effect 70%, reengagement 79%; MSR at 24 months 68%.
- Correlations (Table 3): SF smile effect → social mirroring at 12 months r = 0.29 (n = 47, p < .05); social mirroring at 12 months → DSR at 4 years r = 0.36 (n = 39); SF gaze effect → MSR φ = 0.29 (n = 50); MSR ↔ DSR r = 0.26 (p < .10).
- Logistic regression for MSR (76.5% correctly classified): only the SF gaze effect is an independent predictor (B = 2.27, Wald 4.44, OR 9.64, p = .035).
- Linear regression for DSR (R² = 0.30, F(6,32) = 2.32, p = .056, marginal overall): social mirroring β = 0.43 (p < .05), verbal IQ β = 0.34 (p < .05), MSR β = 0.29 (p < .10).
- Girls ahead on social mirroring at 12 months (M 1.74 vs 1.11) and on MSR (85% vs 51%).

## What it means for the design
1. **Form and timing are separate signals, and infants use both.** With timing equated, 12-month-olds still prefer the partner whose *form* matches their own action. A design that reads "you responded to me" only from timing (lead–lag, contingency indices) misses this; one that reads it only from form match invites copying. The agent needs both, kept apart.
2. **Infants probe with abrupt self-generated changes** ("sudden stop and restart … while eyeing the experimenter"). Contingency is tested actively by the self's own innovations, which is the developmental counterpart of using the agent's own variation as an instrument (already in the own-body and partner-regulator designs).
3. **Effect sizes are small and the overall DSR model is marginal**; n per cell 39–51. Treat as weak evidence that responsiveness to being mirrored at 12 months relates to a later temporally extended self, not as a mechanism.
