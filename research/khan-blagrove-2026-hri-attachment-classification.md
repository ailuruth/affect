# Khan & Blagrove 2026, "A Procedure for Classifying Attachments and Affective Social Bonds in Human–Robot Dyads" (arXiv 2609.29063v1, cs.HC, 24 Sep 2026)

Source read: arXiv PDF v1. Read: abstract, introduction, the criteria list (Section 2 headings and criteria 1-6), and the "Potential Study Designs" passage that proposes the still-face and the TSST. Sections applying the procedure to published HRI studies, and the ethics section, were only grepped, NOT read.

## Claims
- HRI uses "attachment" and "bonding" "without the behavioural and physiological criteria that give them content in their source disciplines". The paper proposes "a standardised four-question procedure" classifying a human–robot tie as "an attachment, an affective social bond, or no relationship".
- Ainsworth's affectional-bond criteria are used, including "involuntary separation causes distress". Security provision (criterion 6) is the one "any HRI study claiming an 'attachment' must meet".
- On separation distress: "The still-face structure is a first example, in which an agent remains physically present and becomes briefly unresponsive before resuming [116]. This is a well-validated developmental paradigm, which takes less than one minute, and is fully reversible within the same session." Reference [116] is Mesman et al. 2009.
- Physiological regulation is the hard criterion, because "selectivity is easy to fake".

## Evidence
Position and methods paper. No new data in the parts I read. It cites a 2025 paper, "Robot attachment modeling via vision-based human following in strange situation test-like scenarios" (Lonklang et al., FAIR 2025), which I could not find or read.

## Verdict: watch
It runs the still-face the other way round from this project. The robot goes still and the *human's* separation distress is measured, physiologically. That is a human-side use, so it fits this project's rules and matches the brief's S5/S6 aim of measuring the operator, not just the Bot.

Note one discrepancy: "takes less than one minute" does not match the 2-3 min episodes in the meta-analysis it cites. If used, run the published episode lengths.

Its criterion list is also a useful check against over-claiming "attachment" for the Bot from contact-seeking behavior alone.
