# Kirkham, Hayes, Pawling & Tipper 2015, "Facial Mimicry and Emotion Consistency: Influences of Memory and Context" (PLoS ONE 10(12):e0145731, doi 10.1371/journal.pone.0145731)

Link: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4689420/ (open access).

## What was read
The full PMC text: introduction, methods, all results (trust, reaction times, accuracy, EMG for both stages, within-expression analyses), general discussion, conclusions, and the caption of supporting figure S1 (an additional explicit experiment). The supporting tables and the S1 figure itself were not opened.

## What it says
- **Design.** 28 women (EMG n = 24). Eight face identities. **Learning stage:** 80 trials (10 per identity, about 10 s each, so about 13 min by my estimate). Participants classified a positive or negative scene and were told to ignore a face beside it; the face shifted gaze to the scene and morphed (350 ms) into a smile or a frown. Four identities were always consistent with the scene (smile at positive, frown at negative), four always inconsistent. **Retrieval stage,** a few minutes later: the same faces smiled or frowned with no scene, 8 times each (64 trials), and participants classified the expression. EMG over corrugator and zygomaticus in 500-ms bins.
- **The partner's consistency is encoded.** Scene classification was slower on inconsistent trials (928 vs 986 ms, F(1,26) = 21.7, η²p = .46). Trust fell for inconsistent identities (post-learning −13.8 vs −2.2; Consistency × Time F(1,27) = 5.42, η²p = .17).
- **Online, mimicry follows consistency with the shared scene.** In the learning stage, corrugator matching (frown > smile) and zygomaticus matching (smile > frown) appeared only for consistent faces (corrugator Expression × Consistency F(1,23) = 5.20; zygomaticus consistent smile > frown at 3.0–3.5 s). For inconsistent faces there was no matching, with a marginal reversal on the corrugator at 3.5–4 s. Within smiles: inconsistent smiles gave *more* corrugator than consistent ones (F(1,23) = 6.58, η²p = .22). Frowns were not discriminated by consistency. The differences sit at 2.0–4.0 s after the face began to move.
- **Nothing persists to the face.** At retrieval, corrugator matched every face (frown > smile, F(1,23) = 19.1, η²p = .45) with no Consistency interaction (F = 0.014); the zygomaticus showed no matching at all. So implicitly learned consistency changed trust but not later mimicry.
- **An explicit version gates strongly** (S1 figure caption and discussion, an unpublished study, n = 24 per group): when participants imagined friends and enemies reacting to their exam result, mimicry of faces whose expression was inappropriate to the participant's outcome was "completely suppressed".

## What matters for this engine
- **Within the moment, matching depends on whether the face agrees with the shared event.** That is co-appraisal: a smile at a good event is matched; a smile at a bad event draws a brow response instead. It acts at 2–4 s, after the early part of the response, which fits fast matching then context.
- **Implicit partner history did not carry over to the face within minutes.** Ten exposures per identity changed trust but not mimicry. Together with Korb 2019, where the partner predicted the observer's own outcome and the gating did carry over, it suggests that what transfers to later mimicry is what the partner meant *for the observer*, not how well the partner's face tracked events.
- **Learning signal: approval-closed.** The scene and the face were never contingent on the observer's face. The encoded variable is a prediction (does this face agree with events?), not a reward.
- **For the build core.** The online part maps to the echo gate g for the smile group: if the shared event is in his words, g reads them (Λ_V, 𝒟_p(N_L)) and can close the smile echo when they are negative, at 0.45 s, as fitted on recorded listeners. A frown on his face draws no frown back (BL has a zero ceiling), so the brow side of the result is reached only as absence of copying. The null retention agrees with the build core's lack of any within-session learning from his expressions; it is a test the engine should pass (no change in echo or cone toward an identity whose face was merely inconsistent with his words).

## Limits
Women only; static photographs morphing over 350 ms; faces task-irrelevant; inconsistency at 50 % made the whole context unreliable (the authors' own explanation for the null zygomaticus at retrieval); the five trial bins were defined but not analysed, so no learning curve is reported.

## Verdict: adopt (as evidence that online matching depends on agreement with the shared event, and that implicit partner history did not reach later mimicry)
