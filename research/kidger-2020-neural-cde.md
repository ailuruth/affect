# Neural Controlled Differential Equations for Irregular Time Series (Kidger, Morrill, Foster & Lyons)

Citation: P. Kidger, J. Morrill, J. Foster, T. Lyons. "Neural Controlled Differential Equations for Irregular Time Series." NeurIPS 2020. arXiv:2005.08926.

Source read: arXiv PDF, text-extracted. Read in full: abstract, sections 1–7 (introduction, background, method, universal approximation statement, evaluation, comparison with alternative ODE models, adjoint training, intensity as a channel, partial observation, batching, experiments on CharacterTrajectories, PhysioNet sepsis and Speech Commands, related work, discussion, limitations, conclusion) and Appendix C (Theorem C.1 with proof). Read in part: Appendix A (spline construction; the bound derivations skimmed) and Appendix B (only the statement of Theorem B.14). Not read: Appendix D (experimental details).

## The failure it answered (the derivation story)
- The bottleneck was structural, not empirical: "the solution to an ordinary differential equation is determined by its initial condition, and there is no mechanism for adjusting the trajectory based on subsequent observations." Earlier fixes (ODE-RNN, GRU-D) interrupt the ODE with a jump at each observation, which breaks adjoint training.
- The fix was imported from rough analysis rather than invented: replace dt by dX. z_t = z_{t0} + ∫ f_θ(z_s) dX_s, where X is a spline through the observations (time included as a channel). "Just as Neural ODEs are the continuous analogue of a ResNet, the Neural CDE is the continuous analogue of an RNN."
- **The key theorem is a subtraction of input nonlinearity.** Theorem C.1: any model dz = h_θ(z, X_s) ds can be represented by a CDE dz = f_θ(z) dX_s (with a larger state that records X), "however the converse statement is not true … a Neural CDE can easily represent the identity function between paths, whilst the alternative cannot." The proof's counterexample is a constant f that copies X into the state.
- Measured: CharacterTrajectories with 30/50/70% dropped, 98.7/98.8/98.6% vs ODE-RNN 95.4/96.0/95.3%; PhysioNet sepsis AUC 0.880 with observational intensity, but only 0.776 without it (ODE-RNN 0.833, GRU-D 0.850); Speech Commands 89.8 ± 2.5% vs ODE-RNN 65.9 ± 35.6%. All five runs per model; hyperparameters tuned on the ODE-RNN.
- Design notes the authors give: time must be a channel because "a CDE is blind to speed at which X is traversed"; without an initial-value network the model is translation invariant; a final tanh on f "constrains the rate of change of the hidden state"; the spline is not causal, so the published model "is infeasible … in real-time settings".

## What it means for the design
1. **The partner as a driver, not an input, is a precise object.** In a CDE the partner path would enter as dX: it changes *which direction* the agent's own vector fields push, and does nothing when the partner does not move. Held partner levels drive nothing (dX = 0). This is the mathematical form of the "partner as regulator of my own dynamics" idea already in the designs (design-partner-regulator.md), but with the time integral taken against the partner's motion instead of the clock.
2. **The paper's own theorem is the warning.** The thing CDEs do easily that ODEs cannot is *copy the driving path* (f constant gives z = X). If the agent's face is a readout of a state driven by dp, copying is the cheapest function in the class. A CDE core must restrict what the driver is: driving by dp invites copying; driving only by antisymmetric level-2 terms of (own, partner) does not contain the identity map (see chevyrev-kormilitzin-2016-signature-primer.md).
3. **Per-channel observational intensity** (a cumulative count channel per stream) is a parameter-free way to let event *rate* act, and the sepsis result shows it matters a great deal. For the dyad it is also a hazard: rate becomes a dial unless its effect is bounded.
4. **Real time needs a causal path** (the authors defer this to later work, not read here); the project's 30 Hz loop would use a causal interpolation.

## Verdict
- [Neural controlled differential equations (Kidger et al.)](kidger-2020-neural-cde.md) — 2020 — watch (as substrate; pattern adopted) — the fix for "an ODE cannot hear later data" was to integrate against the data path, not the clock; Theorem C.1 shows the CDE strictly contains input-driven ODEs *because* it can copy its driver with a constant field, so a dyadic CDE must not be driven by the partner's raw increments; non-causal splines block real time.
