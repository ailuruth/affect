# Hofman, Bos, Schutter & van Honk 2012, "Fairness modulates non-conscious facial mimicry in women" (Proceedings of the Royal Society B 279:3535-3539, doi 10.1098/rspb.2012.0694)

Source read: full text on PubMed Central (PMC3396901), all sections.

## Claims
- During a bargaining game, happy mimicry toward partners who just made an unfair offer disappears.
- After the game, partners' acquired reputation changes anger mimicry in opposite directions: more anger mimicry toward unfair partners, less toward fair partners; happy mimicry returns to baseline for both.
- Mimicry "may be strategically employed to enforce norm compliance"; it can be complementary, not only assimilative.

## Evidence
- 30 women, 4 excluded for no detectable EMG change, final N = 26 (the reported df, e.g. t15 and F1,15, imply about 16; the paper does not explain this).
- EMG corrugator and zygomaticus, left side, 2048 Hz, 30-500 Hz; -1000 to +4000 ms windows around morph onset; baseline = mean of the 1,000 ms before onset; 16 bins of 250 ms.
- Block 1 (baseline): 16 neutral-to-happy and 16 neutral-to-angry morphs (2 s morph + 2 s full). Zygomaticus to happy F(1,15) = 102.8, η² = .873; corrugator to angry F(1,15) = 87.8, η² = .854.
- Block 2 (64 trials): participant donates 25 points to one of two neutral-looking confederates, pre-programmed fair (75% split) or unfair (75% keep all); then the chosen one shows the happy or angry morph. Zygomaticus fairness effect F(2,30) = 46.7, p < .001, η² = .757; mimicry absent after unfair offers, t15 = 9.62, d = 1.87; after fair offers a trend to a small decrease, t15 = 1.99, p = .066. Corrugator F(2,30) = 3.5, p = .042, no contrast significant.
- Block 3 (reputation, like block 1): corrugator F(2,30) = 20.73, η² = .58; more anger mimicry to unfair confederates, t15 = -4.21, p = .001, d = 0.26; less to fair ones, t15 = 3.66, p = .002, d = 0.36. Zygomaticus no effect, F(2,30) = 0.62.

## What it implies for the design
- The relation is learned from the partner's behavior within minutes, and once learned it reshapes the response to specific expressions in different directions (anger return up for the unfair partner, down for the fair one). A single relation gain would move both expressions the same way.
- There is a short-term state (just treated unfairly: smile return gone) and a longer-term reputation (anger return changed, smile return back to normal). The agent needs at least two time scales for the partner model, which matches the brief's "time scales from reflex to temperament".
- Small N with a df discrepancy; women only; morphs of four Ekman actors. Use as a qualitative pattern.

## Verdict: adopt (as evidence that learned partner history changes response shape)
