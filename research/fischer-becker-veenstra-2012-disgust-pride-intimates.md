# Fischer, Becker & Veenstra 2012, "Emotional mimicry in social context: the case of disgust and pride" (Frontiers in Psychology 3:475, doi 10.3389/fpsyg.2012.00475)

Source read: full article PDF from Frontiers (open access): methods, results, discussion.

## Claims
- In live dyads, disgust displays were not mimicked, by intimates or strangers.
- Smiles were mimicked only between intimates (friends or family), both the smiles that accompanied the disgust event and the smiles after a compliment (pride event).
- Empathy, not mimicry, related to recognising the partner's disgust.

## Evidence
- 112 Dutch women (mean age 45.3) at a magazine summer festival, 56 dyads: intimates (N = 60) vs strangers (N = 52). Assignment not fully random (people who came together were intimates). One woman was the expresser, the other observed in silence with eye contact.
- Disgust: expresser smelled asafetida (~5 s clips). Pride: praised after a "difficult" task (~2 s clips). FACS intensity 0-3 by the first author: AU9/10, AU43 (incl. looking away), AU4, AU12; a certified coder recoded 61%, inter-coder r .80 to .91.
- Mimicry was operationalised as the correlation across dyads between expresser and observer intensity in the same clip.
- Familiarity check: intimates M = 6.25 vs strangers 1.44, F(1,104) = 571.19, η²p = .85.
- Disgust composite (AU9/10, AU43, AU4): intimates r = -.04, p = .85; strangers r = .16, p = .43. Observers did show some frowning and nose wrinkling, at lower intensity than expressers (F(3,52) = 10.83, η²p = .38).
- Smiles during the disgust event: intimates r = .47, p = .009; strangers r = -.05, p = .82.
- Smiles after the compliment: intimates r = .59, p = .001; strangers r = .07, p = .73.

## What it implies for the design
- In the one live study of familiar vs unfamiliar partners, familiarity did not raise mimicry across the board: it switched on smile coupling and left disgust uncoupled. Strangers smiled about as much, but not in response to each other. So the relation changes which channels are coupled, and the coupling is to the partner's moment-to-moment smile rather than to a mean level.
- During the partner's negative event, the familiar observer's coupled response was a smile, not a copy of the negative display. An agent built for a long relation with one operator should be able to answer the operator's displeasure with a coupled positive display, a pattern a pure copy cannot produce.
- Limits: correlation across dyads over a few seconds is coarse and says nothing about lag; single main coder; women only; intimates self-selected.

## Verdict: adopt (as the live-dyad evidence that familiarity gates specific channels)
