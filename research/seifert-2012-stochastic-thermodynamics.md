# Seifert 2012, "Stochastic thermodynamics, fluctuation theorems and molecular machines" (Rep. Prog. Phys. 75:126001; arXiv 1205.4176v1)

Source read: arXiv PDF v1 (18 May 2012) via text extraction: abstract; section 2 (Langevin dynamics, NESS, first law, housekeeping/excess heat, strong-coupling caveat, alternative work definition, stochastic entropy); section 3.2.1 (Jarzynski) and 3.3 (IFT for total entropy, SSFT, Hatano-Sasa, IFT for housekeeping heat); section 4.4.2 (periodic driving); section 7.3.1 and 7.3.7 (Kelvin-Planck and feedback); section 10.1 closing paragraph and 10.3.2 (efficiency of isothermal machines); section 11.3 opening. Not read: the rest of this 105-page review (sections 5, 6, 8, 9, most of 7, 10 and 11, the conclusion and references). The published journal version was not compared.

## Claims
- Dynamics (Eq. 1): "ẋ = µF(x, λ) + ζ = µ(−∂ₓV(x, λ) + f(x, λ)) + ζ", noise "⟨ζ(τ)ζ(τ′)⟩ = 2Dδ(τ − τ′)", Einstein relation "D = Tµ". f is a non-conservative force; "Distinguishing these two sources is relevant only in the case of periodic boundary conditions on a ring".
- Sekimoto's first law (Eqs. 14-19): sign convention "work applied to the particle (or more generally system) is positive as is heat transferred or dissipated into the environment." "đw = (∂V/∂λ)dλ + f dx" (15), "đq = đw − dV = F dx" (16), integrated "w[x(τ)] = ∫₀ᵗ[(∂V/∂λ)λ̇ + f ẋ]dτ" (17), "q[x(τ)] = ∫₀ᵗ F ẋ dτ" (18), and "w[x(τ)] = q[x(τ)] + ∆V" (19) "on the level of an individual trajectory", with products evaluated by the "mid-point, i.e., Stratonovitch rule".
- Entropy: medium entropy "∆sₘ[x(τ)] ≡ q[x(τ)]/T" (27); system entropy "s(τ) ≡ −ln p(x(τ), τ)" (28); mean total entropy production rate "Ṡᵗᵒᵗ(τ) = ∫dx j(x, τ)²/(Dp(x, τ)) = ⟨ν(x, τ)²⟩/D ≥ 0" (35).
- Second law: "⟨exp(−∆sᵗᵒᵗ)⟩ = 1" (64) "for arbitrary initial distribution p(x, 0), arbitrary time-dependent driving λ(τ) and an arbitrary length t of the process", giving "⟨∆sᵗᵒᵗ⟩ ≥ 0". Caveat: "by using the Langevin equation a fundamental irreversibility has been implemented from the very beginning. Thus, this IFT should definitely not be considered to constitute a fundamental proof of the second law." Jarzynski (58) gives "⟨w⟩ ≥ ∆F" but "requires that one starts in the equilibrium distribution". FT validity "essentially rests on assuming that the noise in the Langevin equation (1) is not affected by the driving."
- Housekeeping/excess split (Eqs. 21-23): "q ≡ qʰᵏ + qᵉˣ"; "The housekeeping heat is the heat inevitably dissipated in maintaining the corresponding NESS", "qʰᵏ ≡ ∫₀ᵗ dτ ẋ(τ)µ⁻¹vˢ(x(τ), λ(τ))"; the excess heat "qᵉˣ = T[−∆φ + ∫₀ᵗ dτ λ̇∂_λφ]" "is the heat associated with changing the external control parameter", with φ = −ln pˢ the "non-equilibrium potential" (11). Housekeeping heat obeys "⟨exp[−qʰᵏ/T]⟩ = 1" (68) "for arbitrary initial state, driving and length of trajectories".
- Periodic driving: Seifert does not state a cycle-work theorem. He states Kelvin-Planck in words, "one cannot extract work from an equilibrated system at constant temperature without leaving any traces", notes that with measurement and feedback one can "extract work in a cyclic process" until erasure cost is counted, and that "The Kelvin-Planck statement of the second law does not hold for micro-canonical initial conditions". On periodic machines: "even making explicit statements about the periodic steady state is much harder than for the NESS engine at constant external parameters."
- Weak-coupling assumption: the heat identification "relies on the implicit assumption that the unavoidable coupling between particle ... and the degrees of freedom making up the heat bath does neither depend crucially on x nor on the control parameter λ."

## Verdict: adopt (definitions of work and heat; the periodic theorem must be proven in our own notes)
- For (a): valence-as-work and arousal-as-dissipation can use Eqs. 15-18 directly. Under the review's sign convention w is work done on the system, so "valence = work" needs a stated sign. With f ≠ 0, part of the dissipation is housekeeping (paid even at fixed λ) and part is excess (paid for change). That is a natural split between tonic and phasic arousal.
- For (b): the theorem "no periodic protocol yields positive mean extracted work" is not stated in the review. It follows from what was read, and I derived it here (not a quote). In a periodic steady state, p(x, t + tₚ) = p(x, t), so over one period ⟨∆V⟩ = 0 and ⟨∆s⟩ = 0. Eqs. 19 and 27 then give ⟨w⟩ = ⟨q⟩ = T⟨∆sₘ⟩ = T⟨∆sᵗᵒᵗ⟩ ≥ 0 by Eq. 64. By Eq. 35 the bound is strict unless the current j vanishes throughout the cycle. Conditions the read text attaches: one bath at temperature T; Einstein relation; noise not affected by driving; weak coupling; canonical (not microcanonical) ensemble; and no measurement-and-feedback. Feedback is the stated loophole, and an agent that senses and acts is a feedback system, so the theorem holds only for open-loop protocols or once information costs are counted.
