# Interpersonal synchrony feels good but impedes self-regulation of affect (Galbusera, Finn, Tschacher & Kyselo)

Laura Galbusera, Michael T. M. Finn, Wolfgang Tschacher & Miriam Kyselo, *Scientific Reports* 9:14691, 2019, doi:10.1038/s41598-019-50960-0 (PMC6789117).

## What was read
The full PMC open-access text: introduction (enactive framing), results with Tables 1–2, discussion, and methods (participants, setup, the Body-Conversation Task, questionnaires, Kinect behavioural imaging, synchrony computation). Supplementary instructions and videos were not seen.

## Findings, precisely

**Design.** 66 unacquainted adults (45 F, 21 M; mean 27.5 years; 33 dyads), Berlin. After a 5-min solo warm-up facing away from each other, a **5-min Body-Conversation Task**: "Improvise movement together with the other person. Imagine you are having a conversation without words, only through movement"; whole body, move at all times, each in a half-ellipse, no touching. State questionnaires before and after: PANAS positive/negative affect; State-DERS (modulate subscale = felt difficulty in modulating one's state in the moment; total score); state self-control (SSCCS).

**Synchrony measure.** Two Kinect v2 depth cameras, 11 limb segments at 30 Hz (angular speed). Windowed cross-correlations over **±5 s lags** in 30-s windows, **absolute values**, Fisher-z, averaged. Interpersonal = all 121 between-person segment pairs; intrapersonal = 55 within-person pairs. Presence tested against segment data shuffled within 10-s windows.

**Results.**
- Both present vs shuffled: intrapersonal t(65) = 23.4, d = 2.89; interpersonal t(32) = 15.5, d = 2.70. Intrapersonal (M = 0.12) > interpersonal (0.09).
- **Strong mutual adaptation of style:** partners' velocities r = 0.63; partners' movement variability r = 0.38; **partners' intrapersonal synchrony r = 0.71** (each person's internal coordination comes to resemble the other's).
- Interpersonal and intrapersonal synchrony correlate **r = 0.83**.
- Positive affect rose after the task (d = 0.65). Mixed model (pre-score controlled, dyad random): interpersonal synchrony **+0.31** (SE 0.14, p = .03); intrapersonal synchrony **−0.45** (SE 0.14, p = .0016).
- **Modulation difficulty** (the trade-off test): interpersonal synchrony **B = +0.23** (SE 0.12, t = 2.17, p = .03; partial R² 0.185); intrapersonal −0.18 (p = .086). No interaction. Pre-score B ≈ 0.9 dominates (marginal R² ≈ 0.80). Robust to adding velocity and variability.
- General measures: S-DERS total n.s.; self-control −0.27 (p = .087).

**Limits.** No control condition, so correlational. The synchrony index uses **absolute** cross-correlations at any lag up to ±5 s, so in-phase, anti-phase and lagged complementary coupling all count as "synchrony". The 0.83 collinearity between inter- and intrapersonal synchrony makes the separate estimates fragile. The key effect is p = .03 on 66 people with self-report outcomes.

## What it means for the designs
1. **A cost of high coupling to the self's own regulation.** The relevant property for a design: when coupling to the partner is high, the agent's ability to return its own state toward its own set point is reduced. Stated mechanically: the partner-driven term must not be able to take over the self-restoring dynamics; there must be a separate, self-referenced restoring force whose strength is **not** lowered by coupling, or, if it is lowered (as these data suggest happens in people), the design must include something that pulls coupling down when self-restoration has been weakened for a while. Either way, unbounded coupling is not a neutral state.
2. **Partners converge in style, not in movements.** The strongest numbers here are not the interpersonal synchrony but the convergence of *each person's own* coordination pattern (r = 0.71) and speed (r = 0.63). This is a slow, held-level convergence of parameters of the person's own dynamics — a different object from copying the partner's movements. A design can allow this kind of slow parameter drift toward the partner while keeping moment-to-moment output non-copying.
3. **Within-self coherence moves opposite to between-person coherence on the outcomes.** Intrapersonal coordination predicted the opposite effects. A design should treat the coherence of its own channels as a separate quantity from coupling to the partner.
4. **Weight:** watch-level. One study, correlational, an absolute-value index that cannot separate copying from complementary coupling.
5. **Time scale:** a single 5-min episode; effect measured immediately after.

## Verdict
**watch** — suggestive evidence (p = .03, n = 66, correlational) that high whole-body coupling over 5 min goes with weaker self-modulation; the more solid finding is that partners converge in their own movement style (r = 0.63–0.71) rather than in the movements themselves.
