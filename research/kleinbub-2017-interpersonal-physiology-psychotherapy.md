# Kleinbub 2017, "State of the Art of Interpersonal Physiology in Psychotherapy: A Systematic Review" (Front Psychol 8:2053, doi 10.3389/fpsyg.2017.02053); with Palumbo et al. 2017, "Interpersonal Autonomic Physiology: A Systematic Review of the Literature" (PSPR 21(2):99-141, doi 10.1177/1088868316628405)

Source read, 2026-11-25:
- **Kleinbub 2017:** full text, open access, from frontiersin.org. Read: abstract, method, Table 1 (all 19 studies), and the trends, terminology, theory, assessment and design sections, plus the conclusions.
- **Palumbo et al. 2017: abstract only** (PubMed 26921410; the URI Digital Commons record has no file, only a link to SAGE, and SAGE returned 403). Palumbo was the requested source. Kleinbub, which builds on Palumbo and covers the role-asymmetric therapist-client case, was read instead. Nothing below about Palumbo goes beyond its abstract.

## Claims
- **Palumbo (abstract):**
  - Interpersonal autonomic physiology is "the relationship between people's physiological dynamics, as indexed by continuous measures of the autonomic nervous system".
  - Synchrony "has been found in both new and established relationships across a range of contexts".
  - "This scientific literature is fragmented, making it difficult to evaluate consistency across reports."
- **Kleinbub:**
  - IP in psychotherapy "can be considered an established fact" (at least for skin conductance and cardiac activity), but "its dynamics and clinical meaning ... are still almost completely unknown".
  - Most studies use correlation, which ignores non-stationarity and autocorrelation ("SC ... is indeed highly nonstationary and autocorrelated").
  - Lagged correlation cannot establish lead and lag: "it can't be established if a high synchronization at a specific lag is caused by leading-pacing dynamics or just by synchronization at different phases."
  - Negative correlations are handled inconsistently. They are counted as IP in Motion Energy Analysis work and as absence of IP in Marci's index, and they could be "a different form of co-regulation underlying different behaviors (a yet unexplored direction)". He links this to "anti-phase physiological linkage (Reed et al., 2013) or complementarity (Dale et al., 2013)".
  - "The implicit assumption present in most studies that 'more IP' ... is always better, might be an oversimplification."

## Evidence (Kleinbub 2017)
- **Corpus:** database hits of 500 (Scopus), 98 (WoS), 163 (PsycINFO) and 98 (PubMed). 10 matched the criteria and 9 more came from references, for 19 studies in total from 1955 on; "only 9 publications since 2010". The most common terms were concordance (n = 7) and synchrony (n = 5). Theory was empathy in 6 studies and unspecified in 5. 11 of 19 used nomothetic designs.
- **Role- and content-dependent sign:**
  - Dimascio et al. 1957 (1 dyad, 12 sessions): patient and therapist HRV were "similar in 'Tension,' 'Tension release,' and 'Neutral' segments, but opposite in 'Antagonism' segments".
  - Coleman et al. 1956 (1 dyad, 44 sessions): HR was similar for all patient emotions "except 'extra-punitive hostility' characterized by relatively higher therapist's HR".
  - Päivinen et al. 2016: "In some occasion therapist showed opposite arousal in respect to patients" during blaming.
  - Di Mascio et al. 1955 named inverse HR correlation "discordance".
- **Empathy:**
  - Robinson et al. 1982 (21 dyads): the number of patient skin-conductance peaks matched by the counselor within 7 s "showed strong correlation with perceived empathy".
  - Marci & Orr 2006 (20 dyads): SC concordance and perceived empathy were "significantly lower in the 'emotional distance' dyads".
  - Marci et al. 2007 (20 dyads): concordance correlated with perceived empathy.
  - Kleinbub et al. 2012 (39 dyads): concordance correlated with perceived but not observer-rated empathy, and "the three levels of training predicted higher concordance at different lags".
- **Lead and lag by role:**
  - Palmieri et al. (in press; 18 dyads): secure-attachment priming of interviewers "increased SC synchrony at negative lags (i.e., therapist leading) but not overall".
  - Karvonen et al. 2016 (10 tetrads): synchrony mostly "with a lag up to 1 s"; "therapist-therapist dyads had the most synchrony, while patient-patient ones the least".
- **More is not better (outside therapy, cited):** "Levenson and Ruef, 1992" found high synchrony not necessarily good. "A study on mother-infant dyads found both very high and very low levels of prosodic synchronization to be predictive of insecure attachment (Jaffe et al., 2001)."

## Formal models
None beyond correlation procedures:
- Marci's ratio of positive to negative moving-window correlations;
- windowed cross-correlation (Boker et al. 2002);
- lag analysis;
- one cross-recurrence and Markov-transition study (Orsucci et al. 2016).

Kleinbub recommends Granger causality (with windowing or a Toda-Yamamoto correction for non-stationarity) and "system dynamics (Ferrer and Helm, 2013)" as better-suited models.

## What it implies for a dyadic affective core
- **In an asymmetric-role dyad (helper and helped) the helper's coupling depends on content.** Concordant during tension and its release, opposite during antagonism. A helper that copies the helped person's arousal during hostility is the wrong model, and so is one with a fixed gain. The agent-user dyad is role-asymmetric in the same way.
- **The helper's role shows up in lead and lag, not in overall amount.** Priming security in therapists changed who led (negative lags), not total synchrony. A single gain parameterizes amount only; the human effect is in the lag structure.
- **Inverse coupling is plausibly its own regime:** "a different form of co-regulation". The two existing scoring schemes treat it in opposite ways, so any evaluation should keep the sign of the coupling.
- **Inverted-U evidence:** Jaffe et al. 2001, via Kleinbub, found both very high and very low synchrony predictive of insecure attachment. Maximal coupling is not the target (Principle 6).
- **What is NOT shown:**
  - The corpus is tiny and mostly single-dyad case reports or n ≤ 39.
  - All results are correlational and use unvalidated window parameters (most copy Marci & Orr's without a rationale).
  - The sign-by-content observations come from 1950s single cases and qualitative plots.

## Verdict: watch
- **Why watch:** the review is sound on method and points the right way: keep the sign, model lead and lag, and do not assume more is better. Its role-asymmetric evidence is case-level.
- **What would change it:** read Palumbo et al. 2017 in full before citing its counts; it is the broader systematic review the brief asked for.
