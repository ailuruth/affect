## The computation, precisely

The review's claim is not that modulators scale a circuit's gain. It is that **the synaptic graph underdetermines the computation, and a modulatory state selects which computation the same graph performs**:

> "the functional connections that give rise to a specific circuit output are specified, or in fact 'configured', by the neuromodulatory environment"
> "the connectivity diagram by itself only establishes potential circuit configurations, whose availability and properties depend critically on which of many neuromodulators are present at a given moment"
> "Under some modulatory conditions, anatomically 'present' synaptic connections may be functionally silent, only to be strengthened under other modulatory conditions."

Stated as a map: let $G$ be the fixed anatomical graph, $m \in \mathcal{M}$ the modulatory state (a vector of concentrations of many co-released substances), and $\theta(m)$ the induced parameter set (per-neuron intrinsic conductances **and** per-synapse strengths). The circuit computes $F_{\theta(m)}(\text{input})$. Four properties of this map are the content of the review:

1. **Membership is not fixed.** Neurons change which rhythm they belong to: "This is one of many examples of circuit switching in the STG, in which neurons switch from being part of the pyloric or gastric circuits." Red Pigment Concentrating Hormone (RPCH) "strengthens synapses from the IVN neurons to STG network neurons and creates a single, conjoint rhythm from neurons that ordinarily are part of the cardiac sac and gastric rhythm". So $m$ changes the *partition* of the cells into circuits, not an amplitude.
2. **Intrinsic properties, not only synapses.** Modulators "can qualitatively alter the neurons' intrinsic properties, transforming neurons from tonic spiking to those generating plateau potentials or bursts". Dopamine "modulates several currents in the same neuron, and ... the same current can be modulated differently in different target neurons". Electrically coupled neurons "could respond differently to the same modulatory substance".
3. **Degeneracy.** Many different $m$ reach similar outputs ("To some extent these constitute degenerate mechanisms that can, as a first approximation substitute for each other, if it is more important that a rhythm exist than its exact form"), and many different $\theta$ reach similar output ("there are many different sets of parameters that can produce similar output patterns"). Also: "random assignment of parameters to a single neuron or a circuit will lead to significantly more failures than successful models."
4. **Five structural brakes that prevent $m$ from acting as one gain.** These are the sharpest part of the paper for this project:
   - *Opposing processes:* dopamine acts "on both inward and outward currents", keeping neurons "within their operating range".
   - *Voltage dependence as a built-in ceiling:* proctolin's current is "blocked at hyperpolarized membrane potentials by extracellular Ca2+, and has a reversal potential about 0 mV", so its peak is near threshold; it "increases the amplitude of the oscillations generated by bursting neurons without producing a depolarization of the baseline", whereas nicotine (ionotropic) "depolarizes the baseline ... and can result in a depolarization block". The peptide has "a built-in brake".
   - *Convergence and occlusion:* CCAP, RPCH, TNRNFLRFamide, SDRNFLRFamide and CabTRP1a all activate the same voltage-dependent current, so "they occlude each other's actions ... if a neuron is already highly activated by one of these modulatory substances, a second of them will be relatively ineffective." The sum of many modulators saturates by construction.
   - *Postsynaptic saturation:* RPCH gives "several-fold increases in the amplitude of the inhibitory LP to PD synapse" yet "this increase in synaptic strength does not necessarily change the frequency of the pyloric rhythm", because "the effect of the inhibitory input to an oscillator often saturates as synaptic strength is increased". The network is "de facto protected against over-modulation".
   - *Coordinate multi-site action:* modulators act on central pattern generator, neuromuscular junction and muscle together to keep presynaptic activity "appropriately matched to their muscle targets".
5. **Tonic tone versus phasic release are different channels of the same substance.** Hemolymph concentrations are "in the nanomolar range, while release from nerve terminals can produce substantially higher concentrations"; non-synaptic DA receptors signal "tonic modulatory tone", and "tonic low concentrations of DA seem to be important for maintaining circuit basal function, while phasic, higher concentrations produce shorter-term modulation".
6. **State dependence and sign changes.** "many modulators show state-dependent actions, so that the activity or prior history of activity of the network determines the extent or sign of modulator action", and "modulator action may depend critically on other modulators".

## Evidence with numbers
Thin, and this must be said plainly. The crab STG has "~30 neurons", the cardiac ganglion 9; "25 pairs of descending modulatory neurons bring a host of substances into the neuropil of the STG"; hemolymph modulator concentrations are nanomolar; "whether there are 5 or 12 or 25 modulators that can influence the output of a given circuit". Everything else is cited to primary papers (Swensen & Marder 2000 for occlusion; Dickinson et al. 1990 for the conjoint rhythm; Blitz et al. 1999 for three proctolin neurons with different cotransmitter complements producing different motor patterns; Golowasch & Marder 1992 for the voltage-dependent proctolin current; Thirumalai et al. 2006 for synaptic saturation; Grashow et al. 2009 for reliable modulation across variable networks, with "a small proportion of networks responded anomalously"). None of those were read here.

## Is this a new computation relative to the excluded frameworks?

Parts list: (i) a fixed graph; (ii) a parameter vector set by extrinsic chemical state; (iii) conductance-based neuron dynamics; (iv) saturating/occluding receptor convergence; (v) voltage-dependent (state-dependent) coupling.

Honest verdict: **the implementation is not new — (iii) is a conductance ODE and (ii)+(iii) together are parameter scheduling, which an SSM with input-dependent coefficients already contains** (that is Mamba-style selection, or the bilinear port-Hamiltonian scheduling already recorded as a recombination in `foundations/designs.md`). What is *not* in the excluded list is the pair of claims that make the biology work:

- **The modulatory variable changes the partition of units into subsystems** (which cells belong to which rhythm), i.e. it changes the *signature* of the computation, not a coefficient inside a fixed one. No excluded framework has a partition as its state.
- **Over-modulation is impossible by construction, not by tuning**: reversal potential at 0 mV, receptor convergence and occlusion onto one current, and saturation of an oscillator's response to inhibitory strength. These are three distinct derived ceilings, each of which deletes the top end of a dial rather than setting it.

So: recombination as dynamics, genuinely outside the list as a *constraint* on how an outside signal may enter.

## What it suggests for an affective core where the partner is structured, not a gain

1. **Partner context as a partition selector.** If the agent's expressive machinery is a fixed graph of units, let the partner's signal (through the agent's own perception) select *which units participate in which sub-circuit* — a discrete, low-entropy choice — while all magnitudes stay the agent's own. The flat-to-copy dial is then not a scalar but a choice among partitions, and "copying" is a partition that must be reachable, which can be shown to be absent from the reachable set.
2. **Occlusion as the derived ceiling (the invariant worth stealing).** Make all partner-driven and all self-driven modulatory channels converge on one current whose receptors share a single saturable site: $g_{\text{tot}} = g_{\max}\,\frac{\sum_j c_j}{K + \sum_j c_j}$. Then the partner's influence is bounded *by the agent's own concurrent state* — the more the agent's own needs already drive the channel, the less the partner can add, "relatively ineffective", with no free scalar anywhere. This is the one-line invariant this project asks for (D75): it is not a ceiling parameter $\vartheta$, it is a shared denominator.
3. **Voltage-dependent entry as a brake on baseline capture.** A partner term whose driving force vanishes at the agent's resting state (reversal potential at rest) can only *amplify the agent's own ongoing oscillation* and can never shift its baseline. Formally: a coupling $I_p = g_p(\text{partner})\,(x - E)$ with $E = x_{\text{rest}}$ has zero effect at $x = x_{\text{rest}}$, so a neutral agent cannot be dragged anywhere by any partner strength. That is a precise statement of "the partner acts only through the agent's own body", and it is a property, not a setting.
4. **Tonic versus phasic as two channels of one substance.** A relationship variable (slow, low concentration, sets basal function) and an event variable (fast, high concentration, short-term) can be the same quantity read at two scales, giving time scales from structure rather than from hand-set constants.
5. **Degeneracy as the honest answer to individual differences.** Many parameter sets give the same expression; the agent's history picks one. This predicts that two agents can match on all outputs and still respond differently to the same partner, which is a measurable property and matches the reading that modulator effects are reliable across animals "although ... a small proportion of networks responded anomalously".

## Verdict
**adopt (as the source of the occlusion/reversal-potential invariants and of the partition-selection idea); not a new computation on its own.** Its dynamics are parameter scheduling (excluded as SSM/linear-operator-with-structure); its value is three derived ceilings that remove the top of a dial by construction, and the claim that a modulatory state changes *which* circuit exists rather than how loud one is.
