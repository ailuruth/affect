## What it does
- **The object.** A neural CDE is dZ = f_θ(Z) dX, with X an interpolation of the data. The NRDE replaces the pointwise derivative of X by interval summaries: pick r_0 < r_1 < … < r_m (m ≪ n), and on [r_i, r_{i+1}) drive the hidden state by

  dZ/dt = f̂_θ(Z) · LogSig^N_{r_i, r_{i+1}}(X) / (r_{i+1} − r_i)

  (equation 9), solved with an ordinary ODE solver (rk4 in torchdiffeq). The depth-1 log-signature is the increment; the depth-2 term is the Lévy area ("the signed area between the curve and the chord joining its start and endpoints", Figure 2).
- **Where it comes from.** Figure 3 linearises f in the CDE: the first two Taylor coefficients are exactly the level-1 and level-2 signature terms, with f(Z_a) and Df(Z_a)f(Z_a) as their coefficients. The log-ODE method (Definition A.8) turns this into an ODE on [0, 1] whose vector field is f̂(z)·LogSig. Remark A.10: in a Lyndon basis, the log-ODE vector field needs only f and its iterated Lie brackets; the level-2 area of channels i, j multiplies the bracket [f_i, f_j].
- **What the paper does not keep.** "We do not attempt to model the specific structure of f̂ … Instead, we model f̂ as a neural network directly." The bracket structure of Remark A.10 is dropped; f̂ is an MLP whose last layer is reshaped to a (hidden × β) matrix, with tanh on the last hidden layer to bound the rate of change.
- **Generalisation.** With depth 1 and step equal to the data step, the NRDE is the NCDE with linear interpolation (section 3.2). So the NRDE strictly contains the NCDE, and inherits its Theorem C.1 property (it can represent the identity map between paths).
- **Convergence (Appendix B, cited, not proved there).** Local error ≤ C‖f‖^γ_{Lip(γ)} ‖X‖^γ_{p-var} (Boutaib et al. 2014); global error a sum of these over the partition (Gyurkó 2008). For linear vector fields the log-ODE is a matrix exponential bounded by exp(Σ_m K^m |π_m LogSig|).
- **Depth and step are hyperparameters, not accuracy settings.** "This makes use of the log-ODE method a modelling choice rather than an implementation detail." One step size per model; the grid was 2 … 1024 with depths 2 and 3.

## Evidence with numbers
- **EigenWorms** (UEA; length 17,984; 6 channels with time; 5 classes; 3 repeats; 70/15/15 split). NCDE step 1: 62.4 ± 12.1 % accuracy, 22.0 h, 176.5 MB. Best NRDE (depth 2, step 4): 83.8 ± 3.0 %, 2.4 h, 180 MB. Depth 3, step 32: 75.2 ± 3.0 %, 0.6 h. Naive subsampling of the NCDE (steps 4–128) was faster but no better; the folded ODE-RNN was worst at every step and could not run at step 1.
- **BIDMC** (PPG and ECG at 125 Hz; length 4,000; 3 channels with time; L2 loss on RR, HR, SpO2). Depth-3 NRDE best at every step and task: RR 1.49 ± 0.08 (step 512), HR 2.97 ± 0.45 (step 128), SpO2 1.29 ± 0.15 (step 512), against NCDE step 1: 2.79 ± 0.04, 6.75 ± 0.9, 1.98 ± 0.31 ("reducing test loss by 30–59 %"), with about an order of magnitude less training time. NRDE loss improved as the step grew; NCDE loss worsened.
- **Memory.** Adjoint backpropagation: in one experiment 3.6 GB to 47 MB.
- **Hyperparameters** were tuned on the NCDE at a step giving 500 updates and reused for the NRDE (hidden 16/32/64, 2–4 layers, multiplier 1–3; Adam, lr 0.032/batch). Three repeats per cell; standard deviations are large on EigenWorms (up to ± 14 %).
- **Limitations stated by the authors:** two new hyperparameters with large effects; β(v, N) grows exponentially in the number of channels v, so the method is "most feasible with few input channels".
- **Related work they name:** hierarchical subsampling (Graves 2012; an RNN over RNN outputs) and Liao et al. 2019 (a log-signature/RNN pair), which they describe as "an inspired choice of pre-processing" that the NRDE turns into an actual log-ODE solve.

## What it means for the design
1. **It is the nearest neighbour, and it contains the commutator-coupled core as a special case of its architecture.** Take X = (agent's own channels, partner's channels in the agent's coordinates, own body). At depth 2, LogSig² holds every own and partner increment and every pairwise Lévy area. An NRDE whose learned f̂ has zero columns for the partner's increments and for every cross-channel bracket, and keeps only the same-channel brackets [own_k, partner_k], is the commutator-coupled drive at a single step size. Since f̂ is an unconstrained MLP, that masked f̂ is inside the NRDE class. A commutator-coupled design is therefore a *restricted* NRDE: the restriction is new; the machinery is not.
2. **The restriction changes the object, not just a weight.** In the true log-ODE (Remark A.10) the area term multiplies the bracket [f_own, f_partner], which presupposes a partner vector field f_partner acting on dX_partner. Deleting the partner's level-1 term while keeping the area means the state is no longer the log-ODE of any CDE driven by (own, partner). It is a CDE driven by a different control: (own path, Lévy-area process of each (own_k, partner_k) pair). That is a legitimate neural CDE with an augmented control channel, of the same kind as the time and observational-intensity channels Kidger et al. add; it is not a new solver or a new class.
3. **Theorem C.1 carries over to the NRDE and is the reason to mask.** The NRDE generalises the NCDE (section 3.2), so a constant f̂ on the partner's depth-1 column copies the partner's path into the state. Masking that column removes copy as the cheapest function in the class (a constant field on the area channel integrates area, which is zero on any zero-lag affine copy). It does not remove the partner's information: with the own path known, the tree of same-channel areas determines the partner's increments up to adding a multiple of the own increments (checked in design-commutator-coupling.md, C4). So the mask is an input restriction, not an information bound.
4. **Many step sizes at once, rather than one tuned step.** Morrill picks one step and one depth per model by validation loss; the heat maps (Figure 5) show the best step differs by task. A dyadic tree computes every power-of-two step concurrently, and Chen's identity makes the areas of all scales an exact decomposition of the whole window's area. Feeding each scale to its own state block is the hierarchical-subsampling idea the paper cites (and the clockwork-RNN schedule), so it adds no novelty either.
5. **The channel limitation helps the masked version.** With 33 own, 33 partner and 8 body channels, a full depth-2 log-signature has β = 74 + 74·73/2 = 2,775 coordinates; the same-channel mask keeps 74 + 33. The paper's stated bottleneck (β exponential in v) is a practical argument for the mask, not a novelty argument.
6. **Real time.** The log-signatures "can also be easily computed in an online fashion"; a causal dyadic tree emits each node at its completion. Nothing in the paper addresses latency within a node; a node at scale j delivers its summary 2^j frames late.
7. **What the paper does not show.** All tasks are offline classification or regression on fixed, regularly sampled series; nothing is coupled, online or two-party, and nothing says whether learning on area summaries finds long-range structure in dyadic behaviour. Its results support "interval summaries help learning long dependencies", not any claim about mimicry.

## Verdict
- [Neural rough differential equations (Morrill et al.)](morrill-2021-neural-rde.md) — 2021 — watch (nearest neighbour; substrate) — an NRDE drives the hidden state by depth-N log-signatures of the control over intervals through an unstructured learned f̂ (the Lie-bracket structure is dropped); a commutator-coupled core is a depth-2 NRDE on (own, partner) with the partner's level-1 and cross-channel bracket columns masked, a normalised same-channel bracket, and all dyadic steps at once, so it is a restricted NRDE (recombination); EigenWorms 62.4 → 83.8 %, BIDMC loss −30–59 %, no dyadic or online evidence.
