## Findings / the computation, precisely

**Design.** n = 40 socially connected adults (people who lived alone, scored > 50 on UCLA loneliness, or had small networks were excluded). Three sessions, counterbalanced, ≥ 24 h apart: 10 h fasting; 10 h total social isolation (9 am–7 pm, no phone, email, social media, fiction or music; puzzles and non-social games allowed); a non-deprived baseline. Scan at 7 pm with a cue-induced craving task: each participant's own top-10 favourite foods and social activities (stock photos of strangers, own words as captions, e.g. "joking around with friends"), flowers as control; 18 blocks per condition per session. Preregistered (OSF cwg9e).

**Self-report.** After isolation: social craving t(39) = 5.35, loneliness t(39) = 5.17, discomfort t(39) = 5.57, all p < 0.001; happiness fell (t(39) = −4.21). 36 of 40 felt lonelier. After fasting: food craving t(36) = 17.40, hunger t(36) = 23.90. Social craving was more variable than food craving (final ratings 66.4 ± 24.5 vs 80.3 ± 19.4 on 0–100; Levene F = 15.86, p < 0.001).

**Need-specific midbrain response.** In the functionally localized midbrain ROI (top 100 voxels for novel-reward > familiar-no-reward), food cues were higher after fasting than after isolation (b = 0.03, p = 0.001) and social cues higher after isolation than after fasting (b = 0.03, p = 0.01). In the anatomical SN/VTA, the food effect held (b = 0.06, p = 0.002) but the social effect did not on average (b = −0.03, p = 0.18); SN/VTA social response correlated with end-of-isolation social craving (r = 0.32, p = 0.023).

**A shared code across needs.** An SVM trained on SN/VTA food-vs-flower patterns after fasting decoded social-vs-flower after isolation (accuracy 0.54, p = 0.001) but not at baseline after correction (0.53, p = 0.013). Pattern similarity: social-craved to food-craved r = 0.34 vs social-craved to food-not-craved r = 0.20 (p = 0.025). "the presence of a craved object should be more important for SN/VTA activity than the specific object". The striatum split by need: food in nucleus accumbens, social in caudate.

**Three results that bound a homeostatic reading.**
1. *Wanting without deprivation.* At baseline, SN/VTA responses to both cues were "on average as high as on the relevant deprived day" and still tracked self-reported craving (social r = 0.37). The authors: "Rewarding stimuli (including food and social contact) are craved even without specific deprivation", and suggest that "specific acute deprivation might serve to narrow (rather than enhance) the brain's motivational responses to the deprived target".
2. *Chronic deficit mutes, not amplifies.* More chronically lonely participants reported *less* social craving after isolation (r = −0.36) and had muted SN/VTA social responses (r = −0.35; at baseline r = −0.46) and muted food responses (r = −0.38).
3. *Satiation untested.* "A vital question for future research is how much, and what kinds of positive social interaction are sufficient to fulfill this basic need and thus eliminate the neural craving response." The paper measures the deficit side only; no reunion or satiation condition.

They frame the result as support for "social homeostasis", and note that solitude chosen freely "can be restful", whereas this isolation was imposed.

## What it means for the designs
- **Social contact can be a regulated variable of its own.** The Cayley-ledger body (E, A, V, C, H, I; §3.13) has no contact variable, so a reunion moves nothing. Tomova supports adding one: a contact deficit K that rises with the agent's own time without exchange, is need-specific (raises the gain on social cues, not food-like ones), and shares a common "craved object" code with other needs. That gives the reunion a size: relief = drop in K.
- **What fills K must be the agent's own exchange, not the partner's display (Principle 6).** The paper's cues are activities ("chatting and laughing together"), not faces' valence. Make K's refill a function of the agent's own completed turns of exchange (it spoke and was answered, in either direction) with a saturating dose–response, never of the partner's smile, gaze or session length. Saturation in dose, plus a set point, makes "more time" earn nothing once K is in its box.
- **Wanting at baseline and the chronic-mute result argue against a pure deficit model.** A small cue-triggered appetite should exist at K ≈ 0 (incentive gain as in zhang-berridge-2009), but bounded; and a very large, long-held K should *reduce* approach gain rather than raise it without bound. Both are falsifiable in the agent: after a long silence, the first reunion response should be large; after a pathological, prolonged silence it should be smaller and slower, not larger.
- **No hand-named variable.** K is a body variable named by function (contact deficit), as Panksepp's separation-distress subsystem is in panksepp-primary-affective-systems.md; "lonely" is a word Claude may use when retrieved text describes the state.
- **Limits.** fMRI effects are small (decoding 0.54); the anatomical-ROI social effect was null on average; the preprint and the published paper may differ; the satiation side, which is what this project needs most, is not measured.
