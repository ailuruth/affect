# Sterling 2012, "Allostasis: a model of predictive regulation" (Physiology & Behavior 106(1):5-15)

Source read: the full text of the 2012 paper was NOT accessible. It is paywalled at Elsevier, Semantic Scholar lists it as "CLOSED", and no author copy was found. Read instead, on 2026-11-25:

2. Sterling 2018, "Point of View: Predictive regulation and human design", eLife 7:e36133 (CC BY 4.0, via the Europe PMC full-text XML). Read: the section on early design principles and the passage citing the 2012 paper, plus the published reviewer exchange.
3. Ramsay & Woods 2014's account of Sterling's position (see ramsay-woods-2014-homeostasis-allostasis.md). That account is secondary and is marked as such below.

## Claims
- The core charge (2012 abstract): "The premise of the standard regulatory model, "homeostasis", is flawed: the goal of regulation is not to preserve constancy of the internal milieu. Rather, it is to continually adjust the milieu to promote survival and reproduction."
- Against feedback as the primary mechanism (2012 abstract): "Regulatory mechanisms need to be efficient, but homeostasis (error-correction by feedback) is inherently inefficient. Thus, although feedbacks are certainly ubiquitous, they could not possibly serve as the primary regulatory mechanism."
- The alternative (2012 abstract): "efficient regulation requires anticipating needs and preparing to satisfy them before they arise." The four claimed advantages are quoted verbatim: "(i) errors are reduced in magnitude and frequency; (ii) response capacities of different components are matched -- to prevent bottlenecks and reduce safety factors; (iii) resources are shared between systems to minimize reserve capacities; (iv) errors are remembered and used to reduce future errors."
- Central brain coordination (2012 abstract): "This regulatory strategy requires a dedicated organ, the brain", which "enforces a system of flexible trade-offs: from each organ according to its ability, to each organ according to its need."
- Affect claims (2012 abstract): a "'stick'" that "corresponds broadly to the sense of anxiety" and a "'carrot'" that corresponds "broadly to the sense of pleasure". "This design constrains anxieties to be non-adapting and pleasures to be brief -- fast-adapting -- to make way for the next anxiety."
- Pathology placed at the brain level (2012 abstract): in hypertension, "Chronically anticipating a need for higher pressure, the brain mobilizes all the low level mechanisms in concert"; so therapy should "reduce demand and increase sense of control -- so that the brain can down-shift its prediction".
- The 2018 restatement (eLife): "Predictive regulation, termed allostasis, minimizes the frequency and size of errors; thus it is intrinsically more efficient than homeostasis, which waits for errors to occur and then corrects them by negative feedback (Sterling, 2012)."
- Sterling's original position as Ramsay & Woods report it (secondary): allostasis rests on homeostasis's "reliance on reactive, post-hoc responses and its insistence on defending a fixed set point" (citing Sterling 2004), and "Sterling (2004) suggested that allostasis should replace homeostasis".

## Evidence
From what was read, the argument rests on design principles (efficiency), physiological examples (thermoregulation by behaviour, hypertension) and evolutionary reasoning. The abstract reports no formal model and no quantitative test. In the 2018 eLife piece, reviewers disputed the "positive feedback" claims about reward learning, and Sterling replied that he had deleted "the terms “positive feedback” and adaptation". How the 2012 body supports the efficiency claim could not be checked.

## Verdict: watch
For (d), this is the strongest critique as far as it could be read. Its target is pure error feedback around a fixed reference: "inherently inefficient", and unable to "possibly serve as the primary regulatory mechanism".
- My reading, not Sterling's: the charge does not obviously apply to an internal-model regulator. Once locked, such a regulator generates the cancelling input from its internal copy of the disturbance generator while the error is zero (francis-wonham-1976-internal-model-principle.md), so it is anticipatory in steady state even though error drives it.
- The project's regulator should therefore be described as error-driven with an internal model and an adjustable reference, not as set-point homeostasis.
- Reason for "watch" rather than "adopt": the efficiency argument and the "non-adapting anxieties / fast-adapting pleasures" claim are stated in the abstract without evidence that could be read. Neither should be cited as established until the 2012 full text is obtained.
