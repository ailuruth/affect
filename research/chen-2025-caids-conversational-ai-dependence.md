# Chen, Wang, Yuan & Zhao 2025, "Development and validation of the conversational AI dependence scale for Chinese college students" (Frontiers in Psychology 16:1621540, doi 10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1621540; correction to Table 1 published Oct 2025, PMC12550587)

Source read: the full 14-page published PDF (CC BY), including Appendix B (the scale), plus the PMC correction notice. Supplementary material was not read. I also searched for other validated AI-dependence or attachment scales and could not read them; they are listed at the end.

## Claims
- CAIDS-20: 20 items, four factors, headed "In the Past Year...". Factors: uncontrollability, withdrawal symptoms, mood modification, negative impacts.
- Response format: the item pool used "A six-point Likert scale ... with 1 representing 'completely disagree' and 6 representing 'completely agree.' Higher scores indicated a higher level of dependency on CAI." The final-scale sections do not restate the format; I assume it was kept (unverified).
- Items, verbatim from Appendix B, in printed order. Factor assignment is not printed beside the items. Grouping them by the corrected Table 1 counts (negative impacts 6, mood modification 4, withdrawal 5, uncontrollability 5) and by content gives the following; this mapping is my inference:
  - Uncontrollability (5): I often spend a lot of time thinking about or planning to use CAI. / CAI has become an indispensable part of my daily life and studies. / I find myself using CAI more and more frequently. / Each time I use CAI, the time spent always exceeds my original plan. / I often rely on the analysis and suggestions provided by CAI to guide my daily decisions.
  - Withdrawal symptoms (5): I feel irritable when CAI is unavailable. / I feel anxious or worried when CAI is inaccessible. / I feel a sense of loss when I can't access CAI. / I feel helpless and confused when I am unable to use CAI. / I feel like I cannot stop using CAI.
  - Mood modification (4): When experiencing negative emotions (such as depression, anxiety, helplessness, guilt, etc.), I tend to use CAI for comfort. / When I feel irritable, I usually use CAI to seek relief. / When I feel lonely, I tend to use CAI to pass the time. / Using CAI effectively helps improve my mood.
  - Negative impacts (6): People around me often say that I spend too much time using CAI. / After frequently using CAI, I find it more difficult to engage in independent thinking. / Frequent use of CAI has negatively impacted my studies or life. / After using CAI frequently, I have lost interest in other hobbies. / After frequently using CAI, my interactions with friends or family have decreased. / After frequent use of CAI, I feel my ability to engage in active thinking has deteriorated.
- Scoring: total and per-dimension scores ("the total score and each dimension"). No cut-off for "dependent" is given.

## Evidence
- Study 1: EFA, N = 547, 51 items reduced to 20, 74.41% variance explained.
- Study 2: CFA, N = 687: "RMSEA = 0.07, SRMR = 0.06, CFI = 0.92, and TLI = 0.91".
- Reliability: "the internal consistency coefficients for the total score and each dimension were 0.86, 0.91, 0.91, 0.88, and 0.94, respectively, while the split-half reliability coefficients were 0.81, 0.86, 0.90, 0.88, and 0.77". No test-retest reliability is reported.
- Criterion validity: positive correlations with BSMAS (social media addiction), the UCLA-3 loneliness scale and short ECR attachment insecurity. Study 3 links CAIDS to "negative psychological outcomes" and lower subjective wellbeing (cross-sectional).
- Limitations as stated: cross-sectional design; generalised CAI rather than "specific subtypes of CAI use (e.g., academic vs. social applications)"; Chinese students only.
- Correction notice: the Table 1 column headers were in the wrong order. Correct order: "Item, Negative impacts, Mood modification, Withdrawal symptoms, Uncontrollability, Communality."

## Verdict: watch
There is no field-standard instrument for problematic attachment to AI companions yet. Every candidate is 2024-2025, validated in one population (mostly Chinese students), and none has independent replication. CAIDS is the one whose items and psychometrics I could check. Its withdrawal and negative-impact items are the closest published operationalisation of the harms Knox et al. name (social withdrawal, loss of other interests). But it measures trait-level dependence over "the past year" on generic conversational AI. It cannot detect a difference between two agent variants in a lab session, so it is not an ablation instrument. Its only fit is a longitudinal deployment: a rise in CAIDS total, or in the withdrawal and negative-impacts subscales, in users of the affect-bearing agent relative to a control arm would count as harm. It has no cut-off, no test-retest data and no English-language validation, so n=1 use is not supported. Revisit once EHARS and the Zhang scale are read.
