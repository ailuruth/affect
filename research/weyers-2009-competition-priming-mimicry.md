# Weyers, Mühlberger, Kund, Hess & Pauli 2009, "Modulation of facial reactions to avatar emotional faces by nonconscious competition priming" (Psychophysiology 46:328-335, doi 10.1111/j.1469-8986.2008.00771.x)

Source read: full article PDF (author-hosted, ursulakhess.com/resources/WMKHP09.pdf), all sections.

## Claims
- Subliminal competition priming removes facial mimicry of happy and sad avatar faces; neutrally primed participants mimic both.
- The competition effect shows up as corrugator activity rising again during a happy face after an initial relaxation, i.e. it appears late in the trial.
- It modulated intensity but did not produce significant counter-mimicry, unlike Lanzetta & Englis (1989), whose competition was explicit. The authors suggest mimicry may be "a strong reflex-like reaction that is difficult to overcome", with later responses reflecting the primed context.

## Evidence
- N = 49 women, 19-30 years, randomly assigned to competition primes (Wettbewerb, Gegner, Konkurrent, Gewinnen) or neutral primes, each word 20 times, 90 ms parafoveal, masked. No participant recognised any prime word.
- Stimuli: Poser avatar, one female face, happy / neutral / sad, three hairstyles; 27 presentations of 8 s, ITI 25-31 s.
- EMG: zygomaticus and corrugator, left side, 1000 Hz, 30-500 Hz band-pass, 125 ms integration; change from the last 1 s before onset; averaged in 1-s epochs (8 per trial).
- Manipulation check (impression of a neutral KDEF face, ~20 min later): competition-primed rated her less likeable, more hostile, less cooperative, more stubborn, F(1,47) = 4.69 to 9.50, p = .018 to .002 (one-tailed).
- Group × Emotion × Muscle × Seconds, F(14,658) = 3.1, p = .008, η²p = .062. Zygomaticus: no interaction involving priming (Group × Emotion F(2,46) = 1.3, p = .28). Corrugator to happy faces: Group F(1,47) = 3.9, p = .027 (one-tailed), η²p = .077; Group × Seconds F(7,329) = 4.6, p = .004, η²p = .088; groups differ from the 4th second on. Corrugator to sad faces: no group effect, F(1,47) = 1.8, p = .186; within-group, neutral-primed show more corrugator to sad than neutral faces in most seconds, competition-primed show no difference. Means for zygomaticus to sad faces in the competition group trend toward counter-mimicry, not significant.

## What it implies for the design
- A relation or goal state that the agent is not aware of can still reshape its face. The modulation does not need the conceptual layer (consistent with Principle 4's fast loop).
- Timing structure: the early part of the response looked similar across groups; the context effect grew over seconds 4-8. A model whose context term only scales an instantaneous copy gain cannot produce a response that starts congruent and then diverges.
- Smile (zygomaticus) return was robust to the competition prime; the change sat on the brow (corrugator) channel. Context acts on some channels and not others.
- Small sample, women only, one avatar identity; the authors flag low power for the sad-face effects.

## Verdict: adopt (as evidence that context effects are late and channel-specific)
