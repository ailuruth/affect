## Claims
- **Core affect.** It is "that neurophysiological state consciously accessible as the simplest raw (nonreflective) feelings evident in moods and emotions". It is "a single integral blend" of pleasure–displeasure and arousal, and "a person always has core affect". It is "Object free (free-floating)" and becomes directed only through attribution.
- **Causes are opaque.** "People have no direct access to these causal connections". Core affect is moved by immune activity, diurnal rhythms, hormones, drugs and cumulative stress, as well as by external events.
- **Function.** "Core affect is a continuous assessment of one's current state." It drives:
  - a search for causes;
  - mood-congruent attention and memory;
  - incidental preference learning;
  - influence on everything from reflexes to decisions.
  It also says: "Decisions thus involve predictions of future core affect." "Core affect is involved in motivation, reward, and reinforcement."
- **Second primitive: perception of affective quality.** This is the stimulus's capacity to change core affect, a "cold" estimate. The two can dissociate: a depressed patient can admit the sunset is beautiful without any change in mood.
- **Attributed affect** has three necessary and jointly sufficient features: a change in core affect, an Object, and attribution of the change to that Object.
- **An emotional episode** has these components: antecedent, affective quality, core affect, attribution, appraisal, instrumental action, physiological and expressive changes, metacognitive experience, emotional meta-experience and regulation. "There is no separate class of 'emotional behavior'", and "there is no nonverbal expressive signal or pattern of autonomic nervous system activity unique to each discrete emotion."
- **Emotional meta-experience is categorization.** It is "not an introspection and naming of an internal event, the fear" but "a self-perception ... a categorization of one's state" using folk prototype concepts. Categories therefore "enter the account only when the emotional episode is observed".
- **Dissociation is the central empirical claim.** "The components have no one common cause and are relatively dissociated." "Fear is not an entity." "No one component is necessary."

## Evidence
- Bradley & Lang (2000): covariation among self-report, avoidance and autonomic arousal to feared stimuli "seldom accounts for more than 10–15% of the variance".
- Averill (1982): angry people aggressed physically 10% of the time.
- Reisenzein (2000), on surprise components: correlations from .19 (reaction time with expression) to .46 (feeling with expression), except appraisal of unexpectedness with felt surprise at r = .78.
- Winkielman, Berridge & Wilbarger (2000): the same subliminally induced core affect shows up as mood in some conditions and as liking for a beverage in others.

## Mechanistic commitments
1. **One continuous, always-present low-dimensional state** (valence and arousal) moved by many causes, internal and external.
2. **A separate perceptual estimate of stimulus value** (affective quality).
3. **Causal attribution** that links state change to an Object.
4. **Categorization by an observer**, which may be the self, using learned prototypes. That is where discrete emotions live.
5. **No central emotion node.** Coherence across components is low and is imposed by the category.

## What it implies for a single formal core
This is the clearest source for Principle 2's "valence and arousal are read out":
- core affect is a readout, always defined, with no object;
- discreteness belongs to Claude's categorization (Principle 4).

Three derivation targets follow:
- **(a)** The core must have a readout that is defined at every moment, not only during episodes.
- **(b)** It must keep **state** (core affect) separate from the **value estimate of a stimulus** (affective quality), because the two dissociate.
- **(c)** An attribution step must link state changes to causes in the world model. Misattribution should be possible: carry-over from an unrelated event changes the evaluation of the next one.

Russell's dissociation claim conflicts with Anderson & Adolphs's single central state with coordinated parallel outputs (anderson-adolphs-2014-emotion-primitives.md). **The engine can measure which one it produces.** The shared variance of face, voice, policy and body channels under perturbation either looks like 10–15% (Russell) or like a common factor (Anderson & Adolphs, Scherer's synchronization).

## What an ablation could separate
- **State versus affective quality.** Lesion the path from stimulus value to core affect but keep the agent's value estimates. The agent should still rank stimuli correctly ("cold" evaluation) while its state stays flat: the depressed-patient dissociation.
- **Misattribution.** Induce a state change from one source (a body-variable perturbation). Measure how the agent evaluates a neutral partner expression right after. With attribution, evaluation shifts toward the state; if attribution is ablated (the state is tagged to its true source), it does not. This is the functional test of mood congruence.
- **Categorization.** Remove Claude. Russell predicts that core affect, attribution and action are unchanged and only meta-experience (Claude's naming) disappears. Constructionists (Barrett) predict more loss. See emotion-elements-synthesis.md.

## Verdict: adopt (core affect as a continuous readout; state versus affective quality; dissociation numbers as a coherence benchmark)
This is the canonical source for Principle 2's readout stance. The 10–15% and .19–.46 figures give the engine a human benchmark for coherence across components.
