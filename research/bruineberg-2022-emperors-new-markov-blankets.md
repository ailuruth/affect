## Claims
- Diagnosis: "a persistent confusion in the literature between the formal use of Markov blankets as an epistemic tool for Bayesian inference, and their novel metaphysical use in the free energy framework to demarcate the physical boundary between an agent and its environment." They name the two "Pearl blankets" and "Friston blankets".
- The general pattern: "a broader tendency within the FEP literature, in which mathematical abstractions are treated as worldly entities." The framework's mathematical surface "can seem to be steeped in unassailable mathematical justification, it also risks the possibility of 'smuggling in' unwarranted metaphysical assumptions. Each new iteration of the theory also introduces novel formal constructs that can make previous criticisms inapplicable".
- Model relativity (5.2): "conditional independence is itself model-relative"; "where Friston blankets are located in a model depends (at least partially) on modelling choices, that is, relevant Friston blankets cannot simply be 'detected' in some objective way".
- Over-breadth: "it is not that difficult to characterize all sorts of systems as free energy minimizing systems. For example, ... even the humble Watt governor can be analysed as a free energy minimizing system. ... the scope of the free energy formula is so broad that it is inadequate to pick out only living or cognitive systems."
- Trilemma for realist use: literalist ("the world just is a network"), realist (blankets represent real boundaries; but "the difficulty lies in finding a non-arbitrary mapping that is privileged for principled reasons", and one needs "a principled justification for why to start from one particular model rather than a different one, at which point it is not clear that the Markov blanket formalism is doing much additional work"), or instrumentalist ("less problematic but also less interesting").
- Conclusion: the realist reading "cannot be justified by just 'doing the maths,' but rather needs to be independently argued for, and no such argument has yet been offered." Extra assumptions "may in the end be doing all of the interesting work themselves."
- Related critiques they cite but this note did not read: Colombo (2020) on "a false equivocation between probability and adaptive value", and Aguilera et al. (2021), who claim "free energy gradients [are] uninformative about the behaviour of an agent or its specific trajectories".

## Verdict: adopt (as a critique the core must answer)
The paper targets Markov blankets, but its argument applies to any thermodynamic or variational vocabulary used to say what an agent is or feels. Stated as strongly as the source states it: the success of the mathematics does not license reading its quantities as worldly (or here, felt) quantities. The mapping from formal object to real object must be argued independently, and once it is, the formalism may be doing no extra work. Its over-breadth point applies directly: if "valence = work, arousal = dissipation" applies to every overdamped Langevin system, it applies equally to a colloid in a laser trap or a Watt governor, so it cannot by itself pick out affect.
- What the engine must therefore claim: the thermodynamic quantities are an instrumental readout chosen for stated reasons, and each mapping (which potential, which bath, which sign) is a modelling choice. The test is behavioral and ablation-based (Principle 5), not "the maths says so".
