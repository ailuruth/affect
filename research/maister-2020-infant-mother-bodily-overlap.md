# Maister, Hodossy, Tsakiris & Shinskey 2020, "Self or (M)other? Infants' sensitivity to bodily overlap with their mother reflects their dyadic coordination" (Child Development 91(5):1631-1649, doi:10.1111/cdev.13361, PMC8651012)

Source read: the full article from PubMed Central (introduction, method for the Mother-Infant Bodily Overlap task and the coordination coding, all results including the Bayesian analyses, discussion, limitations and open questions). Figures were seen through captions only. The supplementary materials were not read. Filippetti et al. 2013 (newborn visual-tactile synchrony) and Koós & Gergely 2001 are cited here and were NOT read.

Read for search pass 8 (topic 4: how infants tell self-caused from other-caused, and what partner experience does to it). An earlier Maister paper on adult enfacement is already noted (maister-2013-mirror-touch-enfacement.md).

## Key claims
- **Framing (from Gergely & Watson, Bahrick & Watson):** perfectly contingent visual-tactile or visual-proprioceptive input is "uniquely 'self-specifying'"; high-but-imperfect contingency signals a social partner. Caregiver turn-taking "provides imperfect contingencies that should support the development of a clear self-other distinction".
- **Task.** 21 infants, 6-8 months (M = 6.6). Side-by-side videos of the mother's face (or an age- and race-matched stranger's) stroked on the cheek, one delayed 3 s; the infant's own cheek stroked in synchrony with one video. Looking time coded frame by frame (ICC .874).
- **Results:**
  - with the mother: synchronous 5.35 s vs asynchronous 4.38 s, t(20) = 4.08, p = .0006, d = .89 (BF10 = 114.99);
  - with the stranger: no preference (4.57 vs 4.83 s, p = .27; BF10 = 0.12, evidence for the null);
  - proportions to synchrony: .569 (mother) vs .476 (stranger), t(20) = 2.97, p = .007; 15 of 21 infants showed the mother > stranger pattern; baseline looking to the two faces did not differ (BF10 = 0.228); age did not moderate.
- **Link to everyday interaction** (5-min free play, continuous coding of each partner separately, coordination = maximum cross-correlation):
  - stronger preference for synchrony with the mother went with **less** affective coordination with her: valence r(18) = −.63, p = .003 (BF10 = 4.65); arousal r(17) = −.52, p = .023 (BF10 = 2.58, anecdotal);
  - not with coordination of attention (r = −.15);
  - not for the stranger-synchrony preference, nor for a general mother-looking preference.
- **Interpretation offered:** infants with less coordinated mothers have more self-other "overlap" with her and seek self-specifying (perfect) contingency in her presence; consistent with Markova & Legerstee 2006 (3-month-olds of less attuned mothers gaze more when imitated) and Koós & Gergely 2001 (infants of poorly coordinated mothers attend to their own perfect contingency in her presence).
- **Limits stated:** small N; one age; coordination with a stranger not measured; the "synchronous" condition may be imperfectly contingent in practice.

## What it means for the engine
1. **Contingency bands as the self/other key.** Perfect (zero-lag) contingency → self; imperfect but responsive → partner; none → independent. This is Watson's analysis again (watson-1997-contingency-two-indices.md) with a body-relevance gate (only for body-like or relationship-relevant stimuli). The engine already distinguishes its own efference from partner input by construction; the useful part is the **imperfect social band** as the signature of a partner who answers.
2. **A human marker of a copy-seeking state.** Seeking perfect, mirror-like contingency from a particular partner goes with poorer affective coordination with that partner. For the engine this is an evaluation probe: if the agent's attention or learning comes to favour mirror-like (self-specifying) partner input from one person, that is a warning sign, the dyadic analogue of the "partner who only mirrors the agent" test already in the battery.
3. **Partner-specificity of self-other overlap** is learned per relationship (mother vs stranger); the engine's relationship code is the natural place, learned from interaction statistics, not set.
4. **No learning signal for expression comes out of this.** The paper measures a perceptual preference; it says nothing about how expression is learned.

## Verdict
- **adopt (as an evaluation marker and as evidence for contingency-band attribution; small N, correlational).** The preference for self-specifying contingency with one partner is inversely related to affective coordination with that partner, a measurable human analogue of a copy-seeking relation.
